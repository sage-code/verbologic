## The letter i

The letter (i) /i/ is called (i) /i/. Its main sound is the vowel /i/, that is a steady vowel, heard in the word (isla) /ˈiz.la/, which means island. This letter makes three sounds.

## Pronouncing the letter

For the first sound, spread your lips wide. Raise your tongue high and forward in your mouth. Let your vocal cords vibrate. For the second sound, push the tongue closer to the roof of your mouth. Glide quickly into the next vowel. For the third sound, glide smoothly from the main vowel toward a high tongue position. Place a hand on your throat to feel continuous vibration.

## Reading the letter

When it forms its own full vowel sound, you say the sound /i/, that is a steady vowel, as in the word (mío) /ˈmi.o/. Before another vowel, it sounds like the quick sound /j/, as in (bien) /bjen/. After another vowel, it becomes the gliding sound /i̯/, that is a closing glide, as in (aire) /ˈai̯.ɾe/.

## Common mistakes

Do not shorten this letter to the relaxed vowel of English sit. Make your lips tighter and keep the sound clean. Do not let the gliding sound become a heavy consonant like the English sound in jump. Keep the airflow smooth without friction.
