## The hard sign

The letter (твёрдый знак) /ˈtvʲɵrdɨj ˈznak/ has no sound of its own. It acts as a hard separator. You hear this separation in (объект) /ɐbˈjekt/, which means object. Because it makes no sound, this letter has zero sounds to learn. It only changes how you say nearby letters.

## Mouth movements

You make no sound for this silent mark. Your mouth pauses briefly after the consonant before it. Keep the consonant hard by leaving your tongue flat and low. Do not raise your tongue body toward the roof of your mouth. Then quickly start the next vowel with a strong y sound. Place a hand on your throat to feel the brief pause.

## Spelling rules

This letter appears after prefixes ending in a consonant and before certain vowels. It keeps the previous consonant hard and inserts a y sound. In this position, the letter is not pronounced, as in (подъезд) /pɐdˈjest/. Another clear case where it stays completely silent is (съел) /sjel/.

## Common pitfalls

A common mistake is blending the consonant directly into the vowel. In English words like subdue, consonants often soften smoothly. With this letter, keep the consonant plain and insert a clean break before the y sound. Do not drop the separating y sound either.
