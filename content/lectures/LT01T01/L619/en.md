## Sounds of ch

The letters ch are called /ʃ/. Their primary sound is heard in the word (chin) /tʃɪn/, which means the chin. These letters can produce four different sounds across English words.

## How to say ch

For the first sound, press your tongue tip behind your upper teeth, then release air sharply. For the second sound, lift the back of your tongue against the soft roof of your mouth and release air. For the third sound, push your lips forward into a circle and blow air gently over your tongue. For the last variant, keep your mouth still because you make no sound. Touch your throat to feel that your voice stays quiet.

## Spelling patterns

In native English words, these letters usually make the sound /tʃ/, as in (rich) /rɪtʃ/. In words borrowed from Greek, they make the sound /k/, as in (chemist) /kɛm.ɪst/ and (echo) /ɛk.oʊ/. In modern borrowings from French, they make the sound /ʃ/, as in (chef) /ʃɛf/ and (machine) /mə.ʃin/. In a few rare borrowings, the letters are not pronounced, as in (yacht) /jɑt/.

## Common mistakes

You may mix up the three main sounds of these letters. Do not say the sound /ʃ/ when a word needs the sharp sound /tʃ/. Do not use the sound /k/ in native words. Pay close attention to word origins to choose the correct sound.
