## Letter ó

The letter (ó) /ɔ/ takes its name from its open sound. Its main sound is heard in the word (olho) /ˈɔ.ʎu/, which means eye. This letter makes four different sounds in European Portuguese.

## Pronouncing the letter

For the first sound, round your lips tightly and pull your tongue back. For the second sound, open your mouth wider and round your lips loosely. For the third sound, close your mouth slightly more and push your lips forward. For the fourth sound, keep that close shape and let the air escape through your nose. Place a hand on your throat to feel continuous vibration.

## Spelling rules

In unstressed syllables, this letter makes the sound /u/, as in (amigo) /ɐˈmi.ɡu/. In stressed syllables with an open vowel, it makes the sound /ɔ/, as in (avó) /ɐˈvɔ/. In stressed syllables with a closed vowel, it gives the sound /o/, that is, a close vowel, as in (boca) /ˈbo.kɐ/. Before the letters m, n, or with a tilde, it produces the sound /õ/, as in (bom) /bõ/.

## Common mistakes

Do not turn the stressed close vowel into a gliding sound like the vowel in boat. Keep your tongue completely still until the sound ends. In unstressed endings, avoid saying an open vowel like the sound in lot. Always round your lips tightly for that unstressed vowel.
