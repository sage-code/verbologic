## The letter es

The letter (S) /ʒ/ is named es. It usually makes the sound /s/, like in the word (sun) /sʌn/, which means the star that lights our daytime sky. This letter has four different sounds across common English words.

## Making the sounds

Put the tip of your tongue close to the gum behind your top teeth. Push air through the small gap with no voice. For the second sound, keep the same position and add your voice. Next, pull your tongue slightly back and let voiced air rush through. Sometimes the letter is silent, so your mouth stays still. Put your hand on your neck to feel the vibration turn on and off.

## Reading the variants

At the start of words or before quiet consonants, it sounds like the sound /s/ in (sit) /sɪt/. Between vowels or at the end of many words, it makes the sound /z/ in (busy) /bɪ.zi/ and (rose) /roʊz/. Before endings like ion or ual after a vowel, it sounds like the sound /ʒ/ in (vision) /vɪ.ʒən/ and (usual) /ju.ʒu.əl/. In a few special words, the letter is not pronounced, such as in (island) /aɪ.lənd/ and (aisle) /aɪl/.

## Avoiding common traps

Do not add an extra vowel sound before this letter at the start of words. Keep your voice quiet when you say the first sound, so it does not turn into a buzz. When a word ends with a buzz, let your throat vibrate fully instead of whispering the finish. Make sure to keep the letter completely silent in the rare words where it makes no sound.
