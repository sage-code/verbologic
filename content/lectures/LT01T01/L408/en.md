## The letter di

The letter (di) /di/ makes the sound /d/, heard in (dove) /ˈdo.ve/, which means where. This letter has only one sound in Italian. It stays clear in all positions.

## Making the sound

Press the tip of your tongue against the back of your upper front teeth. Build up gentle air pressure behind it. Drop your tongue tip to release the air. Vibrate your vocal cords at the same moment. Put your fingers on your throat to feel the strong buzz.

## Reading the letter

This letter always makes the sound /d/ in all positions. You hear it at the start of a word, as in (dente) /ˈdɛn.te/. You also hear it between vowels, as in (cadere) /kaˈde.re/.

## Common mistakes

In English words like door, your tongue touches the gum ridge above your teeth. For the Italian sound, move your tongue forward. Touch the flat back of your upper teeth instead. Keep the sound light, with no extra puff of air.
