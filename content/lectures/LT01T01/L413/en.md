## Sound of letter i

The name of this letter is (i) /i/. Its main sound is heard in (isola) /ˈiːzola/, which means island. This Italian letter produces two spoken sounds, and sometimes it stays silent.

## How to say it

For the vowel sound, raise your tongue high near the roof of your mouth. Spread your lips wide and keep your voice steady. For the second sound, make this shape very quickly as you glide into the next vowel. Touch your throat to feel your vocal cords buzz continuously.

## Spelling rules

This letter makes the pure vowel sound /i/ on its own, as in (amici) /aˈmiːtʃi/. Next to another vowel in the same syllable, it sounds like /j/, as in (piatto) /ˈpjatto/ and (ieri) /ˈjɛːri/. It is silent when it softens a preceding c or g before a, o, or u, as in (ciao) /ˈtʃaːo/ and (giorno) /ˈdʒorno/.

## Common mistakes

Do not pronounce the vowel sound like the short vowel in English bit. Keep your tongue high and tense, like in machine. When it sits between a c or g and another vowel, do not add an extra sound. Keep it completely silent in those words.
