## The Sound of the Letter C

The letter **C** produces two distinct sounds in Romanian, behaving identically to Italian orthography. In front of **e** or **i**, it sounds like the soft English *ch* in *church* (`/tʃ/`). In all other contexts—before **a**, **o**, **u**, consonants, or word-finally—it makes a clean, unaspirated hard *k* sound (`/k/`).

## How to Pronounce C

For the hard sound `/k/`, press the back of your tongue against the soft palate and release with minimal air. For the soft sound `/tʃ/`, place the blade of your tongue against the alveolar ridge behind your upper teeth and release smoothly into a gentle friction. Keep lips relaxed and avoid the strong puff of aspiration common in English.

## Spelling and Rules

Romanian uses the digraph **ch** followed by **e** or **i** (`che`, `chi`) specifically to preserve the hard `/k/` sound before front vowels. Conversely, to produce the soft `/tʃ/` sound before back vowels, the letter **i** serves as a silent palatalising marker, as seen in words like *ceas* or *ciorobor*. Standard capitalisation applies at the start of sentences and proper nouns.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Contrast the hard `/k/` in *cal* with the soft `/tʃ/` in *cer*. English speakers frequently make the error of softening **c** into an *s* sound before **e** and **i**, as in French or English *center*; in Romanian, this must always be pronounced as `/tʃ/`. Additionally, avoid pronouncing the letter **h** in combinations like *ch* as a guttural sound, as it functions solely as a phonetic hardener.
