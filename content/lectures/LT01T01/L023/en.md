## The Sound of T

The Romanian **T** represents the voiceless dental plosive `/t/`. Unlike the English 't', which is alveolar and pronounced with a puff of air (aspiration), the Romanian `/t/` is dental, crisp, and completely unaspirated. At the ends of certain plural nouns followed by an ultra-short morphological final **i**, it can become slightly palatalised as `[tʲ]` (though in many cases it alternates to `[ts]` spelled **ț**).

## How to Pronounce It

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front teeth, rather than on the alveolar ridge behind them. Build up slight air pressure behind the tongue, then release it cleanly without letting out a noticeable puff of breath. Keep your vocal cords completely still, as this is a voiceless stop.

## Spelling Rules

The letter **T** is the twenty-fourth letter of the standard Romanian alphabet. It appears in all word positions, including word-initially, medially, and word-finally. Be careful not to confuse **T** with **Ț** (T with comma below), which represents the affricate `/ts/`.

## Common Pitfalls

English speakers must avoid aspirating **T** at the beginning of words like *tată*, which should sound sharp and clean rather than breathy. Also, remember that **T** never turns into a flapped sound or glottal stop as it often does in American English. Finally, ensure you pronounce **T** distinctly from **Ț** (*pat* 'bed' vs. *paț* 'happen').
