## The letter ípsilon

The letter (ípsilon) /ˈipsilõ/ makes the sound /j/ as in the word (yoga) /ˈjɔ.ɡɐ/, which means yoga. This letter is rare and comes from foreign words. It can make two different sounds in European Portuguese.

## Pronouncing ípsilon

To make the first sound, lift your tongue high toward the roof of your mouth. Glide quickly into the next vowel while your vocal cords vibrate. For the second sound, smile slightly and hold your tongue high and forward without moving. Touch your throat to feel the steady vibration of your voice.

## Spelling rules for ípsilon

This letter sounds like /j/ next to another vowel, as heard in (playboy) /plɐjˈbɔj/. It sounds like the pure vowel /i/ when it stands on its own in borrowed names, as in (Sydney) /ˈsid.nej/.

## Common mistakes

Do not let this letter turn into an open English vowel sound like the first part of nylon. Keep the sound tight and clear like the start of yes or the vowel of machine.
