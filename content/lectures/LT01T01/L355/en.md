## The letter En

The German letter (En) /ɛn/ makes the main sound /n/, that is a standard n sound. You hear this sound in (Name) /ˈnaːmə/, which means name. This letter has three different sounds across German words.

## Making the sounds

For the first sound, press the tip of your tongue against the ridge behind your top teeth. Let the air flow through your nose with your voice turned on. For the second sound, press the back of your tongue against the soft roof of your mouth. For the third sound, keep the tongue tip on the ridge while the sound forms a whole beat. Feel the vibration in your nose with your fingers.

## Reading the variants

In most regular positions, this letter sounds like the standard sound /n/, that is a standard n sound, as in (nein) /naɪ̯n/ or (Sonne) /ˈzɔnə/. Before the sounds /k/ or /ɡ/, it sounds like /ŋ/, as in (danke) /ˈdaŋkə/. In unstressed word endings, it forms its own small beat as /n̩/, that is a syllabic n sound, as in (leben) /ˈleːbn̩/.

## Avoiding mistakes

You might insert an extra vowel sound before the final nasal ending. Do not say a vowel like the one in the English word fun before the last consonant. Keep your tongue pressed firmly against the ridge behind your teeth without opening your mouth again.
