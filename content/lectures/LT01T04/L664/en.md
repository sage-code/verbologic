## What Are Hard and Soft Sounds?
Romanian consonants can be hard or soft. A hard consonant sounds plain and direct. A soft consonant has a slight airy release or a raised tongue posture. You often hear this difference before vowels like 'e' or 'i'. When you practice, you should compare two words side by side. One word has the hard sound, and the other word has the soft sound. Listening closely helps your ears catch the small shift in sound.

## Mouth Position, Stress, and Tone
For hard sounds, keep your tongue relaxed or low in the mouth. When you say the 'g' in **gară** /ˈɡa.rə/, the back of your tongue taps the soft roof of your mouth. For soft sounds, arch the middle of your tongue higher toward the palate. Do not add a full extra syllable. Keep your vocal tone steady and even. Put clear stress on the correct syllable so the consonant stays sharp and clean.

## Listening to the Example
Look at the Romanian word **gară** /ˈɡa.rə/, which means station. The first sound is a hard 'g'. Your vocal cords vibrate as the back of your tongue drops away from your palate. You can compare this hard beginning to a softer consonant sound. Saying word pairs aloud trains your mouth muscles. You will notice the contrast immediately when you switch back and forth between them.

## Mistakes to Avoid
English speakers often turn a soft sound into an extra syllable like 'ee'. Never add an extra beat to the word. Also, do not soften a hard sound by accident. Keep the 'g' in **gară** /ˈɡa.rə/ deep and firm. Do not drop your pitch at the start of the word. Practice slowly in front of a mirror to watch your mouth shape.
