## The sound pattern

In Romanian, the letter c usually sounds like ch in cheese when it comes before e or i. When the letter h is added between them, the sound changes completely. The letters **che** and **chi** make a single soft k sound. The letter h is completely silent here. It only acts as a sign to keep the sound sharp and crisp. A great example of this is the word **chiar** /kjar/, which means even or really.

## How your mouth makes the sound

To make this sound, lift the middle of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth. Press the sides of your tongue against your upper molars. Build a tiny burst of air behind your tongue. Release the air quickly without letting your vocal cords vibrate right away. The sound feels lighter and sharper than the normal k in kangaroo. Keep your lips relaxed and ready for the next vowel sound. Your voice should stay steady and bright.

## Common mistakes to avoid

English speakers often see the letters ch and want to say the ch sound from chicken. Another common mistake is trying to breathe out an audible h sound after the k. Remember that both letters merge into one single stop. Treat **chiar** /kjar/ as starting with a sharp k followed smoothly by a quick y sound. Never make the buzzing or dragging sound of an English ch. Keep the release brief, clean, and light.
