## The Rule for Ghe and Ghi
In Romanian, the letter **g** before **e** or **i** usually sounds soft like the English *j*. To keep the **g** hard, Romanian adds a silent letter **h**. The groups **ghe** and **ghi** always have a hard **g** sound, like in *guitar* or *game*. You never pronounce the **h** as a breath. It works only as a silent helper to protect the hard sound.

## How Your Mouth Makes the Sound
Push the back of your tongue against the soft roof of your mouth. Build up a small puff of air behind your tongue. Release the air suddenly while vibrating your vocal cords. Your tongue stays down in the front of your mouth. Do not push your tongue against the front ridge like you do for a *j* sound.

## Stress and Romanian Examples
Listen carefully to the word **ghici** /ˈɡi.tʃi/, which means to guess. The stress lands on the first syllable **ghi-**. Your voice starts a bit higher on the hard **g** and drops slightly on **-ci**. Another common word is **ghem** /ˈɡem/, which means ball of yarn. In both words, the sound is crisp and firm.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
English speakers often want to pronounce the letter **h** as a puff of breath. In Romanian **ghe** and **ghi**, the **h** makes no sound at all. Another common mistake is turning the **g** into a soft *j* sound like in *giant*. Always remember that the silent **h** locks the hard sound in place.
