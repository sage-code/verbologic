## The Sound Pattern
In Romanian, vowels can change when word endings shift. A stressed **o** frequently turns into the diphthong **oa**. This happens often when comparing singular and plural nouns. A good example is the singular noun **floare** /ˈflo̯a.re/, which means flower. In its plural form, the word becomes **flori**, returning to a simple **o**. This vowel shift is very common across Romanian vocabulary.

## How to Make the Sound
The diphthong **oa** is spoken in one single breath. First, round your lips slightly to make a short [o] glide. Immediately drop your jaw open into a clear [a] sound. The main stress lands directly on the [a]. Keep your voice steady without pausing between the two letters. Your tongue moves quickly from the back of your mouth down to the center.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
English speakers often try to split **oa** into two separate syllables. Do not say "flo-ah-reh" with a pause in the middle. Another common trap is making the glide sound like an English "w" as in "water". Keep the starting sound soft and rounded, not overly heavy. Always remember that the stress belongs completely on the vowel [a].
