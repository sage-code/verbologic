## The sound
Listen to (floare) /ˈflo̯a.re/, which means flower.
Notice the two letters run together.
You hear a glide from an o-sound into an a-sound.
In the plural (flori) /ˈflorʲ/, this glide turns back to a simple sound.
The sound shifts when the word ending changes.

## How to say it
Start with your lips rounded for a quick 'o'.
Open your jaw quickly into a clear 'ah' sound.
Do not make two separate beats.
Keep the stress on the open vowel.
Your voice glides smoothly in one single pulse.
Practice with the word (floare) /ˈflo̯a.re/.

## Reading rules
When you see the letters o and a together, read one sound.
Many singular words use this sound before an open ending.
Look at (floare) /ˈflo̯a.re/ again.
When the plural ending arrives, the sound often shortens to a simple o.
Look at the plural form (flori) /ˈflorʲ/.
The letters show you the exact change.

## Common mistakes
Do not split the sound into two syllables.
Do not say 'o-ah' slowly.
Blend the two vowels into one quick glide.
Also, remember to change the sound back in plural forms.
Do not say the glide in (flori) /ˈflorʲ/.
Keep the plural vowel short and round.
