## Two Special Romanian Letters

Romanian uses two special letters with a small comma underneath. The letter **ș** has a comma under s. The letter **ț** has a comma under t. You must never skip these little marks. They completely change how the letters sound. In Romanian, **ș** always sounds like the English *sh*. In Romanian, **ț** always sounds like the English *ts*. This rule has no exceptions at all. Whenever you see these letters, their sounds stay exactly the same.

## How Your Mouth Makes the Sounds

To say **ș**, push your lips out a little bit into a small circle. Put your tongue near the roof of your mouth, just behind your top teeth. Blow air smoothly over your tongue without using your voice. To say **ț**, place the tip of your tongue against the back of your top front teeth. Stop the air completely for a split second. Then, pull your tongue back quickly to release a sharp burst of air. Keep your voice box quiet for both sounds.

## Examples and Word Stress

A great example word is **școală** /ʃkoˈa.lə/, which means school. Notice how the first sound is a soft rushing whisper. The strong beat or stress lands on the syllable *a*. Another common word is **țară** /ˈt͡sa.rə/, which means country. Here, the sharp *ts* sound starts the word. The stress falls on the first syllable. Keep your pitch steady and natural as you move through the word. Do not let your voice rise too high.

## Common Mistakes for English Speakers

English speakers often mistake the letter **ș** for a regular *s*. Always remember that the comma gives it a *sh* sound. Never pronounce **ș** like the *s* in sun. Another mistake happens with **ț**. English speakers sometimes pronounce it like a normal *t*. Remember that **ț** is a combination sound like *ts* in cats. Never say just *t* when you see the comma. Practice switching smoothly between the soft *sh* and the sharp *ts*.
