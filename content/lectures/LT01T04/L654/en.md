## The consonant change
In Romanian, many words change their root consonant when they become plural. A very common change happens with the letter **t**. When masculine nouns take the plural ending **-i**, the sound /t/ shifts to /t͡s/, which is spelled **ț**. This sound shift is called a consonant alternation. It happens because the plural marker softens the consonant before it. A clear example is the singular word *frate* (brother), which becomes the plural **frați** /frat͡sʲ/ (brothers).

## Mouth and tongue position
To pronounce **ț**, touch the tip of your tongue against the ridge behind your upper teeth. Stop the air for an instant, then release it with a sharp hiss. It sounds like the "ts" in the word "cats". The final letter **i** in **frați** is not a full vowel. It is a tiny puff of breath that raises the middle of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth. Keep your lips relaxed and do not add an extra syllable.

## Stress and tone
The stress does not jump to the plural ending. In the word **frați** /frat͡sʲ/, the stress stays firmly on the first syllable, **fra-**. Make this first syllable louder and slightly higher in pitch. The ending **-ți** is spoken softly and quickly with a falling pitch. Keep your voice natural and steady. Another common example is the singular noun *bărbat* (man), which follows the exact same pattern and becomes *bărbați* (men).

## Common mistakes to avoid
English speakers often make two simple mistakes with this plural form. First, do not keep the hard **t** sound from the singular. Saying *frati* instead of **frați** sounds unnatural in Romanian. Second, avoid pronouncing the final **-i** as a loud vowel like "ee". The word **frați** has only one syllable, not two. Make sure you stop the sound promptly after the crisp hissing burst.
