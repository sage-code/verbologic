## The basic sound rule
In Romanian, the letter **c** changes its sound depending on the vowel that follows it. When you see **c** right before **a**, **o**, or **u**, it always makes a plain sound like the English letter *k*. It never sounds like *s* or *ch* in these positions. A classic vocabulary word is **casă** /ˈka.sə/, which means house. Notice how the first letter sounds crisp and sharp before the vowel **a**. Other examples work the exact same way before **o** and **u**.

## Mouth and tongue position
To make this sound properly, lift the back of your tongue toward the soft part of the roof of your mouth. Close off the airflow completely for a split second. Then, release the back of your tongue quickly so the trapped air bursts out. Keep your vocal cords completely still, because this is an unvoiced sound. In Romanian, do not blow out a huge puff of extra air. The burst should be tight, direct, dry, and immediately blend right into the next vowel.

## Stress and vocal tone
The consonant **c** itself does not take stress, but it starts the syllable with energy. In the word **casă** /ˈka.sə/, the stress falls strongly on the first syllable. Make that first syllable slightly louder and hold its pitch steady. As you move to the second syllable, drop your pitch slightly and relax your voice. The hard sound should never wobble or change tone. Give it a firm, steady start so the rhythm of the word stays clear and natural.

## Mistakes to avoid
Native English speakers often push out a small puff of air when saying a *k* sound at the start of a word. In Romanian, this extra puff makes your speech sound heavy and foreign. Try holding your hand before your mouth while saying **casă** /ˈka.sə/. You should feel almost no breath hitting your fingers. Another mistake is softening the consonant into a *ch* sound. Keep the tongue pressed firmly at the back of the mouth, not near the front teeth.
