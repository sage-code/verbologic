## The soft G sound

In Romanian, the letter **g** changes its sound depending on the vowel that follows it. When **g** stands right before **e** or **i**, it becomes soft. It sounds like the English **j** in the word **jump**. You hear this exact sound in many common Romanian words. If **g** stands before **a**, **o**, or **u**, it stays hard like in **go**. Remembering the two vowels **e** and **i** will help you read every word correctly.

## How to make the sound

Place the tip of your tongue against the roof of your mouth, just behind your top front teeth. Press the tongue gently to block the airflow for a tiny moment. Push air forward and let your tongue pull back quickly. Your vocal cords must vibrate to create a buzzing voice sound. Keep your lips slightly rounded and relaxed. In the word **gene** /ˈdʒe.ne/, put more stress and a slightly higher pitch on the first syllable.

## Practice with words

Listen closely to the audio recordings and repeat each word clearly. A great example to practice is **gene** /ˈdʒe.ne/, which means eyelashes or genes. Notice how the sound starts cleanly without any delay. You can also hear this sound in the word **ger** /ˈdʒer/, which means severe cold. Another good word is **girafă** /dʒiˈra.fə/, which means giraffe. Say the words slowly at first so your tongue finds the right spot.

## Mistakes to avoid

English speakers sometimes forget to use the soft sound before these vowels. Never pronounce this letter as a hard **g** like in **garden** when reading **ge** or **gi**. Another common mistake is making a soft French **zh** sound without the tongue tap. Make sure your tongue touches the roof of your mouth first to create the **d** part of the sound. Keep the vowel right after the consonant crisp, clear, and steady.
