## The Sound of Romanian 'io'

The combination **io** most commonly functions as an ascending diphthong pronounced `[jo]`, blending a brief glide into a pure open-mid or close-mid back vowel. It sounds very much like the **yo** in English *yonder* or *yoga*. In certain loanwords and hiatus positions, however, both letters are articulated distinctly as two full syllables `[i.o]`.

## How to Pronounce 'io'

Start with your tongue high and forward near the hard palate as if forming an English 'y', but immediately glide down and retract towards the back of the mouth to produce `[o]`. Keep your lips relaxed for the onset and rapidly round them into an oval shape for the vowel. Ensure the glide is quick and smooth without inserting any pause or glottal stop.

## Spelling Patterns

This spelling occurs word-initially in terms like **iod** or names like **Ion**, as well as word-medially after consonants such as in **ghiozdan** or **chior**. In native vocabulary and naturalized words, it regularly marks the diphthong `[jo]`. In technical, scientific, or Neoclassical borrowings (such as **radio** or **biologie**), it typically represents two distinct syllables in hiatus.

## Variants and Common Mistakes

Learners often struggle to distinguish between the single-syllable diphthong `[jo]` in words like **iod** and the two-syllable hiatus `[i.o]` in words like **avion** or **biologie**. Avoid dragging out the glide into a stressed, separate vowel when a true diphthong is required. Also, take care not to over-aspirate the preceding consonant or drop the rounding on the final `[o]`.
