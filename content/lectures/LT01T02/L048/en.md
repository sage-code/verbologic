## The Sound of “iau”

The Romanian sequence **iau** represents a true triphthong pronounced as `[jaw]`. It starts with a brief palatal glide like the *y* in *yard*, shifts cleanly into an open, central *a*, and glides out into a rounded bilabial semivowel like the *w* in *cow*. Unlike in English where three vowels are often split across syllables, all three vowel elements here merge tightly into a single uninterrupted pulse.

## How to Pronounce “iau”

Start with your tongue high and forward as if saying a quick `[j]`. Immediately drop your jaw low to articulate a clear, resonant `[a]`, which carries the main stress. Without pausing the vocal cord vibration, round your lips and raise the back of your tongue toward the soft palate to glide out into a soft `[w]`.

## Spelling and Occurrence

This combination is written simply as **iau** in lowercase and **Iau** at the beginning of a sentence. It functions most famously as the monosyllabic verb form *iau* (meaning 'I take' or 'they take', from *a lua*). It also appears at the end of imperfect tense verbs such as *tăiau* ('they were cutting') or *beiau* ('they were drinking').

## Comparing and Common Errors

Learners commonly make the mistake of breaking this triphthong into two or three separate syllables, pronouncing it like *ee-ah-oo*. In Romanian, `[jaw]` must remain strictly monosyllabic and fluent, sliding seamlessly through the three qualities. Pay attention to keeping the middle vowel `[a]` full and open while treating the outer elements as light, rapid glides.
