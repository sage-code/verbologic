## The Sound

The sequence **eu** typically forms a falling diphthong pronounced `/ew/`, gliding smoothly from a mid-front vowel to a rounded semivowel. It resembles the vowel glide in the Portuguese word *meu* or a rapid transition from English *bed* to *book*. Note that in the singular subject pronoun **eu** ('I'), standard Romanian adds a prothetic glide, pronouncing it as `/jew/`.

## How to Say It

Start with your lips unrounded and your tongue positioned mid-front, exactly as you would pronounce the Romanian vowel `[e]`. Without breaking the airflow, glide your lips smoothly into a tight, rounded shape while pulling the tongue back toward `[w]`. Keep both parts within the same syllable, allowing the first vowel to take the primary acoustic stress.

## Spelling Patterns

This letter pair is written as lowercase **eu** and capitalized as **Eu** at the beginning of sentences. It frequently appears at the end of adjectives, adverbs, and nouns, such as in **greu** ('heavy') or **muzeu** ('museum'). In most lexical items it forms a true diphthong in a single syllable, though in neologisms with Greek or Romance roots it may sometimes split into hiatus.

## Accent and Variations

Do not confuse the falling diphthong `/ew/` found in **greu** or **mereu** with the pronoun **eu**, which standard orthoepy transcribes with an initial glide as `/jew/`. Foreign learners often make the mistake of separating the two letters into two distinct syllables like *e-u*. Ensure you glide the sounds together into one coherent unit unless an explicit grammatical hiatus occurs.
