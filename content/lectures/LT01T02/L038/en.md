## The Sound of “ue”

In Romanian phonology, the sequence **ue** usually forms a diphthong where the `u` is pronounced as a rapid non-syllabic labiovelar glide `[w]` followed by `[e]`. It is comparable to the sound of *we* in English **wet**, though in Romanian it occurs rarely, typically in specialized verb forms like *înșeuează*. Unlike standard English, the transition into the front vowel is crisp and does not alter the preceding consonant.

## How to Pronounce It

Begin by rounding your lips tightly as if you are about to produce an English `w` sound or a Romanian `u`. Immediately release the lip rounding and glide directly into a clean, mid-front Romanian `e` with relaxed, unrounded lips. Ensure both sounds blend into a single fluid syllable without pausing or inserting an audible glottal stop between them.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter combination **ue** is relatively infrequent as an actual diphthong in native Romanian words, mostly surfacing in conjugated forms of verbs derived from stems ending in diphthongs (such as *a înșeua*). More commonly, in loanwords like *duet* or *statue*, the two letters represent two separate vowels pronounced in hiatus as `[u.e]`. Always check whether the morphological context calls for a glide or two distinct syllabic beats.

## Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

Foreign speakers frequently misread **ue** as a German-style front rounded vowel `[y]` / `[ø]` or attempt to split it into two heavy syllables when it should glide seamlessly. Compare the monosyllabic diphthong glide in *înșeuez* (`/ɨn.ʃeˈwez/`) with the hiatus in the neologism *duet* (`/duˈet/`). Avoid dropping the `w` glide entirely or confusing it with the much more common Romanian diphthong **oa**.
