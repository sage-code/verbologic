## The Sound of the Letter B

The letter **B** in European Portuguese usually represents the voiced bilabial plosive `[b]`, just like the English *b* in "boat". However, between vowels in natural connected speech, it softens into a voiced bilabial fricative `[β]`, where the lips do not fully seal. Unlike English, it is pronounced with less aspiration and tension.

## How to Pronounce B

To make the standard `[b]` sound, bring both lips completely together to stop the airflow, vibrate your vocal cords, and release the air cleanly. When it softens to `[β]` between vowels, simply bring your lips close enough to create slight friction without letting them snap completely shut. Keep your tongue relaxed and neutral at the bottom of your mouth.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **B** is written identically in uppercase (*B*) and lowercase (*b*) as in English. It appears frequently at the beginning of words (*bom*, *boca*) and internally before vowels or liquids (*branco*, *abrir*). Double *bb* never occurs in native Portuguese vocabulary, appearing only in foreign loanwords.

## Accents and Pitfalls

In careful speech and at the start of words, **B** sounds firmly like `[b]`, but between vowels it softens to `[β]` (as in *saber* `[sɐˈβeɾ]`). English speakers often make the mistake of aspirating it or failing to soften it in running speech. In northern Portugal, some speakers merge **B** and **V** into `[b]` or `[β]`, but standard European Portuguese keeps them strictly separate.
