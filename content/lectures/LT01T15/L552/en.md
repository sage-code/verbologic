## The Sound of Z

In European Portuguese, the letter **Z** represents a voiced alveolar fricative `/z/` when it begins a word or sits between vowels, exactly like the English 'z' in *zoo*. However, at the end of a word or syllable, it undergoes European Portuguese sibilant assimilation. In final position, it sounds like `/ʃ/` (English 'sh') before a pause or voiceless sound, or `/ʒ/` (the 's' in *measure*) before a voiced consonant.

## How to Pronounce Z

To make the standard `/z/` sound, place the tip of your tongue near your upper tooth ridge without completely stopping the airflow. Push air through the narrow channel while vibrating your vocal cords. For the syllable-final variants, simply retract your tongue slightly toward the palate into an 'sh' or 'zh' posture.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **Z** commonly appears at the beginning of words (*zona*), between vowels (*beleza*), and as a feminine or abstract noun ending (*rapaz*, *timidez*). Across word boundaries, final **-z** links to a following vowel sound, seamlessly turning back into a voiced `/z/` as in *paz e amor* (`/pa.z‿i.ɐˈmoɾ/`).

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Learners often make the mistake of pronouncing word-final **Z** as an English `/z/`. In European Portuguese, *paz* is never pronounced with a buzzing 'z', but rather with a soft 'sh' sound (`/paʃ/`). Remember to listen carefully to the consonant that follows: final **-z** adjusts continuously between `/ʃ/`, `/ʒ/`, and `/z/` depending on the environment.
