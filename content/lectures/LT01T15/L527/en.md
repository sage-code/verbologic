## The Sound of the Letter A

In European Portuguese, the letter **A** has two primary oral sounds: an open `[a]` like the vowel in British English *bath*, and a closed, reduced `[ɐ]` similar to the *u* in *cut* or the final *a* in *sofa*. When stressed, it is usually open, but in unstressed positions, it automatically reduces to the much centraler `[ɐ]`. Additionally, before nasal consonants or under a tilde, it acquires a distinct nasal quality `[ɐ̃]`.

## How to Produce the Sound

For the open `[a]`, drop your lower jaw wide, keep your tongue low and flat on the floor of your mouth, and relax your lips completely. To make the reduced `[ɐ]`, keep your jaw slightly higher, retract the tongue toward the center of your mouth, and make a relaxed, neutral vocalisation. Avoid rounding your lips for either sound.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **A** can appear with various diacritics that indicate stress, openness, or nasality. An acute accent (**á**) marks a stressed open `[a]`, a circumflex (**â**) denotes a stressed closed or nasal `[ɐ]`, and a tilde (**ã**) marks the nasal vowel `[ɐ̃]`. In unstressed word-final positions, plain **a** is almost always pronounced as the reduced `[ɐ]`.

## Contrasts and Common Mistakes

Do not pronounce every **a** with a bright, open quality as in Spanish or Italian. For instance, in the word *casa* (`/ˈka.zɐ/`), the first vowel is open, but the final vowel must be reduced to `[ɐ]`. A frequent mistake for English speakers is to lengthen the unstressed final syllable instead of clipping it short and central.
