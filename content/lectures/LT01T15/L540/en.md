## The Sound of the Letter

The letter **N** in European Portuguese usually sounds like the English `n` in *net* when it appears before a vowel, represented as `/n/`. However, at the end of a syllable or before another consonant, it is not pronounced as a distinct consonant; instead, it nasalises the preceding vowel, as in *canto* (`/ˈkɐ̃.tu/`). This nasalisation is softer and does not end with a full tongue contact on the alveolar ridge.

## How to Produce the Sound

To pronounce the initial sound `/n/`, place the tip of your tongue against the alveolar ridge behind your upper teeth while letting air flow through your nose and vibrating your vocal cords. When **N** closes a syllable, keep your tongue resting in the mouth rather than touching the roof, letting the air escape simultaneously through your nose and mouth to produce a pure nasal vowel. Do not press the tongue up at the end of nasal vowels.

## Spelling and Positions

**N** can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of syllables. When combined with **H** as the digraph **NH**, it represents the palatal nasal `/ɲ/`, similar to the sound in *canyon*. At the end of words, true **N** is rare in native Portuguese words, with final nasal sounds usually represented by **M** instead.

## Variants and Pitfalls

The main variation is between the clear alveolar consonant `/n/` before vowels (such as *noite*) and the vowel-nasalising function inside syllables (such as *ponte*). English speakers frequently make the mistake of pronouncing a hard English `/n/` or `/ŋ/` before consonants in words like *canto*. Remember to simply nasalise the vowel without completing a dental or velar closure.
