## The Sound of J

The letter **J** in European Portuguese always represents the voiced postalveolar fricative `/ʒ/`. It sounds exactly like the 's' in English **measure** or **vision**, or the 'g' in **genre**. Unlike English, it is never pronounced as an affricate `/dʒ/` like the 'j' in **jump**.

## Mouth and Tongue Placement

Gently round your lips and push them slightly outward. Place the blade of your tongue close to the alveolar ridge just behind your upper teeth, allowing air to squeeze through continuously. Simultaneously vibrate your vocal cords to make the sound voiced and smooth.

## Spelling Rules and Context

The letter **J** appears before all vowels (**a**, **e**, **i**, **o**, **u**) and consistently keeps the `/ʒ/` sound regardless of its position. Before **e** and **i**, it shares the exact same sound with the letter **G** (as in **gente**). Unlike some languages, Portuguese never doubles this consonant as 'jj'.

## Avoiding Mistakes

English speakers often default to an explosive `/dʒ/` sound, which should be strictly avoided in standard European Portuguese. Spanish speakers must also take care not to pronounce it as a harsh guttural `/x/` or `/h/`. Always ensure the airflow is smooth, buzzing, and continuous in words like **jogo** and **jardim**.
