## The Sound of Y

The letter **Y** (*ípsilon*) is not historically native to European Portuguese and appears strictly in foreign loanwords, symbols, and proper names. It is pronounced either as the palatal glide `[j]`, like the **y** in English *yes*, or as the pure vowel `[i]`, identical to the **ee** in *feet*. Unlike the German *ü* or French *u*, it never produces a rounded front vowel.

## Pronouncing the Letter

To articulate the semivowel `[j]`, spread your lips into a smile and raise the front of your tongue close to the hard palate. Allow air to pass through continuously while immediately transitioning into the following vowel sound. If it serves as a pure vowel, keep the tongue blade high and fronted without letting the mouth glide.

## Spelling and Usage

Following the 1990 Orthographic Agreement, **Y** is formally part of the 26-letter Portuguese alphabet, though its role is restricted. You will encounter it primarily in international terminology such as **yoga** or scientific symbols like **yd** (yard). In traditional Portuguese vocabulary, this sound is almost universally spelled with **I**.

## Nuances and Common Pitfalls

Notice the difference between the glide `[j]` in **yoga** (`/ˈjɔ.ɡɐ/`) and the full vowel `[i]` in names like **Sydney** (`/ˈsid.nej/`). A common mistake for learners from Germanic backgrounds is attempting to pronounce **Y** with rounded lips as `[y]`. In European Portuguese, always keep your lips completely unrounded as you would for a standard *i*.
