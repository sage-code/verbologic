## Sound of the Letter G

The Portuguese letter **G** has two main sounds depending on the vowel that follows. Before **a**, **o**, or **u**, it represents a hard voiced velar plosive `/ɡ/`, just like the English 'g' in 'gate'. When placed before **e** or **i**, it softens into a voiced postalveolar fricative `/ʒ/`, identical to the 's' in English 'measure'.

## How to Pronounce It

For the hard sound `/ɡ/`, press the back of your tongue against the soft palate at the back of your mouth, build up slight air pressure, and release it while vibrating your vocal cords. For the soft sound `/ʒ/`, gently arch the front of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth behind the upper teeth and let voiced air flow smoothly through the narrow channel with relaxed lips.

## Spelling Rules

To maintain the hard `/ɡ/` sound before **e** or **i**, Portuguese spelling requires inserting a silent **u**, forming the digraph **gu** as in *guerra* (`/ˈɡɛ.ʁɐ/`). Conversely, if a `/ʒ/` sound appears before **a**, **o**, or **u**, the letter **J** must be used instead, as in *janela*.

## Comparing Variants and Common Mistakes

Compare the hard `/ɡ/` in *gato* (`/ˈɡa.tu/`) with the soft `/ʒ/` in *gente* (`/ˈʒẽ.tɨ/`). A frequent mistake for English speakers is pronouncing the soft **G** with an affricate `/dʒ/` (as in 'judge'); be sure to produce a smooth, continuous friction sound without any initial 'd' stop.
