## Sound of the Letter H

In European Portuguese, standalone **H** is completely silent and produces no sound whatsoever. Unlike English, where 'h' often represents an aspirated breath like `[h]`, Portuguese words beginning with **H** start directly on the vowel. It only modifies pronunciation when combined into digraphs like **ch** (`/ʃ/`), **lh** (`/ʎ/`), and **nh** (`/ɲ/`).

## How to Pronounce H

When you encounter an isolated **H** at the beginning of a word, simply do not produce any breath or friction. Relax your vocal tract entirely and prepare your mouth directly for the following vowel. Do not attempt to exhale or add an English-style aspiration.

## Spelling and Rules

Isolated **H** appears primarily at the start of words for etymological reasons inherited from Latin or Greek, as in **homem** or **hora**. It never appears alone at the end of native words. Crucially, it forms the bedrock of three essential Portuguese digraphs: **ch**, **lh**, and **nh**.

## Usage and Common Mistakes

The most frequent error for English speakers is aspirating initial **H** in words like **hotel** (`/ɔˈtɛɫ/`) or **hoje** (`/ˈo.ʒɨ/`). Always treat the letter as completely invisible unless it is paired behind **c**, **l**, or **n**. Pronouncing an audible breath can sound unnatural and distort the natural vowel linking between words.
