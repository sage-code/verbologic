## The Sounds of C

In European Portuguese, the letter **C** represents two entirely distinct sounds depending on what letter follows it. Before the vowels **a**, **o**, or **u**, or before a consonant, it sounds like `/k/` as in *cat*, but unlike in English, it is pronounced with almost no aspiration (a puff of air). Before the front vowels **e** and **i**, it softens to a voiceless sibilant `/s/`, exactly like the *s* in *sun*.

## How to Pronounce C

For the hard sound `/k/`, press the back of your tongue against the soft palate at the roof of your mouth, build up slight air pressure, and release it crisply without an extra breath. For the soft sound `/s/`, bring the tip or blade of your tongue very close to the alveolar ridge behind your upper front teeth. Blow a continuous, narrow stream of air across the ridge without vibrating your vocal cords.

## Spelling and Context

The hard variant occurs before **a**, **o**, and **u** (e.g., *carro*, *curto*) or before consonants (e.g., *claro*, *acto*). Before **e** or **i**, it consistently shifts to the soft sound (e.g., *centro*, *cinema*). If Portuguese requires a `/s/` sound before **a**, **o**, or **u**, the cedilla letter **Ç** is used instead, as in *coração*.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Notice the difference between the hard `/k/` in **casa** (`/ˈka.zɐ/`) and the soft `/s/` in **cedo** (`/ˈse.du/`). English speakers often make the mistake of heavily aspirating the `/k/`, which sounds foreign in Portuguese; keep it tight and clean. Another frequent error is pronouncing soft **C** before **e** or **i** as an English *ch* (`/tʃ/`), which never happens in standard European Portuguese.
