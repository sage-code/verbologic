## The Sound of K

In European Portuguese, the letter **K** represents the voiceless velar plosive `/k/`, identical to the 'c' in *cat* or 'k' in *skate*. Unlike in English, this sound is pronounced with almost no aspiration (no strong puff of air) following the burst. Because the letter is not native to Portuguese vocabulary, it appears strictly in loanwords, international acronyms, and metric abbreviations.

## How to Produce the Sound

Raise the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate (velum) to stop the airflow completely. Build up gentle air pressure behind the closure without voicing. Release the seal crisply into the following vowel without adding an extra breathy 'h' sound.

## Spelling Rules & Position

Officially integrated into the Portuguese alphabet via the 1990 Orthographic Agreement, **K** remains restricted to loanwords, brand names, and derived forms like *kantiano*. Native Portuguese words exclusively use **C** (before *a*, *o*, *u*) or the digraph **QU** (before *e*, *i*) to represent the sound `/k/`. Capitalization follows international standards, such as **km** for kilometre or **K** for potassium.

## Variants and Pitfalls

The letter **K** has only one pronunciation in Portuguese: `/k/`. English speakers should avoid heavy aspiration, especially before front vowels like `/i/` in words like *kilo*. Additionally, take care not to confuse spelling habits; standard Portuguese terms use *quilo* or *quilómetro*, while **kilo** and **km** are maintained as metric symbols or foreign borrowings.
