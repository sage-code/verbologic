## The Sound of U

In European Portuguese, the letter **U** normally sounds like the close back vowel `/u/`, similar to the 'oo' in English 'boot' but noticeably tighter and shorter. In unstressed syllables, it keeps this pure round quality without drifting into the lax vowel found in English 'foot'. When followed by **m** or **n** in the same syllable, as in **um**, it becomes fully nasalized as `/ũ/`.

## How to Pronounce It

Round your lips firmly into a tight, small circle and pull your tongue body high up toward the soft palate at the back of your mouth. Keep the vocal cords vibrating while holding the tongue steady, avoiding any glide into a diphthong. For nasal variants like **um**, lower your soft palate so air escapes through your nose simultaneously.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **U** appears in stressed and unstressed roots alike, such as **uva** or **luta**. It also acts as a glide in diphthongs like **mau** and follows consonants in combinations like **qu-** and **gu-**, where it is sometimes silent before **e** or **i** (as in **querer**) but pronounced before **a** (as in **quase**). When written with an acute accent (**ú**), it signals an irregular stressed syllable.

## Variants and Pitfalls

The standard vowel `/u/` contrasts with the semivowel `/w/` found in rapid diphthongs (**água**) and the nasal vowel `/ũ/` (**fundo**). English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing **u** as `/juː/` (like in 'music' or 'cute') after consonants like **d**, **t**, or **n**. Remember that Portuguese **U** is always a pure monophthong unless explicitly written with an accompanying vowel.
