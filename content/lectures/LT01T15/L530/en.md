## Sound of the Letter

The letter **D** in European Portuguese represents a voiced dental plosive `/d/` or its soft approximant counterpart `[ð]`. Unlike English, where 'd' is alveolar and pronounced against the gum ridge behind your teeth, the Portuguese **D** is strictly dental, with the tongue tip touching the back of the upper front teeth. Notably, it never turns into an English 'j' sound `[dʒ]` before the vowel 'i', remaining crisp and purely dental.

## How to Say It

Place the tip or blade of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front incisors to block the airflow. Build up a small amount of air pressure behind the closure, engage your vocal cords to create voicing, and release the contact cleanly. In casual speech between two vowels, soften the contact slightly without completely stopping the airflow, creating a smooth friction similar to the 'th' in 'this'.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **D** appears in uppercase as **D** and lowercase as **d**, commonly found at the beginning of words and between vowels. It rarely appears at the very end of native Portuguese words, with the exception of a few recent loanwords. Double 'dd' does not exist in standard Portuguese spelling.

## Accents and Pitfalls

The most frequent error for learners familiar with Brazilian Portuguese or English is affricating **D** before 'i' or unstressed 'e', pronouncing *dia* as 'jee-ah'. In European Portuguese, *dia* must always start with a clean `/d/`. Additionally, ensure you soften **d** into a voiced dental approximant `[ð]` between vowels in words like *vida* or *nada* to achieve an authentic, natural cadence.
