## Sound of the letter

At the beginning of a syllable, the letter **M** represents the bilabial nasal consonant `/m/`, exactly like the English 'm' in *mother*. However, when it appears at the end of a syllable or word, it is not pronounced as a physical consonant; instead, it nasalises the preceding vowel and often generates a nasal glide, as in `bem` (`/ˈbɐ̃j̃/`).

## How to say it

To pronounce `/m/` before a vowel, close your lips firmly together and vibrate your vocal cords while letting the air escape freely through your nose. When **M** closes a syllable, do not close your lips to articulate an 'm' sound; simply keep your mouth in the position of the vowel and lower your soft palate to redirect the airstream through the nasal cavity.

## Spelling conventions

The letter **M** appears initially, medially, and word-finally in European Portuguese. Before the consonants **b** and **p**, the nasal marker is always written with **m** rather than **n** (as in *campo* or *lembrar*). At the end of words, it commonly marks nasal diphthongs, such as the third-person plural verb ending *-em* or *-am*.

## Variants and common mistakes

Contrast the clear bilabial `/m/` in *mar* with the syllable-closing nasalisation in *bom* (`/ˈbõ/`). A very common mistake among English speakers is pressing their lips together at the end of words like *falam* or *viagem*; ensure your lips stay open and let only the nasal vowel or diphthong carry through.
