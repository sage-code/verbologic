## The Sound of V

In European Portuguese, the letter **V** represents the voiced labiodental fricative `/v/`. It sounds exactly like the English 'v' in 'vine' or 'voice'. Unlike Spanish, standard European Portuguese maintains a sharp, clear distinction between the sounds of **b** and **v**.

## How to Produce /v/

Rest your upper front teeth lightly against your lower lip, just as you would for an English 'v' or 'f'. Push air through the narrow contact while vibrating your vocal cords. Ensure the sound remains a sustained friction rather than an explosive stop like **b**.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **V** appears at the beginning of words and between vowels, as in **vinho** (wine) and **ouvir** (to hear). It never combines with other letters to form silent digraphs and retains its voiced fricative value consistently across standard European Portuguese orthography.

## Regional Accents and Common Pitfalls

Standard Portuguese keeps `/v/` and `/b/` strictly separated, contrasting words like **vale** (valley) and **bale** (dance). However, learners should be aware that in certain northern dialects of Portugal, **v** is frequently merged with **b** (producing `/b/`). Avoid this northern merger unless aiming for a specific regional dialect.
