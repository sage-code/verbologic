## The Sound of T

The Portuguese letter **T** represents a voiceless dental plosive, written as `/t/`. Unlike English /t/, which is pronounced behind the teeth on the alveolar ridge and accompanied by a puff of air, European Portuguese `/t/` is entirely dental and completely unaspirated. Moreover, unlike Brazilian Portuguese, it never softens into a "ch" sound before `i` or unstressed `e`.

## How to Pronounce T

Touch the tip of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front teeth rather than the gum ridge behind them. Build up slight air pressure behind the seal and release it cleanly without letting out a burst of breath (aspiration). Keep the tongue firm and avoid turning the sound into an affricate.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **T** appears at the beginning of syllables across all word positions, as in **tempo** (`/ˈtẽ.pu/`) and **noite** (`/ˈnoj.tɨ/`). It also occurs in learned consonant clusters such as **ct** (e.g. **facto**), where it remains clearly articulated. In native vocabulary, double **tt** is not used in modern Portuguese spelling.

## Regional Comparisons and Common Pitfalls

English speakers must avoid releasing a breathy puff of air, as well as placing the tongue too far back on the alveolar ridge. The most crucial contrast to bear in mind is with Brazilian Portuguese: in Portugal, **noite** is pronounced `/ˈnoj.tɨ/` with a crisp dental `/t/`, never with an affricate like `[tʃ]`. Keep the articulation forward, sharp, and dry in every environment.
