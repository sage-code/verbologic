## The Sound of W

In European Portuguese, the letter **W** is not native and appears almost exclusively in foreign loanwords, international acronyms, and names. In English borrowings like **web**, it represents the labio-velar glide `[w]`, sounding exactly like the English 'w'. In German loanwords or proper names, it is pronounced as the voiced labiodental fricative `[v]`, matching the sound of native Portuguese **v**.

## How to Produce It

For English loanwords, round your lips tightly into a small circle while pulling the back of your tongue toward your soft palate, producing a smooth glide `[w]`. For German borrowings, rest your upper front teeth lightly on your lower lip and engage your vocal cords to create the buzzing friction of `[v]`. Avoid adding an extra vowel sound before the glide.

## Spelling Rules and Usage

The letter **W** was officially integrated into the Portuguese alphabet under the 1990 Orthographic Agreement. It is exclusively used for foreign words, scientific symbols such as **W** for watt, and proper nouns like **Washington**. Outside of these loan contexts, the sound `[w]` is naturally represented in native words by the vowel letter **u**.

## Variants and Potential Pitfalls

The main variation depends entirely on the language of origin: it yields `[w]` in terms like **whisky** and `[v]` in Germanic terms like **wagneriano**. A frequent mistake for learners is attempting to pronounce English borrowings with a `[v]` sound out of an overgeneralized assumption that all European languages do so. Always adapt the pronunciation according to the origin of the loanword.
