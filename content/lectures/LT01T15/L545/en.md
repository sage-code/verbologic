## Sound Overview

The letter **S** is one of the most versatile consonants in European Portuguese, changing its sound depending on its surrounding letters. At the start of a word, as in `sol`, it sounds just like the crisp English voiceless `s` in *sun*. However, in other positions it regularly transforms into a voiced `z`, a soft `ʃ` (like English *sh*), or a voiced `ʒ` (like the *s* in *measure*).

## Mouth & Tongue Positioning

To pronounce the initial sound in `sol` (`/s/`), keep your tongue tip close to the alveolar ridge just behind your upper teeth without touching it. Allow a steady, voiceless stream of air to hiss through the narrow central groove of your tongue. Your vocal cords should remain completely still and relaxed.

## Spelling Patterns

At the beginning of a word, a single **S** is always pronounced `/s/`. Between two vowels, a single **S** sounds like `/z/`, which is why words needing a voiceless sound in that position are spelled with double **SS**, such as *passo*. At the end of syllables or words, it automatically turns into a palatal fricative (`/ʃ/` or `/ʒ/`).

## Pronunciation Variations & Pitfalls

Notice how the sound shifts across environments: compare **sol** (`/s/`), **casa** (`/z/`), and **festa** (`/ʃ/`). A frequent mistake for English speakers is pronouncing the coda **S** as a flat `s` or `z` instead of European Portuguese's signature palatal `sh` sound. Pay careful attention when linking words in connected speech, where a final **S** before a vowel becomes `/z/`.
