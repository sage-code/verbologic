## The Sound of L

In European Portuguese, the letter **L** has two main sounds depending on its position. Before a vowel, it is a clear `[l]` like the English 'l' in 'light'. Syllable-finally, it becomes a prominent velarised or **dark L** `[ɫ]`, similar to the 'l' in English 'ball' or 'milk', but even more resonant.

## How to Pronounce It

For the clear `[l]`, place the tip of your tongue against the upper gum ridge and let voice flow around the sides. For the dark `[ɫ]` at the end of a syllable, keep the tongue tip near or on the gums while simultaneously arching the back of your tongue toward the soft palate. Avoid turning the final **L** into a 'w' sound.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **L** is written as uppercase **L** and lowercase **l**. It regularly appears alone or doubled (**ll**, mostly found in loanwords), and combines with other consonants in clusters like **bl**, **cl**, or **fl**. Note that the digraph **lh** represents a completely different palatal sound `[ʎ]`.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Notice the difference between the initial clear sound in **lua** `[ˈlu.ɐ]` and the dark sound in **sol** `[sɔɫ]`. Brazilian Portuguese speakers often turn final **L** into `[w]` (pronouncing **sol** like 'sou'), but European Portuguese maintains a distinct velarised consonant `[ɫ]`. English speakers should be careful not to drop the tongue-tip contact when producing this syllable-final sound.
