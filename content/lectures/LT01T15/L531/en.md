## The Sounds of Letter E

The letter **E** is one of the most versatile vowels in European Portuguese. In unstressed positions, it frequently reduces to a unique close central vowel `[ɨ]`—akin to a very short, muffled murmur—or to `[i]` at the start of words like in **escola**. Under stress, it splits into a closed `[e]` (like in "hey" without the glide) or an open `[ɛ]` (like in "bed").

## Pronunciation Guide

To produce the typical unstressed European Portuguese sound `[ɨ]`, place your tongue halfway between `[i]` and `[u]` with completely relaxed, unrounded lips, almost muttering behind clenched teeth. For the closed `[e]`, lift the tongue body high and keep the lips unrounded, forming a clean, pure sound without drifting into an English 'y' glide. For the open `[ɛ]`, drop your lower jaw slightly lower, keeping the tongue flat and tension low.

## Spelling and Written Accents

Written **E** is accented to resolve ambiguity: the circumflex (**ê**) forces the closed sound `[e]`, while the acute accent (**é**) indicates the open sound `[ɛ]`. Unaccented **E** adapts dramatically depending on its rhythm within the word, often becoming so reduced in speech that native speakers nearly drop it entirely.

## Key Contrasts and Mistakes to Avoid

Learners often mistakenly pronounce unstressed **E** as an open `[e]` or `[ɛ]` as they would in Spanish or Brazilian Portuguese; this immediately gives away a foreign accent. Make sure not to turn unstressed final **-e** (as in **noite**) into an English "ee" sound—keep it muffled as `[ɨ]` or let it fade into near silence.
