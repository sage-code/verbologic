## The Sound of the Letter I

In European Portuguese, the letter **I** most commonly represents the close front unrounded vowel `/i/`, much like the 'ee' in English *see* or *machine*, but articulated with slightly greater tension. Unlike some English varieties that relax this sound into a loose `/ɪ/` (as in *sit*), Portuguese keeps it crisp and bright. It can also function as the glide `/j/` when forming diphthongs alongside other vowels.

## How to Produce the Sound

Spread your lips into a gentle smile and raise the front of your tongue high towards the hard palate, keeping it tense without quite touching. The edges of your tongue should rest gently against your upper side teeth while your vocal cords vibrate freely. Ensure you do not slacken your jaw into an English short 'i' sound; hold the position firmly.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **I** appears across all word positions, whether initial (*ilha*), medial (*vida*), or final (*quase* sometimes sounds similar, though spelled with 'e'). An acute accent (**í**) marks an irregular stress on this sound (*país*, *saúde*), particularly to break an otherwise natural diphthong via hiatus. When followed or preceded by another vowel in the same syllable, it acts as a glide without requiring a dedicated spelling change.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

While usually pronounced `/i/` in words like *fita*, it transforms smoothly into `/j/` in diphthongs such as *foi* or *noite*. English speakers often make the mistake of reducing unstressed Portuguese **i** to a lax `/ɪ/` as in *bit*, which sounds unnatural. Remember to keep the vowel clean, bright, and tense across all syllables.
