## The Sound of Q

In European Portuguese, the letter **Q** always appears combined with **U**. Depending on the vowel that follows, the combination is either pronounced as a single voiceless velar stop `/k/` or as the cluster `/kw/`. Unlike in English, it never produces a standalone sound without this pairing.

## Pronouncing Q

To produce the base sound, press the back of your tongue against the soft palate and release a sharp burst of unvoiced air without vibrating your vocal cords. When followed by **a** or **o**, round your lips tightly immediately upon release to produce the glide `/w/`. When followed by **e** or **i**, keep your lips neutral and articulate only the clean `/k/`.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **Q** is never written without an accompanying **u** in native Portuguese vocabulary. Before the vowels **e** and **i**, the digraph `qu` serves purely as an orthographic device to keep the hard `/k/` sound intact. Before **a** and **o**, the **u** is fully vocalised as a glide.

## Sounds and Pitfalls

The primary challenge for learners is knowing when the **u** is voiced. In words like **quatro** `/ˈkwa.tɾu/`, you must articulate the `/w/`, whereas in **quero** `/ˈkɛ.ɾu/` or **quem** `/kɐ̃j̃/`, the **u** is completely silent. Avoid pronouncing the English 'kw' sound before front vowels like **e** and **i**.
