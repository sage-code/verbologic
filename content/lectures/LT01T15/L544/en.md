## The Sounds of R

In European Portuguese, the letter **R** represents two distinct phonemes depending on its position: a strong guttural sound `/ʁ/` and a soft alveolar tap `/ɾ/`. The strong **R** at the start of words or spelled **rr** is articulated deep in the throat, much like the French or modern German **r**. The weak single **R** between vowels is a quick flick of the tongue against the gum ridge, identical to the tapped sound in Spanish **caro** or American English **water**.

## How to Pronounce R

To make the strong sound `/ʁ/`, raise the back of your tongue toward the uvula at the rear of the soft palate and let the air friction pass without engaging the tongue tip. For the soft tap `/ɾ/`, keep the back of your tongue relaxed and quickly strike the alveolar ridge behind your upper front teeth with the tip of your tongue once. Never curl your tongue backward into the retroflex American or English 'r'.

## Spelling Patterns

A single initial **R** (as in **rua**) or an internal double **rr** (as in **carro**) always triggers the strong guttural `/ʁ/`. A single internal **R** between vowels (as in **caro**) or after an initial consonant (like **prato**) is always the tapped `/ɾ/`. At the end of syllables or words like **falar**, the tap `/ɾ/` is standard, though it is articulated very lightly and often almost silenced in casual rapid speech.

## Contrasting Sounds and Common Mistakes

The most critical distinction in Portuguese is between the tap in **caro** (`/ˈka.ɾu/`, expensive) and the guttural roll in **carro** (`/ˈka.ʁu/`, car), as swapping them alters the meaning entirely. English speakers frequently substitute their native postalveolar approximant `/ɹ/`, which makes words sound heavily accented or unintelligible. Pay careful attention to whether **R** is single or doubled, and articulate the correct mouth position accordingly.
