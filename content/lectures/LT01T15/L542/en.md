## The Sound of European Portuguese P

The letter **P** in European Portuguese represents an unaspirated voiceless bilabial plosive `/p/`. Unlike English `p` at the beginning of words, which is accompanied by a puff of air, the Portuguese sound is produced crisply without any aspiration. It sounds very close to the `p` in the English word *spin* rather than *pin*.

## How to Pronounce P

Bring both lips completely together to block the airflow from your lungs momentarily. Build up slight air pressure behind the lips without vibrating your vocal cords. Then, release the lips cleanly and sharply into the following vowel without letting out an extra puff of breath.

## Spelling and Rules for P

The letter **P** occurs at the beginning of words like **pão** and between vowels as in **copo**. In older orthographies, silent consonants were common, but under the current Orthographic Agreement, silent **p** has been eliminated in words where it was unpronounced (such as *ação* instead of *acção*). However, it remains fully pronounced when written in learned clusters like **apto** (`/ˈa.ptu/`) or **psicologia** (`/psi.ku.lu.ˈʒi.ɐ/`).

## Accent Tips and Common Mistakes

English speakers must avoid aspirating this sound, as producing a breathy `[pʰ]` will instantly reveal an accent. Compare **pão** (`/ˈpɐ̃w̃/`) with the English word *pound*; keep the lips tight and the release dry. In consonant sequences such as **captar** or **pneu**, ensure the `/p/` is audibly articulated rather than dropped.
