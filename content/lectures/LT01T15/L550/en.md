## The Sound of X

In European Portuguese, the letter **X** is famous for having multiple phonetic values, though its primary and most iconic sound is the voiceless postalveolar fricative `/ʃ/`. This default sound is virtually identical to the English **sh** in *shoe* or *crush*. However, depending on etymology and position, **X** can also sound like `/z/`, `/s/`, or `/ks/`.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce the default `/ʃ/` sound, round your lips slightly and raise the blade of your tongue toward the hard palate just behind your alveolar ridge. Expel air smoothly through this narrow channel without vibrating your vocal cords. For the voiced `/z/` variant (as in *exame*), simply place your tongue tip near the upper teeth and vibrate your vocal cords like a buzzing bee.

## Spelling and Patterns

At the start of native words like *xadrez* or *xícara*, and following diphthongs like in *caixa*, **X** is invariably pronounced `/ʃ/`. When the prefix **ex-** is followed by a vowel, it systematically shifts to `/z/`, as in *exercício*. Learned or international words usually preserve `/ks/` (e.g., *táxi*), while a handful of historic exceptions use `/s/` (e.g., *máximo*).

## Variants and Common Traps

Learners commonly make the mistake of pronouncing every **X** as `/ks/` due to English or Spanish habits. Remember that native Portuguese words almost never use the `/ks/` cluster; words like *peixe* or *baixo* must strictly have the soft `/ʃ/` sound. Be cautious with words like *próximo* (`/ˈpɾɔ.si.mu/`), where pronouncing `/ks/` sounds overly foreign to native ears.
