## The Sound of 'cl'

The consonant blend **cl** represents the sequence `/kl/`, combining a voiceless velar stop with an alveolar lateral approximant. In English, when `/l/` follows an aspirated stop like `/k/` at the start of a stressed syllable, the `/l/` is often partially devoiced. It sounds very crisp compared to more voiced or darker consonant clusters found in some other languages.

## How to Pronounce It

Start by pressing the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate to block the airflow for `/k/`. As you release this burst of air, immediately place the tip of your tongue against the upper gum ridge behind your front teeth to let the sound flow out laterally for `/l/`. Ensure the transition is seamless without inserting any faint vowel sound between the two consonants.

## Spelling and Patterns

This blend is almost universally spelled with the letters **cl** at the start of words like *clear*, *class*, and *climb*. At the ends of words, the same consonant cluster is commonly spelled as **-cle**, as seen in *circle* or *article*, where it often forms a syllabic `/kl̩/`. It rarely changes spelling unless borrowed directly from Greek forms using *chl-* like *chlorine*.

## Common Pitfalls

Learners often make the mistake of inserting an epenthetic vowel, pronouncing *clean* as `*kə-leen`. Another common issue is failing to devoice the `/l/` slightly after the strong burst of `/k/`, which can make the onset sound unnatural to native ears. Keep the two consonants tightly fused together without separating the tongue movements.
