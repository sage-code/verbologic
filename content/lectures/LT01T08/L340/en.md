## The Sound of "st"

The consonant cluster **st** represents the sequential blend `/st/`, combining a voiceless alveolar sibilant with a voiceless alveolar stop. Crucially, when the `/t/` follows `/s/` within the same syllable, it is pronounced **unaspirated**, meaning there is no puff of air like in standalone words like *tea*. Learners often over-aspirate the `/t/`, which can sound unnatural to native speakers.

## How to Pronounce /st/

Begin by placing the tip of your tongue near the alveolar ridge behind your upper front teeth to produce a hissing `/s/`. Without pausing, press the tongue tip firmly against the ridge to completely stop the airflow for the `/t/`. Finally, release the contact cleanly into the following vowel or silence without adding a burst of breath.

## Spelling and Positions

The cluster **st** occurs very frequently across English vocabulary in initial, medial, and final positions. Common examples include words like *stop*, *master*, and *past*. Note that in a handful of words such as *listen*, *castle*, and *whistle*, the letter **t** is completely silent, leaving only the `/s/` sound.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Speakers of languages like Spanish often add an extra vowel sound at the beginning, pronouncing *stop* as *"estop"*, which should be strictly avoided. Additionally, speakers of German may instinctively pronounce initial **st** as `/ʃt/` (like *Stadt*), but in English it must always remain a crisp `/st/`. Ensure the `/t/` is released sharply without aspiration.
