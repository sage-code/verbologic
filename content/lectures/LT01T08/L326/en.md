## The Sound of 'oi'

The vowel digraph **oi** represents the closing diphthong `/ɔɪ/`. It starts on an open-mid back rounded vowel like the 'aw' in *thought* and glides smoothly into a near-close front unrounded vowel like the 'i' in *bit*. It sounds very similar to the 'oy' in *boy*.

## How to Produce the Sound

Begin with your jaw relaxed and slightly dropped, shaping your lips into a loose, open circle as if saying `/ɔː/`. Then, push your tongue upward and forward toward the roof of your mouth while unrounding your lips. The movement should be continuous and fluid within a single syllable.

## Spelling and Distribution

This spelling pattern appears almost exclusively in the **middle** or at the **beginning** of root words, such as in *oil*, *join*, and *voice*. At the end of English root words, this exact same sound is virtually always spelled with **oy** instead (as in *toy* or *joy*).

## Common Traps and Variations

Unlike vowels such as 'ea' or 'oo', the digraph **oi** is exceptionally stable and almost never varies in native English words. The most frequent pitfall for learners is pronouncing each letter separately as two distinct vowels instead of a single, unified gliding diphthong.
