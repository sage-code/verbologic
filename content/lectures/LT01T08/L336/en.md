## The Sound of CR

The consonant cluster **cr** represents the sequence `/kr/`. It combines the voiceless velar stop `/k/` with a smooth, postalveolar approximant `/r/`. Because `/r/` immediately follows the unvoiced `/k/`, the `/r/` sound often becomes partially devoiced, sounding slightly breathy.

## How to Pronounce CR

Start by raising the back of your tongue against the soft palate to trap air for the `/k/`, then release it sharply. As you release the stop, curl or bunch the tip of your tongue slightly backward behind your tooth ridge without letting it touch the roof of your mouth. Round your lips slightly to facilitate the transition into the English `/r/`.

## Spelling and Distribution

This blend appears almost exclusively at the beginning of words and roots, written as lowercase **cr** or capitalized **Cr**. Common examples include everyday vocabulary such as *crash*, *cream*, and *crowd*. In English spelling, **cr** consistently yields this exact sound without silent letters.

## Common Traps and Comparisons

Learners whose native languages have a trilled or tapped *r* often roll the consonant in **cr**, which sounds unnatural in English. Avoid letting your tongue tip tap the alveolar ridge; the English `/r/` in `/kr/` is smooth and gliding. Additionally, do not insert a weak vowel between the two consonants, keeping the transition from `/k/` to `/r/` tight.
