## The Sound of 'ee'

The digraph **ee** represents the long, tense close front vowel `/iː/`. It sounds like the vowel in **fleece**, pronounced with more muscular tension and length than a short lax `[ɪ]`. Unlike some European languages where 'e' is mid-front, in English double **ee** is almost always high and front.

## How to Produce the Sound

Spread your lips slightly as if you are giving a gentle smile. Raise the front of your tongue high toward the hard palate without touching it, keeping the tongue body tense. Hold your vocal cords vibrating steadily to produce a clear, sustained `/iː/`.

## Spelling and Distribution

This spelling pattern appears frequently in the middle and at the end of root words, such as in **deep**, **tree**, and **between**. Unlike single **e**, the double **ee** spelling almost never reduces to a schwa, making it one of the most reliable indicators of the `/iː/` sound in English orthography.

## Common Traps and Contrasts

Learners often confuse the long tense `/iː/` in **sheep** with the short lax `/ɪ/` in **ship**, leading to misunderstandings. Keep your tongue tense and held higher for **ee** than you would for standard short vowels. Avoid shortening this vowel prematurely when followed by voiced consonants like in **seed**.
