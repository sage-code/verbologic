## The Sound of OU

The digraph **ou** most characteristically represents the diphthong `/aʊ/`, as heard in **house** or **cloud**. It begins with an open front-to-central vowel and glides smoothly up toward a rounded back vowel. Unlike a simple vowel, it requires continuous articulatory movement throughout the syllable.

## How to Pronounce /aʊ/

Open your mouth fairly wide with your tongue low and flat to produce the initial open sound. Then, seamlessly glide your tongue backward and upward toward the soft palate while rounding your lips into a small circle. Keep the airflow steady and make sure the transition feels smooth rather than stepped.

## Spelling and Patterns

The spelling **ou** usually appears in the middle of native English root words like **sound** and **round**, whereas **ow** often takes its place at word ends. However, **ou** is famously irregular, also spelling `/uː/` in **soup**, `/ʌ/` in **touch**, and `/oʊ/` in **dough**. Watch out for morphological boundaries and common high-frequency exceptions.

## Variants and Common Mistakes

Learners frequently confuse the primary `/aʊ/` sound with the long monophthong `/uː/` or the vowel `/oʊ/`. Avoid pronouncing words like **house** as 'hoos' or 'hose'. Pay close attention to word etymology, as French borrowings consistently preserve `/uː/` (e.g., **route**, **group**) rather than the regular diphthong.
