## The Sound of 'ai'

The vowel digraph **ai** most commonly produces the diphthong `/eɪ/`, which glides from an open-mid front vowel toward a high front position. It sounds identical to the 'long A' sound found in words like **name** or **day**. When followed by the letter 'r', it shifts to the centring diphthong `/ɛə/` as in **pair**.

## Pronouncing 'ai'

Start with your jaw mid-open and unrounded lips, placing your tongue in the position for a short 'e' sound. Smoothly glide your tongue upward and slightly forward toward the roof of your mouth, ending near a relaxed 'ee' shape. Keep your vocal cords vibrating throughout this smooth continuous transition.

## Spelling Patterns

This combination usually appears in the middle of base words such as **train**, **wait**, and **brain**. English spelling conventions typically prefer **ay** at the end of root words rather than **ai**. In suffixes like '-tain', it is frequently reduced to a weak `/ɪ/` or `/ə/`, as heard in **captain**.

## Common Traps and Variations

A frequent mistake for non-native speakers is pronouncing **ai** as two separate vowels or as a pure monophthong like `/e/`. Be sure to produce a true glide rather than a static vowel sound. Also remember that before 'r' (e.g., **fair**), the sound changes entirely to `/ɛə/` rather than the standard `/eɪ/`.
