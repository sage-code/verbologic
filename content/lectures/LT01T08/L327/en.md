## The Sound of 'oy'

The digraph **oy** represents the closing diphthong `/ɔɪ/`, heard in words like *boy* or *toy*. It begins with an open-mid back vowel similar to the sound in *thought* and glides smoothly up toward a lax front vowel like the sound in *kit*. Unlike a simple monophthong, this sound requires a continuous, fluid movement of the vocal tract.

## How to Pronounce It

Start with your lips moderately rounded and the back of your tongue lowered in your mouth to produce the initial `/ɔ/` quality. Then, glide your tongue upward and forward toward the position of `/ɪ/`, relaxing your lips from rounded to neutral or slightly spread. Keep the vocal cords vibrating steadily throughout the glide so the sound remains unified.

## Spelling Patterns

The spelling **oy** typically appears at the end of syllables or words, such as in *joy*, *enjoy*, and *loyal*. When this exact same diphthong appears in the middle of a morpheme followed by a consonant, English usually spells it with **oi**, as in *coin* or *boil*. Exceptions are rare and usually occur in compound or borrowed words.

## Comparing and Nuances

Learners sometimes over-enunciate the second half of this sound, turning it into a tense `/iː/` instead of gliding gently into `/ɪ/`. Be careful not to separate the two elements into distinct syllables; *toy* must remain a crisp, single syllable rather than sounding like *to-ee*. In contrast to the monophthong `/ɔː/` in *saw*, make sure your tongue actively shifts forward.
