## Short and Sharp Melody

When you give an order in Romanian, you speak with clear energy. Commands do not sound like polite questions or relaxed stories. Instead, a command is brief, firm, and decisive. You push the air out quickly from your lungs. Your voice stays punchy across the whole phrase. Most importantly, your vocal pitch drops immediately on the final syllables. This steep downward melody tells the listener to act right away.

## How Your Mouth and Voice Move

To make this sound, start with good breath support. Open your mouth crisply for each vowel without slurring the sounds together. Push more air on the stressed syllable to give it a strong accent. Immediately after that peak, relax your vocal cords slightly so your pitch falls steeply. Stop the sound suddenly at the end of the phrase. Do not let your voice ring out or float. The airflow stops cleanly like turning off a switch.

## Practicing the Example

Listen closely to the audio for **Vino aici!** /ˈvi.no aˈitʃʲ/. This phrase means "Come here!" Notice how the first word starts with firm energy on the syllable *vi-*. The pitch rises slightly on that strong beat. Then, your voice drops quickly onto *-no*. When you reach the word *aici*, your pitch plunges down to the lowest note. The final soft sound stops short, leaving no lingering tone.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

English speakers often make commands sound like questions by raising their pitch at the end. That makes you sound hesitant or doubtful. In Romanian, never let your pitch float upward on an order. Another mistake is lengthening the last vowel. Do not stretch out the words to sound gentle. Keep your vowels short, maintain a firm rhythm, and let the voice fall directly downward.
