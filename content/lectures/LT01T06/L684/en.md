## The question pattern
A yes-no question is a question that you can answer with yes or no. In Romanian, the word order often stays exactly the same as a normal statement. You do not need to add extra helping words at the beginning. Because the words do not change, your voice must do the work. The melody of the sentence stays relatively flat across the early words. Then, your voice climbs steeply at the very end. That sudden climb tells the listener that you are asking a question.

## How your voice moves
Keep your throat relaxed during the first part of the sentence. Speak the early words with a steady, middle pitch. When you reach the final stressed syllable, push slightly more air from your lungs. Tighten your vocal cords just a little so the musical note rises sharply. Keep your mouth open clearly to let the vowel ring out. Do not let your voice drop back down after the rise. Imagine your pitch jumping up onto a high shelf and stopping right there.

## Practicing the example
Listen carefully to the Romanian question **Ești român?** /ˈeʃtʲ roˈmɨn/. This question means "Are you Romanian?". The first word starts at a normal, neutral speaking pitch. When you reach the word *român*, the stress lands firmly on the second syllable. As you pronounce the vowel sound in that last syllable, lift your pitch noticeably higher. Your mouth should stay gently open while your vocal tone finishes higher than where it started.

## Common mistakes to avoid
Many English speakers let their voice fall at the end of a sentence. If your pitch drops, the Romanian listener will hear a simple statement instead of a question. Another mistake is rising too early on the first word. If you raise your pitch too soon, the sentence melody feels uneven. Keep your voice steady on the opening words. Save the high jump for the final stressed vowel. This sharp lift makes your question clear and natural.
