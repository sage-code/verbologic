## What Three Dots Mean
In written Romanian, three dots are called an ellipsis. They show that a speaker stops for a moment. This break can mean hesitation, doubt, or deep thinking. It is longer than a normal comma pause. The voice does not drop down completely like at the end of a sentence. Instead, the sound hangs quietly in the air.

## How to Use Your Voice
When you reach the dots, keep your pitch slightly flat or gently drifting downward. Do not cut the sound off abruptly with your throat. Let the final vowel fade softly, as if you run out of breath. Then, hold your mouth completely still during the silence. Keep your vocal cords relaxed. When you start the next word, begin with a fresh, calm breath.

## Practice with an Example
Look at this common sentence: **Ei... nu știu.** /ej nu ʃtʲiu/. The word *Ei* starts with an open, relaxed mouth and glides smoothly. Then you stop speaking completely during the three dots. Your voice trails off into silence without snapping shut. After a brief rest, say *nu știu* with steady stress on the second word.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
Many learners rush across the dots without taking any pause at all. This makes the sentence sound anxious rather than thoughtful. Another common error is raising your pitch too high, making it sound like an unfinished question. Keep the tone level and quiet. Finally, do not close your vocal cords sharply with a hard catch. Let the silence feel natural and calm.
