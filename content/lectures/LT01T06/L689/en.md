## The dialogue dash
In Romanian stories and plays, a dialogue line starts with a dash. Writers use a long horizontal bar called a dialogue dash. They do not use quotation marks to open spoken lines. This dash signals that a character has started talking out loud. When you see this symbol, you know a real voice is speaking. It tells your brain to switch from quiet reading to conversational speaking mode immediately.

## Voice, stress, and pitch
When you see the dash, take a short breath before you speak. Start your voice with a fresh, lively conversational pitch. Look closely at the greeting **— Bună!** /bu.ˈnə/. The stress falls strongly on the second syllable. Your lips start rounded and closed for the first sound. Then, open your mouth slightly and keep your tongue relaxed in the center for the final sound.

## Saying the greeting
Practice speaking the full greeting aloud with energy. Treat the dash as a visual cue to sound friendly and open. Say **— Bună!** /bu.ˈnə/ with a warm, rising intonation. Push air smoothly from your chest as you hit the stressed syllable. Do not rush through the word. Keep your jaw relaxed so the sound stays natural and bright.

## Common reading mistakes
Many English speakers look for quotation marks and feel confused by the dash. Do not read the line in a flat narrative tone. Another mistake is forgetting the breath pause at the start of the line. Make sure you do not say a hard English "uh" sound at the end. Keep your tongue resting flat and middle in your mouth.
