## The Sound of U

The German letter **U** primarily represents two vowel sounds: the long tense `/uː/` and the short lax `/ʊ/`. Long `/uː/` is a deep, pure sound similar to the 'oo' in 'boot', but pronounced with noticeably more lip rounding and tension. Short `/ʊ/` is close to the vowel sound heard in English 'put'.

## How to Pronounce It

For long `/uː/`, purse your lips tightly into a small, forward-projecting circle while pulling the back of your tongue upward toward the soft palate. Keep your lips rigid and do not let the vowel glide into an English-style diphthong. For short `/ʊ/`, relax the tongue slightly and keep the lips rounded but much looser.

## Spelling Patterns

A single **u** followed by one consonant or an **h** (as in `Uhr`) is generally long, producing `/uː/`. When followed by a double consonant or consonant cluster (as in `Butter` or `und`), it is almost always pronounced short as `/ʊ/`. German words do not use double 'uu' except in rare loanwords.

## Comparing Long and Short U

Contrast the long vowel in `Mut` (`/muːt/`) with the short vowel in `Mutter` (`/ˈmʊtɐ/`). English speakers often make the mistake of not rounding their lips enough, or they allow long `/uː/` to glide into a 'w' sound at the end. Keep the vowel pure, steady, and fully rounded from start to finish.
