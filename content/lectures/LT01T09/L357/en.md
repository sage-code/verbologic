## The Sound of P

The letter **P** in German is pronounced as a voiceless bilabial plosive `/p/`. Unlike in French or Spanish, it is strongly aspirated when it begins a stressed syllable, sounding like an English *p* with a noticeable puff of air. Before vowels, make sure to release the burst crisply.

## How to Produce /p/

Bring both lips completely together to block the airflow coming from your lungs. Build up slight air pressure behind your lips without vibrating your vocal cords. Then, part your lips suddenly to release a sharp burst of air.

## Spelling and Occurrence

German **P** appears at the beginning, middle, and end of words, as well as in the doubled form **pp**, which signals a preceding short vowel as in *Kappe*. It also forms the common affricate digraph **pf** as in *Pferd*, where the lips transition immediately into the upper teeth touching the lower lip. In native vocabulary, single *p* is less frequent than *b*, but it is standard in loanwords and everyday terms.

## Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

Take care not to confuse voiceless `/p/` with voiced `/b/`, especially since German devoices final *b* to `[p]`, making *Dieb* end in the same sound as *Typ*. Learners from Romance language backgrounds must remember to aspirate the sound before stressed vowels, producing `[pʰ]` rather than an unaspirated stop. When encountering **pf**, avoid dropping the *p*; pronounce both parts tightly together.
