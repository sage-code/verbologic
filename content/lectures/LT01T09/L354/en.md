## The Sound of M

The German letter **M** represents the voiced bilabial nasal consonant `/m/`. It sounds virtually identical to the English 'm' in words like *mother* or *summer*. Unlike some other German consonants, it does not change its quality at the end of words.

## How to Pronounce M

Close both lips completely to block the airflow through your mouth. Lower your velum (soft palate) so the air resonates and escapes freely through your nose while your vocal cords vibrate. Release gently into the following vowel or consonant without adding an extra puff of air.

## Spelling and Patterns

This sound is written as a single **m** or a double **mm**. A doubled **mm**, as in *kommen* or *Sommer*, indicates that the preceding vowel is short and lax. As with all German nouns, words starting with this letter are capitalised when used as substantives, such as *die Mutter*.

## Things to Watch For

German **M** is very consistent and rarely poses difficulties for English speakers. Avoid making the doubled **mm** noticeably longer in normal speech; it merely signals the shortness of the preceding vowel rather than a prolonged consonant. Be careful not to drop the final nasal resonance when it appears in endings like *-em* (e.g., *mit dem*).
