## The Sound of G

The German letter **G** most commonly represents a voiced velar plosive `/ɡ/`, identical to the hard 'g' in English *garden*. At the end of a syllable or word, it undergoes terminal devoicing and sounds like a sharp `/k/`. In the standard suffix **-ig**, it uniquely takes on the soft palatal fricative sound `/ç/` as in *ich*.

## How to Pronounce It

Press the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate (velum) at the back of your roof mouth. Build up air pressure behind the closure, then release it crisply while engaging your vocal cords for `/ɡ/`. If it appears at the end of a word like *Tag*, release it without voice as `/k/`.

## Spelling and Patterns

**G** is written as uppercase **G** and lowercase **g**, appearing widely in native Germanic vocabulary. In standard German orthography, the suffix **-ig** is always written with a **g** despite sounding like `/ç/` before pauses or consonants (e.g., *wichtig*). Double **gg** indicates a preceding short vowel, as in *Flagge*.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Notice how **G** changes its sound based on position: compare voiced `/ɡ/` in *Garten* to voiceless `/k/` in *Tag* and fricative `/ç/` in *richtig*. English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing **-ig** with a hard `/k/` or soft `/dʒ/`, neither of which is standard in high German. Additionally, avoid pronouncing native German **g** as an English soft 'j' sound.
