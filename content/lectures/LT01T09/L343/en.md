## The Sound of B

In German, the letter **B** usually sounds just like the English voiced `b` as in *ball* when it appears at the start of a syllable. However, when it stands at the end of a syllable or word, it undergoes final devoicing and turns into an unvoiced `p`. This means that words like *ab* actually end with the crisp sound of `p` rather than a soft `b`.

## How to Pronounce It

Press your upper and lower lips firmly together to temporarily block the airflow. For the regular voiced sound `/b/`, vibrate your vocal cords as your lips part to release the air. If the letter is at the end of a word or syllable, do not vibrate your vocal cords, resulting in a clean, sharp `/p/` sound without puffing too much air.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **B** is written with standard Latin forms as uppercase **B** and lowercase **b**. It frequently appears initially (*Buch*), medially (*haben*), and doubled (*Ebbe*). A double **bb** marks the preceding vowel as short and is always pronounced as a single consonant sound.

## Sound Differences and Traps

Compare the voiced `/b/` in **Buch** with the devoiced `/p/` in **gelb** or **Urlaub**. A very common mistake for English speakers is continuing to pronounce a voiced `b` at the end of German words. Make sure to clearly devoice syllable-final **b** into `p`, so that *Dieb* sounds just like *deep* without aspiration.
