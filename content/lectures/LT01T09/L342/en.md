## The Sound of the Letter A

The letter **A** in German represents two distinct vowel lengths: short `/a/` and long `/aː/`. Unlike in English, German **A** never shifts toward a schwa or a diphthong like the vowel in 'cat' or 'cake'. It remains a pure, open central vowel very close to the 'a' in 'father', differing primarily in duration and tension.

## How to Pronounce A

Open your mouth moderately wide with your jaw relaxed and lowered. Rest the tip of your tongue gently behind your lower front teeth, keeping the body of the tongue flat in the floor of the mouth. For the long sound in `Abend`, sustain the tone with steady tension, while for the short sound in `Apfel`, cut the airflow quickly with a crisper release.

## Spelling Rules for A

A single **A** is pronounced short when followed by two or more consonants, as in **Apfel** or **Kamm**. It is pronounced long before a single consonant, at the end of an open syllable, when doubled (**Aal**), or when followed by a silent **h** (**Zahn**). Remember that all German nouns, including those beginning with **A**, must always be capitalized.

## Contrasting Sounds and Pitfalls

Contrast the long `/aː/` in **raten** with the short `/a/` in **Ratten**, where vowel length completely changes the word's meaning. A common trap for English speakers is reducing an unstressed **A** to an indistinct schwa sound at the end of words. Keep vowels like the final **a** in **Sofa** crisp and clearly articulated.
