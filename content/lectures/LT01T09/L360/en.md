## The Sound of S

In German, the letter **S** has three main sounds depending on its position: voiced `/z/`, voiceless `/s/`, and postalveolar `/ʃ/`. At the beginning of a word or before a vowel, it is typically voiced like the English *z* in *zoo*, as heard in `Sonne` (`/ˈzɔnə/`). At the end of a syllable it hardens to `/s/`, and when it stands before *p* or *t* at the start of a syllable, it sounds like English *sh*.

## How to Pronounce S

For the voiced `/z/` sound in `Sonne`, bring your teeth lightly together and place the tip of your tongue near the gum ridge behind your upper teeth. Push air out while vibrating your vocal cords to make a buzzing sound. Keep your lips relaxed rather than rounded.

## Spelling Rules

Initial **S** followed by a vowel is almost always pronounced `/z/` in standard German. When followed by **p** or **t** at the onset of a root syllable, it is written simply as **S** but pronounced `/ʃ/` (e.g., `Sport`, `Stein`). At the end of words or morphemes, it yields the voiceless `/s/`, contrasting with double **ss** and **ß** which denote voiceless `/s/` after vowels.

## Common Mistakes

English speakers often pronounce initial **S** as a voiceless `/s/` like in *sun*, which makes German words sound unvoiced or foreign. Remember that German `Sonne` starts with a buzz like an English *Z*. Be careful not to confuse this with word-final **S**, which devoices completely to `/s/` as in `Haus`.
