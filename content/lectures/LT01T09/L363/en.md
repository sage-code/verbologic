## The Sound of the Letter V

In German, the letter **V** usually sounds like a voiceless `/f/` in native Germanic words, just like the English 'f' in **father**. However, in loanwords derived from Latin, Romance languages, or modern English, it is pronounced as a voiced `/v/` identical to the English 'v' in **vase**.

## How to Pronounce V

Place your upper teeth gently against the inner edge of your lower lip and push air through the narrow gap. For native words like *Vater*, keep your vocal cords still to produce a crisp voiceless `/f/`. For loanwords like *Vase*, vibrate your vocal cords while keeping the same mouth position to make a voiced `/v/`.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **V** appears at the beginning of many fundamental German words like *viel*, *von*, and *vor*, where it invariably sounds like `/f/`. When found in international words such as *Verb*, *Visum*, or *Klavier*, it regularly carries the voiced `/v/` value. At the end of any syllable or word, German final devoicing forces it to sound like `/f/` regardless of origin.

## Comparing Pronunciations

A frequent mistake for English speakers is pronouncing every German **V** as a voiced `/v/`, turning *Vater* into something that sounds wrong to native ears. Contrast the native `/f/` in *vier* (`/fiːɐ̯/`) with the borrowed `/v/` in *Villa* (`/ˈvɪla/`). Always verify whether a word is native Germanic or borrowed to choose between `/f/` and `/v/`.
