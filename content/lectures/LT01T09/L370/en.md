## The Sound of Ü

The letter **Ü** represents a close front rounded vowel, which does not naturally exist in standard English. In its long form it sounds like `/yː/`, similar to the French **u** in *lune*, while its short counterpart is `/ʏ/` as in *fünf*. English speakers tend to substitute an English 'oo' sound, but **Ü** requires voicing with fronted tongue placement.

## How to Pronounce Ü

Position your tongue as if you are about to pronounce the long 'ee' sound `/iː/` as in *see*. Without moving your tongue or jaw, tightly round your lips into a small, tense 'o' shape. Blow voice through this shape to produce the pure `/yː/` sound without sliding into a diphthong.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **Ü** appears throughout native German words and can occur in lowercase (**ü**) or uppercase (**Ü**). If the letter appears in an open syllable or before a single consonant, it is usually long (`/yː/`), whereas before multiple consonants it is typically short (`/ʏ/`). When a German keyboard layout is unavailable, it is standard practice to transcribe it as **ue**.

## Short vs. Long Ü and Common Pitfalls

Notice the difference between the long `/yː/` in **fühlen** and the short `/ʏ/` in **füllen**. A frequent mistake is unrounding the lips, which erroneously turns **fühlen** into *fielen* (`/iː/`), or failing to front the tongue, making it sound like **buhlen** (`/uː/`). Keep your tongue high and forward while keeping your lips firmly pursed.
