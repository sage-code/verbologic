## The Sound of K

The German letter **K** represents a voiceless velar plosive, standardly transcribed as `/k/`. Before a stressed vowel, it is pronounced with a distinct burst of air (aspiration, `[kʰ]`), very much like the English *k* in *king*. When preceded by an *s* or in unaccented positions, this aspiration disappears.

## How to Pronounce K

Raise the back of your tongue until it forms a tight seal against your soft palate (velum). Allow breath pressure to build behind this closure, then release it crisply to create a sharp burst of air. Ensure your vocal cords remain completely still, as this is a voiceless consonant.

## Spelling Patterns

**K** appears frequently at the beginning, middle, and end of native and loan words like *Katze* and *Kino*. After short vowels, the sound `/k/` is almost always written with the digraph **ck** (as in *Bäcker* or *Ecke*). Additionally, the letter combination **kn-** is always fully pronounced in German, unlike in English.

## Tips and Common Pitfalls

English speakers must remember to pronounce the initial **k** in words like *Knie* (`/kniː/`) or *Knoten* (`/ˈknoːtn̩/`), as it is never silent in German. Learners from languages without aspiration (like Romance languages) should make sure to release a puff of air when **K** begins a stressed syllable. Be careful not to soften the sound into a fricative or voiced `/ɡ/`.
