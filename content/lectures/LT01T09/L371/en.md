## The Sound of ß

The letter **ß** (called *Eszett* or *scharfes S*) represents the voiceless alveolar fricative `[s]`. It sounds exactly like the 'ss' in English **hiss** or the 's' in **sun**, never vibrating like the 'z' in **buzz**. Unlike a single 's' between vowels in German, which is voiced `[z]`, **ß** is always crisp, sharp, and unvoiced.

## How to Pronounce ß

Bring your teeth lightly together and spread your lips slightly into a neutral position. Place the tip of your tongue close to the alveolar ridge just behind your upper teeth without touching it. Push air steadily through the narrow gap to produce a sharp, continuous hissing sound without engaging your vocal cords.

## Spelling Rules for ß

In modern German orthography, **ß** is written only after long vowels (such as in **Straße**) or diphthongs (such as in **heiß**). It never appears at the very beginning of a word and is replaced by **ss** in Switzerland and Liechtenstein. When writing in all capitals, it is traditionally converted to **SS**, though the capital letter **ẞ** is now officially recognised.

## Comparing ß with Similar Sounds

A frequent error among learners is confusing **ß** with double **ss** or voiced **s**. Notice the vowel length: the preceding vowel before **ß** is always long, as in **Maße** (`/ˈmaːsə/`, measurements), whereas the vowel before **ss** is always short, as in **Masse** (`/ˈmasə/`, mass). Never voice **ß** as `[z]`, even when it stands between two vowels.
