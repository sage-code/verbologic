## The Sound of L

In Standard German, the letter **L** represents the clear alveolar lateral approximant `/l/`. Unlike the 'dark L' common at the end of English syllables, German maintains a **clear, crisp L** in all positions. It sounds bright and fronted, whether at the start of a word or at the very end.

## How to Pronounce It

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the **alveolar ridge**, right behind your upper front teeth. Let the air flow smoothly around the sides of your tongue while keeping the back of your tongue flat and relaxed. Avoid pulling the back of your tongue up toward the soft palate, which creates an unwanted dark English quality.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **L** can appear as a single consonant or doubled as **ll** after short vowels, as seen in `Ball` or `hell`. Doubling does not change the core phonetic quality of the sound, but it serves as an indicator that the preceding vowel is short. It appears freely at the beginning, middle, and end of words.

## Avoiding Mistakes

English speakers must avoid using the dark velarized `[ɫ]` at the end of syllables in words like `voll` or `Apfel`. Keep the tongue arched forward so that every German **L** sounds light and European, similar to the initial sound in 'leaf'. Falling back on a hollow, English-style syllable-final L is one of the most recognizable foreign accent markers.
