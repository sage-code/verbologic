## The Sound of H

At the start of a word or syllable, German **H** is pronounced as a voiceless breath sound `/h/`, just like in the English word **house**. However, when it appears after a vowel (known as *Dehnungs-h*), it is completely silent and signals that the preceding vowel is long. It is never pronounced with friction or raspy throat clearing like the German *ch*.

## How to Pronounce H

Keep your mouth relaxed and open in the position of the vowel that follows. Exhale gently from your throat without engaging your vocal cords, producing a soft sigh. Avoid tightening the back of your tongue, ensuring no rasping or scraping sound is made.

## Spelling and Patterns

Capital **H** and lowercase **h** regularly appear at the beginning of words and roots (e.g., **Hand**, **gehören**). Inside words after a vowel, as in **Zahn** or **Stuhl**, it functions purely as an orthographic length marker. It also forms crucial digraphs and trigraphs like **ch**, **sch**, and **ph**.

## Comparing H Sounds and Pitfalls

Contrast the pronounced `/h/` in **Hut** (`/huːt/`) with the silent letter in **Schuh** (`/ʃuː/`) or **gehen** (`/ˈɡeːən/`). English speakers often try to pronounce the internal or final **h**, producing an awkward pause or aspirate where the vowel should simply flow smoothly. Conversely, French and Romance speakers must take care not to drop the initial `/h/` altogether.
