## The Sound of Z

In German, the letter **Z** is pronounced as a voiceless alveolar affricate `/ts/`, exactly like the *ts* in English *cats* or the *zz* in *pizza*. Unlike in English, it is **never voiced** like a buzzing `/z/` in native German vocabulary. Whenever you see a **Z**, always treat it as a crisp blend of `/t/` and `/s/`.

## How to Pronounce /ts/

Start by pressing the tip of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth, exactly as if preparing to say a `/t/`. Build up a small amount of air pressure, then release the tongue slightly so the air hisses out as an `/s/`. Make sure your vocal cords do not vibrate; the entire sound should remain crisp and unvoiced.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **Z** appears at the beginning, middle, or end of words, such as in **Zug** (train), **Katze** (cat), and **Holz** (wood). After short vowels, the sound is conventionally spelled as **tz** rather than a double *zz*, except in foreign loanwords like **Pizza**. The uppercase **Z** is required for nouns, while lowercase **z** appears elsewhere.

## Variants and Pitfalls

The standard pronunciation is strictly `/ts/`, seen in words like **Zahn** (`/tsaːn/`) and **Zeit** (`/tsaɪ̯t/`). English speakers frequently slip up by pronouncing German **Z** as a buzzing `/z/`, which sounds foreign and can cause misunderstandings. The only rare exception where **Z** sounds like English `/z/` is in recent English loanwords such as **Zoom** (`/zuːm/`).
