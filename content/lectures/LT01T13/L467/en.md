## The Sound of J

The letter **J** in standard French consistently represents the voiced postalveolar fricative `/ʒ/`. It sounds exactly like the *s* in the English word *measure* or *vision*, but unlike in English, it is never pronounced with an initial *d* sound in native words. It provides a smooth, continuous buzzing sound without any explosive release.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce this sound, round your lips slightly and push them forward into a gentle pout. Arch the blade of your tongue close to the roof of your mouth, just behind your upper teeth ridge, leaving a narrow channel for airflow. Vibrate your vocal cords as you force air continuously through this gap without letting your tongue tap the palate.

## Spelling and Patterns

In French, the letter **J** is always pronounced `/ʒ/` regardless of the vowel that follows it (e.g., *jardin*, *je*, *jour*). This contrasts with the letter **G**, which requires an *e* or *i* to sound soft. Note that **J** is rarely found at the end of French words, appearing almost exclusively at the start or in the middle of syllables.

## Common Pitfalls

The most frequent mistake English speakers make is adding an affricate *d* burst, turning *jour* into something sounding like *djour*. Remember that native French **J** is purely fricative and never starts with a stop. Only a handful of recent English borrowings such as *jeans* or *jogging* retain the affricate `/dʒ/` sound.
