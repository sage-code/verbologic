## Sound of the Letter

In modern French, the letter **H** is universally silent and produces no sound of its own. Unlike the English aspirated [h] in *hat*, French never breathes out this letter, leaving it completely mute. Instead, you simply pronounce the vowel that immediately follows it.

## How to Say It

Do not expel any air from the back of your throat or vocal tract. Relax your vocal cords completely and move straight to the position of the following vowel. Think of the letter **H** merely as an empty placeholder on the page rather than a physical sound.

## Spelling and Grammar Rules

French distinguishes between two types of mute H: the **H muet** and the **H aspiré**. A word starting with an *H muet* allows elision and liaison (e.g., *l'homme*, *les_hommes* [le.z‿ɔm]), whereas an *H aspiré* blocks both (e.g., *le héros*, *les héros* [le e.ʁo]). The letter also combines with consonants, notably forming the sound `[ʃ]` in the digraph **CH**.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The single most common pitfall for English speakers is attempting to exhale an aspirated [h] sound in French words like *hôtel* or *histoire*. Another frequent error is making a liaison before an *H aspiré*, such as incorrectly saying *les_héros* with a [z] sound, which can inadvertently sound like *les zéros* (the zeroes).
