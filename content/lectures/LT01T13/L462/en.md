## The Sound of E

The letter **E** is the most versatile vowel in French, primarily representing the neutral 'caduc' sound `/ə/` as in *le*, similar to the English schwa in *sofa* but with slightly rounded lips. Depending on accents and surrounding consonants, it also commonly produces the closed `/e/` (like *école*) or open `/ɛ/` (like *mer*). In many word-final positions, it becomes completely silent.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce the default French `/ə/`, position your tongue mid-central in your mouth, exactly as you would for the 'a' in *about*. Next, round your lips gently into an oval shape while maintaining that vocal position. Ensure the vowel remains steady and does not glide into an English-style diphthong.

## Spelling Patterns

Without accents, **E** sounds like `/ə/` in small grammatical words (*le*, *de*) or closed open syllables, but shifts to `/ɛ/` before doubled consonants (*terre*) or consonants in the same syllable (*bec*). Adding diacritics specifies its quality: **é** always yields `/e/`, while **è** and **ê** yield `/ɛ/`. At the ends of multisyllabic words, an unaccented **e** is almost always silent (*porte*, *vie*).

## Watch Out for Traps

English speakers often default to pronouncing written final **e** as an 'ee' sound `/i/` or reducing all unaccented instances to an English weak schwa. In French, a final unaccented **e** must remain completely silent, so *table* ends crisply on `/l/`. Do not confuse `/ə/` (rounded lips) with `/e/` (lips unrounded and spread as in *café*).
