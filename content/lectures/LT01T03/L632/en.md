## A very common beat pattern

Words are built from small beats called syllables. When you clap along with a Romanian word, one beat usually sounds stronger than the others. In Romanian, that strong beat very often lands on the second-last syllable. Linguists call this the penultimate syllable. Counting backwards from the end of a word helps you find it easily. You find the last beat, then take one step back.

## How your mouth and voice work

To make a stressed syllable, your voice must do three things at once. First, push a little extra air out from your lungs. Second, make your vocal cords vibrate with a slightly higher musical tone. Third, hold the vowel sound a tiny bit longer while opening your mouth clearly. The weak syllables around it stay softer, shorter, and lower. Do not drop the final vowel completely.

## Practicing with copilul

Listen closely to the word **copilul** /koˈpi.lul/, which means the child. It has three beats: co - pi - lul. The middle beat, pi, is the second-last one. When you say **copilul** /koˈpi.lul/, open your lips gently for co, push your voice up strongly on pi, and then drop down softly on lul. English speakers often rush the end, but you must keep the final beat clear and calm.
