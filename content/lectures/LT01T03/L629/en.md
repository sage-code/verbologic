## What is a closed syllable?
A syllable is like a little beat in a word. When a syllable ends with a vowel, the sound stays wide open. But when a syllable ends with a consonant letter, like n or t, we call it a closed syllable. In Romanian, closed syllables are everywhere. The consonant shuts the sound like a little door at the very end of the beat.

## How your mouth works
To say a closed syllable, first make the vowel sound with an open mouth. Then, quickly bring your tongue, teeth, or lips together to shut the air. Do not drop the final sound! Keep your voice steady. If this beat has the stress, say it slightly louder and hold the sound a moment longer so the consonant rings out crisply.

## Listen to the example
Look at the Romanian word **pământ** /pəˈmɨnt/, which means earth. This word has two beats: pă-mânt. The second beat ends with the consonants n and t. It is a closed syllable. The stress falls right on this second part. Your mouth opens for the vowel, then your tongue touches your upper teeth firmly to make a clear, sharp ending.

## Mistakes to avoid
English speakers often swallow sounds at the end of words or make a quiet pause in the throat instead of a real sound. In Romanian, you must never swallow the ending. Tap your tongue against your teeth and make sure the consonant sound pops out cleanly before you pause or say another word.
