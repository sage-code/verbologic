## The special beat pattern
In Romanian, action words can surprise your ears. Often, the strongest beat jumps all the way to the end of the word. Instead of stressing the beginning, your voice hits the last part. This happens very often when we speak to a group of friends or talk about doing things together. Listening for this final beat helps you know who is doing the action.

## How your mouth and voice work
Start smoothly and do not shout the first part. Make your voice climb higher, grow louder, and hold the sound a tiny bit longer on the final syllable. Your mouth opens clearly for the vowel sound at the end. For example, in **vorbiți** /vorˈbit͡s/, the voice taps *vor* softly, then lands firmly on *biți*. The sound at the end is short, crisp, and sharp like a little snake hiss.

## Listen to the example
Let us practice with the word **vorbiți** /vorˈbit͡s/, which means "you speak". Notice how the voice rests strongly on the second beat. If you tap your foot or clap your hands, your hand claps hard on the ending: vor-BIȚI! Other action words do this too, like *citiți* or *dormiți*. The ending always rings out loud and bright.

## Mistakes to avoid
English speakers often like to stress the start of a word. If you say *VOR-biți*, it sounds confusing to Romanian ears. Do not rush through the ending or make it weak. Instead, keep the first syllable light and let your voice save its biggest energy for the final syllable. Keep your tongue relaxed until the quick, neat finish!
