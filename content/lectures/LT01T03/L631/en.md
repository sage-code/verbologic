## The last syllable rule
In Romanian, many words are stressed right at the end. A syllable is a beat of a word. When a word has two beats, the second beat is often the strongest. In the word **ajuns** /aˈʒuns/, which means arrived, the second part sounds stronger than the first part. It does not sound like *A-juns*. Instead, it sounds like *a-JUNS*. You can hear this pattern in many action words and describing words in Romanian.

## How to make the sound
Start with a quiet, relaxed sound for the first syllable. Keep your voice low and calm. Then, make your mouth open slightly wider for the last syllable. Push a little more air out from your chest. Make the vowel sound longer, louder, and slightly higher in tone. For **ajuns** /aˈʒuns/, say "a" quickly and softly. Then push your lips forward for "juns" with more power.

## Listen and practice
Try tapping your foot or clapping your hands. Clap softly on the first beat and clap loudly on the last beat. Say **ajuns** /aˈʒuns/ along with your claps: soft *a*, loud *JUNS*. Listen to the Romanian audio closely. Notice how the voice climbs a little bit on the last syllable before falling down gently. Practicing with a rhythm helps your mouth remember where the big push of air belongs.

## Mistakes to avoid
English speakers often like to stress the first syllable of two-syllable words. If you say *A-juns*, it sounds unusual to Romanian ears. Do not rush through the end of the word. Give the final syllable its full time and energy. Keep the first vowel short and smooth, but never drop the strong beat at the very end.
