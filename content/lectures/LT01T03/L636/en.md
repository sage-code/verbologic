## Two Vowels, Two Beats
Sometimes two vowels stand next to each other, but they do not merge into one sound. Instead, they live in separate syllables. In grammar, we call this a hiatus. When this happens, you must not slide them together. Each vowel gets its own turn to shine. You say the first vowel, and then you say the second vowel clearly. Think of it like taking two small steps instead of one big jump.

## How Your Mouth and Voice Work
To say a hiatus, watch your mouth closely. Say the first vowel, then change your mouth shape for the second vowel. Do not rush! Your voice makes two separate little pulses of air. Your pitch might rise or fall gently between them. One of the syllables might be stronger, but both vowels must stay pure and clear. Your lips and tongue move from one shape to the next without blending the sounds into a single glide.

## Listen to the Word România
A wonderful example is the name of the country: **România** /ro.mɨˈni.a/. Clap your hands as you say it: ro-mâ-ni-a! Notice the very end. The letter "i" and the letter "a" are side by side. But you do not say "nya". You say "ni", and then you say "a". The stress sits on the syllable "ni", and the last syllable "a" floats softly right after it.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
English speakers often like to squish two vowels together into a glide. For instance, you might want to turn "i-a" into "ya" like in "onion". In Romanian, that changes the rhythm of the word. Keep the two sounds distinct. Give each vowel its own small push of breath. If you speak too fast, slow down and tap your finger for every single syllable!
