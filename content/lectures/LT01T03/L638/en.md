## The sound
Stress means you say one part louder and longer. In many words, this strong part stays in the same place. Listen to (copil) /koˈpil/, which means child. Now listen to (copilul) /koˈpi.lul/, which means the child. The strong beat stays on the main part. The ending changes, but the beat does not move.

## How to say it
Open your mouth and push more air on the strong part. Say (copil) /koˈpil/ with more force on the second part. When you add an ending, keep that same force. Say (copilul) /koˈpi.lul/ smoothly. Do not push extra air onto the new ending. Keep your pitch steady on the added part.

## Reading rules
Look at the base of the word first. Find where the main stress lands. When you see a longer form of a noun, do not move the beat. Keep the stress right where it was before. The added letters at the end stay light and short. This rule helps you read new forms clearly.

## Common mistakes
Do not move the stress to the final letters. New learners often push hard on the new ending. If you say the ending too loud, listeners get confused. Always keep the main push on the root. Let the ending sound soft and quick. Relax your throat as you finish the word.
