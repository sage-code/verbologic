## The beat stays in place

Words are made of small pieces called syllables. One syllable always gets the big punch. We call this the stressed beat. In Romanian nouns, this heavy beat loves its home. When you change a noun from one to many, the word grows longer. You might also glue tiny tags to the end, like the word for "the". Even when the word grows, the loud beat stays right on the main root syllable. It does not slide forward to the new ending.

## How to use your voice

When you say the stressed syllable, push more air from your belly. Your voice should sound a little louder, longer, and slightly higher in pitch. Your mouth opens wider and holds the sound with energy. When you attach new endings, do not punch them. Keep your tongue relaxed and let your voice drop lower and softer on those extra parts. Think of the root syllable as a strong drumbeat, while the added endings are tiny, quiet taps that follow softly.

## Practice with the word child

Let us practice with the Romanian word for child: **copil** /koˈpil/. In **copil** /koˈpil/, the strong punch lands on the second beat, "pil". Now let us add the tag "ul" to say "the child": **copilul**. Notice where your voice pushes. It still punches "pil"! You say co-PIL-ul, never co-pil-UL. When you talk about many children, you say **copii**. The beat still stays on that same root vowel. The root holds the power every time.

## Tips for English speakers

English words often like to jump their beat when words get longer. Think of how "photograph" shifts into "photography". Romanian nouns do not jump around like that. English speakers often want to push the very last sound of a word when adding little endings. Do not push the tail of the word! Keep the ending syllables quiet and short. Keep your mouth gentle at the end, and always remember to put the main punch right back on the familiar root syllable.
