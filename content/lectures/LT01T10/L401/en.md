## Sound Overview

The letter **Ь** (мягкий знак) has no independent phonetic sound of its own; instead, it modifies adjacent sounds. Most commonly, it acts as a palatalisation marker, softening the preceding consonant so that it carries a `ʲ` quality, similar to how the 'c' sounds in English 'cute'. When placed before iotated vowels like **е**, **ё**, **ю**, or **я**, it signals an audible `[j]` glide separator between the softened consonant and the vowel.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce the softness indicated by **Ь**, arch the middle of your tongue up toward the hard palate while articulating the consonant itself. Make sure your lips remain neutral and do not add a separate 'ee' vowel sound afterward. When **Ь** functions as a separating sign, articulate the softened consonant, briefly pause airflow, and smoothly glide into the following vowel with a distinct `[j]` onset.

## Spelling and Usage Rules

Because **Ь** modifies consonants, it never appears at the very beginning of a native Russian word and is almost never capitalised except in all-caps text. It frequently marks the grammatical ending of feminine nouns ending in sibilants (like **ночь** or **рожь**) and infinitive verbs (like **читать**). Remember that letters like **ч** and **щ** are inherently soft, while **ж**, **ш**, and **ц** are inherently hard, meaning **Ь** after them serves purely grammatical functions rather than phonetic ones.

## Phonetic Variants and Pitfalls

Notice the stark contrast between words with and without palatalisation, such as **мат** (`/mat/`, foul language) versus **мать** (`/matʲ/`, mother). The separating variant appears in words like **семья** (`/sʲɪmʲˈja/`, family), distinguishing it from simple softened clusters. A frequent mistake for English speakers is inserting a full vowel `/i/` after the consonant instead of simultaneously raising the tongue body during consonant release.
