## The Sound of М

The letter **М** represents a bilabial nasal consonant, practically identical to the English 'm' in **mother**. Russian features two varieties: a hard `[m]` and a soft, palatalized `[mʲ]`. The hard variant is standard, while the soft counterpart sounds like an 'm' blended smoothly with a faint 'y' sound.

## How to Produce the Sound

Press your upper and lower lips firmly together while lowering your soft palate so air escapes through your nose. For the hard `[m]`, keep your tongue resting neutrally in the bottom of your mouth. For the soft `[mʲ]`, raise the middle of your tongue toward the hard palate as your lips part, creating a softened, brighter acoustic quality.

## Spelling and Alphabet Role

**М** is the 14th letter of the Russian alphabet and looks identical to the Latin uppercase 'M'. It functions regularly across all positions in a word, appearing initially, medially, and finally. Its softness is determined entirely by the letter that follows: soft-indicating vowels (**е**, **ё**, **и**, **ю**, **я**) or **ь** turn it into `[mʲ]`.

## Hard vs. Soft and Common Pitfalls

Contrast the hard sound in **мать** (`/matʲ/`) with the soft sound in **мять** (`/mʲatʲ/`). English speakers often struggle with word-final soft consonants, pronouncing words like **семь** as a hard 'sem' rather than the soft `[sʲemʲ]`. Take care not to add a full separate 'y' consonant (`/j/`); the palatalization should occur simultaneously within the consonant itself.
