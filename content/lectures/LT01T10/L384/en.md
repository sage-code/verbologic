## The Russian L sound

The letter **Л** represents two distinct lateral sounds: a 'hard' velarized lateral approximant `[ɫ]` and a 'soft' palatalized one `[lʲ]`. The hard sound is noticeably darker and heavier than the standard English 'l' in 'leaf', resembling the 'dark L' in 'ball' but articulated even further back. The soft variant sounds delicate, similar to the 'l' in 'million'.

## How to articulate it

For the hard `[ɫ]`, press the tip of your tongue against your upper teeth or alveolar ridge while noticeably pulling the back of your tongue up toward the soft palate (velum). Keep your lips neutral or slightly spread without rounding them. For the soft `[lʲ]`, press the flat blade of your tongue against the roof of your mouth behind your upper teeth while letting the tip point slightly downwards.

## Spelling rules and behaviour

The letter **Л** appears freely at the start, middle, or end of Russian words. Whether it is pronounced hard or soft depends on the following letter: hard vowels (**а**, **о**, **у**, **ы**, **э**) make it hard `[ɫ]`, while iotated vowels (**е**, **ё**, **и**, **ю**, **я**) and the soft sign (**ь**) soften it to `[lʲ]`. At the end of words or before hard consonants, it retains its deep, velarized quality unless marked by **ь**.

## Comparing hard and soft variants

Contrast the hard sound in **лук** (`[ɫuk]`, onion) with the soft sound in **люк** (`[lʲuk]`, hatch). A frequent beginner error is rounding the lips on the hard variant, turning `[ɫ]` into an English 'w' sound like 'wuk'. Keep the tongue active and the lips flat to avoid sounding unnatural.
