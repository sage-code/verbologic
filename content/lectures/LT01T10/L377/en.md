## The Sound of Russian Е

The letter **Е** represents two main sounds depending on its surroundings: a glide-vowel combo `[je]` at the beginning of words and after vowels, or a simple `[e]` directly following a consonant. When placed after a consonant, it does not add an English 'y' sound; rather, it palatalises (softens) that consonant before producing an 'e' similar to the vowel in 'bed'. In unstressed positions, it weakens noticeably, turning into a short `[ɪ]` like the 'i' in 'sit'.

## How to Pronounce Е

To pronounce the stressed initial or post-vocalic variant, start by raising the middle of your tongue toward the hard palate to make a crisp 'y' sound, then quickly open into an open-mid front vowel like the 'e' in 'pet'. When **Е** follows a consonant, do not insert an audible glide; instead, arch the middle of your tongue to soften the preceding consonant and immediately release into `[e]`. Keep your lips relaxed and spread slightly without rounding.

## Spelling and Functions of Е

The letter **Е** is one of the five soft vowels in the Cyrillic alphabet and is written almost identically to the Latin 'E'. Its presence systematically indicates that the preceding consonant must be pronounced soft (palatalised). In everyday writing, the two dots of the letter **Ё** (`/jo/`) are very frequently omitted in Russian print, which means you will often see **Е** standing in for **Ё** in signs, books, and menus.

## Nuances and Common Traps

The most frequent error for beginners is pronouncing an overt 'y' sound after consonants, saying 'm-yes-to' instead of the correct soft blend `[mʲes.tə]` for **место**. Another crucial challenge is vowel reduction: unlike in English or Spanish, unstressed **Е** in words like **река** is not pronounced as an 'e', but rather reduces sharply to `[rʲɪˈka]`. Always pay close attention to stress marks, because vowel quality hinges entirely on syllable stress.
