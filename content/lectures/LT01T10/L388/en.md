## The Sound of П

The letter **П** represents a voiceless bilabial plosive, identical to the standard English `p` sound found in words like **spin**. Unlike word-initial English plosives, Russian `/p/` is completely unaspirated, meaning there is no burst of air released when pronouncing it. Before softening vowels, it takes on a palatalized sound `/pʲ/`, produced with a simultaneous 'y' quality.

## How to Pronounce It

Firmly press your upper and lower lips together to completely block the airflow from your lungs. Release your lips abruptly without exhaling an extra puff of breath, keeping the vocal cords completely silent. To form the soft variant `/pʲ/`, flatten and raise the middle blade of your tongue against your hard palate as your lips part.

## Spelling Rules and Behavior

The printed letter **П** looks like a squared arch or Greek pi (π), but cursive lower-case **п** looks very similar to an English handwritten 'n'. It can appear anywhere in a word and undergoes voicing assimilation to become a voiced `/b/` before voiced consonants like **б**, **г**, or **д**. In turn, the letter **б** deafens to a **п** sound at the ends of words.

## Nuances and Common Traps

English speakers often inadvertently aspirate initial `/p/`, making words like **папа** sound foreign or unnatural; practise holding a hand before your mouth to ensure zero airflow escaping. Additionally, ensure you clearly distinguish between hard `/p/` in **полка** and soft `/pʲ/` in **печка**. Neglecting the palatalization before vowels like **е** or **и** is one of the most frequent learner mistakes.
