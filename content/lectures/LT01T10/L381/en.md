## The Sound of Russian И

The letter **И** primarily represents the close front unrounded vowel `/i/`, very similar to the 'ee' in English *meet*. Unlike English vowels, Russian `/i/` is tense and never turns into a diphthong. Importantly, this vowel automatically softens (palatalizes) almost any consonant that precedes it.

## How to Produce the Sound

Spread your lips wide into a gentle smile and raise the front of your tongue high toward the hard palate. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and your tongue steady without gliding toward another sound. When it follows a consonant, raise the middle body of your tongue simultaneously to create the characteristic Russian soft consonant sound.

## Spelling Rules and Contexts

The capital **И** looks like a reversed Latin 'N', while lowercase **и** is written identically in print and cursive forms. It can appear in any position: word-initially, medially, and as an inflectional ending. Following the consonants **ж**, **ш**, and **ц**, standard spelling requires writing **и**, but it is pronounced as the retracted central vowel `/ɨ/` (like **ы**).

## Sound Variations and Common Pitfalls

Under stress, it sounds fully articulated as `/i/` (as in **мир**), but in unstressed syllables, it reduces slightly to a laxer `/ɪ/` (as in **игра**). A frequent mistake for English speakers is pronouncing it with a lingering 'y' glide or failing to soften the preceding consonant. Be careful not to confuse **и** with **ы**, which is a retracted, central vowel produced much further back in the mouth.
