## The Sound of Б

The Russian letter **Б** represents a voiced bilabial plosive, identical to the standard English `b` in 'bed'. Unlike English, however, it comes in two distinct forms: 'hard' `/b/` and 'soft' (palatalized) `/bʲ/`. Additionally, Russian regularly devoices voiced consonants at the ends of words, meaning **Б** often sounds just like a crisp English `p`.

## How to Produce the Sound

To pronounce the basic hard sound `/b/`, bring both lips completely together to block the airflow, build up slight pressure, and release it while vibrating your vocal cords. For the soft version `/bʲ/`, arch the middle of your tongue toward the hard palate at the exact moment your lips part, creating an acoustic blend of `b` and a faint `y`-sound. When producing the devoiced sound `/p/` at the end of words, keep your lips firm and avoid adding a strong puff of air (aspiration).

## Spelling and Alphabet Position

**Б** is the second letter of the Russian Cyrillic alphabet, immediately following **А**. Its printed uppercase form looks like an angular number '6' or a Greek beta with a top bar, while the lowercase **б** features an ascending loop to the right. Take care in handwriting not to confuse **Б** with **В** (which represents the `/v/` sound).

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Listen to the contrast between hard `/b/` in **брат** (`/brat/`) and palatalized `/bʲ/` in **белый** (`/ˈbʲelɨj/`). A major trap for learners is forgetting final devoicing: a word like **клуб** is pronounced `/klup/`, never with a voiced `/b/`. Failing to soften the letter before vowels like **е** or **и** is another frequent mistake that will make your speech sound distinctly non-native.
