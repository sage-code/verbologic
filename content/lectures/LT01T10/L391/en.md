## The Sound of the Letter

The letter **Т** represents a dental plosive consonant, pronounced either hard as `/t/` or soft (palatalised) as `/tʲ/`. Unlike the English 't', the hard Russian `/t/` is strictly **dental** and produced with no puff of air (unaspirated). The soft variant `/tʲ/` sounds noticeably sharper, somewhat akin to the 't' in British 'tune' or a light 'ts'-like release.

## How to Produce It

For the hard sound `/t/`, place the flat blade and tip of your tongue firmly against the **back of your upper front teeth**, not on the gum ridge behind them. Build up air pressure and release it abruptly without blowing out a breath. For the soft `/tʲ/`, press the middle of your tongue against your hard palate while touching the teeth, creating a crisp, softened contact.

## Spelling Rules and Behaviour

Capital **Т** and lowercase **т** look identical to the Latin letter 'T', but handwritten or italic forms typically resemble an italic *m*. It represents `/t/` before hard vowels (а, о, у, ы, э) and `/tʲ/` before soft indicators (е, ё, и, ю, я, ь). At the end of words or before voiceless consonants it remains voiceless, but before voiced consonants like *д* or *б*, it assimilates to `/d/`.

## Nuances and Common Traps

English speakers often make two major errors: aspirating the sound (adding an 'h' puff) and placing the tongue too far back on the alveolar ridge. Compare hard **стол** (`/stol/`) with soft **тень** (`/tʲenʲ/`). Be careful never to articulate hard **Т** like an English alveolar stop, and avoid turning soft `/tʲ/` fully into the English 'ch' sound.
