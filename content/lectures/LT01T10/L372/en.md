## The Sound of Russian А

The letter **А** represents an open central vowel `[a]` when stressed, very similar to the 'a' sound in English *father*. However, in unstressed syllables, Russian undergoes vowel reduction, causing **А** to sound like `[ɐ]` (a shorter 'uh' sound as in *cup*) or `[ə]` (the schwa in *sofa*). It never diphthongises into the flat vowel found in words like *cat* or *face*.

## How to Produce the Sound

To pronounce the stressed sound `[a]`, open your mouth comfortably wide and keep your tongue low and flat on the floor of your mouth. Your lips should remain relaxed and completely unrounded without protruding forward. For unstressed variants, simply keep the mouth slightly less open and relax your vocal tract into a neutral, effortless position.

## Reading and Writing Rules

Uppercase **А** and lowercase **а** look identical to their Latin counterparts, making them immediately recognisable. In standard Russian spelling, **А** indicates that the preceding consonant is 'hard' (non-palatalised), contrasting with **Я**, which indicates palatalisation. Note that after the spelling rule consonants **Ж**, **Ш**, **Ч**, **Щ**, the letter **А** is written instead of **Я**.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing unstressed **А** as a full, clear `[a]`, which sounds unnatural and foreign. Compare stressed **мама** (`/ˈma.mə/`), where the first syllable is an open `[a]` and the second reduces to `[ə]`, with **арбуз** (`/ɐrˈbus/`), where the initial unstressed vowel is `[ɐ]`. Pay close attention to word stress so you can reduce the unstressed vowels correctly.
