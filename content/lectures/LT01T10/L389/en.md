## The Sound of Russian Р

The letter **Р** represents an alveolar trill or tap, similar to the rolled *r* found in Spanish or Italian. Unlike the English postalveolar approximant `/ɹ/`, Russian produces a crisp vibration using the tip of the tongue. It has two main forms: the hard, vibrant `[r]` and the palatalized, softer `[rʲ]`.

## How to Produce Р

Raise the tip of your tongue against the tooth ridge right behind your upper front teeth. Keep the muscles relaxed enough that a brief stream of voiced outgoing air makes the tip vibrate or tap once or twice. For the soft `[rʲ]`, arch the middle body of your tongue upward toward the hard palate simultaneously.

## Spelling and Alphabet Role

Russian uppercase **Р** looks identical to the Latin letter **P**, which is a frequent false friend for beginners. It corresponds to the sound `/r/` and pairs with hard vowels (*а, о, у, ы, э*) as a hard consonant, or soft vowels (*я, ё, ю, и, е*) and the soft sign (**ь**) to signal palatalization. At the end of a word, it retains its trilled voicing and does not devoice into another category.

## Contrasting Hard and Soft Р

Contrast the hard sound in **рука** (`/rʊˈka/`) with the soft palatalized sound in **рис** (`/rʲis/`). English speakers frequently substitute their native gliding approximant sound, which immediately gives away a foreign accent. Make sure your tongue actually contacts the alveolar ridge rather than curling back passively in the mouth.
