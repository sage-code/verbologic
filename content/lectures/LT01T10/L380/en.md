## The Sound of З

The Russian letter **З** corresponds to the voiced alveolar fricative `/z/`, sounding just like the 'z' in English *zoo*. Before soft vowels such as **е**, **и**, or the soft sign **ь**, it softens into `/zʲ/`, a palatalised sound with no direct equivalent in English. At the very end of words or right before voiceless consonants, it loses its voicing completely and sounds like `/s/` or `/sʲ/`.

## How to Pronounce З

To produce the standard hard `/z/`, place the tip of your tongue near your upper tooth ridge, leaving a small slit for airflow while vibrating your vocal cords. For the soft `/zʲ/`, arch the middle of your tongue high toward the hard palate while maintaining the gentle buzzing sound. Keep your lips relaxed and slightly spread, avoiding any puff of air.

## Reading and Writing З

The letter **З** closely resembles the Arabic numeral **3**, which makes it easy to recognise but sometimes confusing for beginners. It occurs in lowercase and capital forms across all word positions, including word-initially as in **зима** (winter). Pay close attention to final positions, where written **з** is devoiced and must be read as a clear `/s/`.

## Sound Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

Notice the difference between the hard `/z/` in **зуб** and the soft `/zʲ/` in **зима**. English speakers frequently forget to palatalise **з** before vowels like **и**, or fail to devoice it into `/s/` at word endings like **глаз** (pronounced `/ɡlas/`). Practise contrasting voiced **з** with its voiceless counterpart **с** to keep your consonants crisp.
