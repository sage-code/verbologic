## The Sound of Ч

The letter **Ч** represents a soft, voiceless alveolo-palatal affricate, transcribed as `[tɕ]`. It is very similar to the English *ch* in *cheese*, but pronounced significantly softer, with the middle of the tongue arched high against the hard palate. Unlike English *ch*, which is slightly retroflex and pronounced with protruding lips, Russian **Ч** is inherently soft (palatalized) in virtually all standard contexts.

## How to Pronounce Ч

Place the blade and body of your tongue broadly against your roof behind the upper teeth and the hard palate, exactly as you would for a very soft *ty*. Begin by blocking the airflow completely and then release it slightly into a narrow channel to create a crisp, hushed hiss. Keep your tongue arched flat and high toward the palate rather than letting the tip curl upward.

## Spelling Rules and Characteristics

The consonant **Ч** is permanently soft in Russian, meaning it has no hard counterpart. Because of Russian historical spelling rules, the vowels **я** and **ю** are almost never written after **Ч**; instead, **а** and **у** are written (as in *час* and *чудо*), yet pronounced fully soft. It can appear in any position within a word: word-initially, word-medially, and word-finally.

## Nuances and Common Pitfalls

Foreign learners frequently make the mistake of pronouncing **Ч** like the harder English *ch* in *church*, which sounds unpalatalized and coarse to native speakers. Keep it crisp, delicate, and soft in words like *час* `[tɕas]` and *чай* `[tɕaj]`. Be aware of common irregular words such as *что* `[ʂto]` and *скучно* `[ˈskuɕːnə]`, where historical assimilation shifts the sound to `[ʂ]` or `[ɕː]`.
