## The Sound of Й

The letter **Й** represents the voiced palatal approximant `[j]`. It sounds essentially identical to the English **y** in *yes* or *boy*. Unlike the full Russian vowel **И**, **Й** is a consonant glide that cannot form a syllable on its own.

## How to Produce It

Arch the middle of your tongue high up toward your hard palate, leaving a narrow channel for the airstream. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and your lips relaxed in a neutral or slightly spread position. Release the sound rapidly into an adjoining vowel or glide into it smoothly from a preceding one without adding friction.

## Spelling Patterns and Rules

Called *и краткое* ('short i'), this letter is visually an **И** wearing a small crescent diacritic (*kratka*). It almost always appears as the offglide in diphthongs at the end of syllables or words, such as in **чай** (*tea*) or **белый** (*white*). It very rarely begins native Russian roots, appearing initially almost exclusively in loanwords like **йогурт**.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

Do not confuse the semivowel **Й** with the full vowel **И**. English speakers often hold the sound too long, turning a one-syllable word like **май** (`[maj]`) into two syllables (*ma-ee*). Keep the articulation brief and tight against the palate so it behaves strictly as a consonant glide.
