## The Sound of Russian Г

The letter **Г** usually represents a voiced velar plosive, identical to the English `g` in "go". Before front vowels like **е**, **и**, or **я**, it softens into a palatalized sound `ɡʲ`. In common grammatical endings like **-ого** and **-его**, it takes on a surprising `v` sound instead of a plosive.

## How to Pronounce It

Press the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate, just as you do for a standard English `g`. Build up air pressure and release it abruptly while vibrating your vocal cords. For the soft variant `ɡʲ`, raise the middle body of your tongue higher toward the roof of your mouth while making contact.

## Spelling and Alphabet Position

**Г** is the fourth letter of the Russian alphabet, corresponding structurally to Greek gamma. The capital printed form resembles a right angle, while the lowercase cursive form looks like a reversed small Latin "s" or a rounded hook. When written at the end of a word or before voiceless consonants, it undergoes final-devoicing and sounds like `k`.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers often default to a hard `ɡ` in all contexts, forgetting that **друг** sounds like `druk` due to word-final devoicing. Another common pitfall is pronouncing the **г** in genitive endings like **хорошего** phonetically as `ɡ` rather than the correct `v`. In a few specific religious words like **Бог**, it is pronounced as a soft fricative `x`.
