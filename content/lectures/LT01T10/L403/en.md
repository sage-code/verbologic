## The Sound of «Ю»

The letter **Ю** represents a 'softening' vowel. At the beginning of a word or after a vowel, it sounds like the 'yu' in English **youth** (`/ju/`). When it directly follows a paired consonant, it indicates that the preceding consonant is palatalized (softened) and produces a pure close back rounded vowel `/u/`, similar to the 'oo' in **moon**.

## How to Pronounce It

To pronounce the initial sound as in **юг**, start with your tongue high and forward as if saying a quick English 'y', then glide smoothly into a rounded, deep 'oo' sound. When reading it after a consonant, do not insert a separate 'y' sound. Instead, raise the middle of your tongue toward your hard palate while pronouncing the consonant itself, transitioning immediately into the vowel.

## Spelling Rules and Behaviour

Capital **Ю** resembles the Latin capital letter 'I' joined by a horizontal stroke to an 'O' (**Ю** / **ю**). It acts as the palatalizing counterpart to the hard vowel **У**. After the Russian hushes and consonants like **ж**, **ш**, and **ц**, **Ю** is almost never written, as spelling rules usually require **у** instead.

## Nuances and Common Traps

In unstressed positions, **ю** shortens slightly toward `/ʊ/` or `/jʊ/`, as heard in **юрист**. The most frequent trap for English speakers is inserting a full English 'y' glide after a consonant, turning a word like **люди** into *l-yoo-di* instead of softening the 'l' directly (`/ˈlʲʉdʲɪ/`). Always ensure the softness belongs to the consonant, not an inserted glide.
