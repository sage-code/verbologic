## The Sound of Н

The letter **Н** represents a dental nasal consonant, either hard `/n/` or soft `/nʲ/`. Unlike the English alveolar 'n', which is articulated on the gum ridge, the hard Russian `/n/` is pronounced with the tongue pressed firmly against the back of the upper teeth. When followed by softening vowels or a soft sign, it becomes the palatalized `/nʲ/`, somewhat resembling the 'ny' in 'canyon'.

## How to Pronounce Н

For the hard sound `/n/`, place the tip and blade of your tongue against the upper front teeth while letting air flow through your nasal passage. Keep the tongue body low and relaxed to prevent accidental palatalization. For the soft `/nʲ/`, press the flat middle part of your tongue upward against the hard palate while touching the teeth, producing a distinctively softened nasal tone.

## Reading and Writing Н

Although **Н** looks identical to the Latin letter 'H', it corresponds phonetically to the Latin letter 'N'. It appears in both uppercase and lowercase forms in all word positions, including prefixes, roots, and suffixes. Double **нн** is very common in Russian adjective and participle suffixes, indicating a lengthened nasal sound.

## Nuances and Common Traps

The most critical distinction is between hard `/n/` as in **нос** (nose) and soft `/nʲ/` as in **нёс** (carried). English speakers frequently confuse the glyph **Н** with the 'h' sound, reading it as an aspiration instead of a nasal. Additionally, be careful not to pull the tongue back to the alveolar ridge, as native Russian ears easily detect the lack of dental contact.
