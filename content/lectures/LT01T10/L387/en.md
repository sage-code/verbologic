## The Sound of Russian О

Under stress, Russian **О** represents a clean mid back rounded vowel `/o/`, comparable to the vowel in British English *thought* or German *Boot*, but never gliding into a diphthong like English *go*. When unstressed, Russian **О** undergoes a process called *akan’ye*, shifting its sound to `/ɐ/` (similar to the *u* in *cut*) right before the stress, or weakening into a neutral schwa `/ə/` elsewhere.

## How to Produce the Sound

To pronounce the stressed `/o/`, round your lips into an oval shape and pull your tongue slightly back into your mouth, raising the back of the tongue toward the soft palate. Avoid closing your lips further at the end so you do not produce an English-style glide (`/oʊ/`). For unstressed occurrences, relax the lips completely, drop the jaw slightly, and produce a short, open sound similar to a relaxed *a*.

## Spelling Rules and Characteristics

The letter **О** is one of the five basic non-palatalising vowels in the Cyrillic alphabet, meaning it signals that the preceding consonant remains hard (non-palatalised). It appears in all positions in words, both at the beginning, middle, and end. Because Russian stress is dynamic and unmarked in standard texts, the letter **О** is the most frequent site of vowel reduction mistakes for non-native readers.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Notice the stark contrast between stressed `/o/` in words like **стол** (`/stol/`) and unstressed reduction in **окно** (`/ɐkˈno/`) or **молоко** (`/məlɐˈko/`). English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing an unstressed **О** as a full `/o/` or as an English diphthong `/oʊ/`. Remember the golden rule: only pronounce a full, round **О** when you are completely certain the syllable carries the primary stress.
