## Pronouncing С

The letter **С** corresponds to the voiceless alveolar fricative `s`, very similar to the English 's' in *sun*. Unlike English, however, it comes in two distinct forms: hard `s` and palatalized soft `sʲ`, the latter having a sharper, hiss-like acoustic quality. Furthermore, it undergoes voicing assimilation, sounding like `z` or `zʲ` when followed immediately by voiced consonants.

## How to Produce the Sound

For the basic hard `s`, place the tip of your tongue behind your lower front teeth or against the alveolar ridge and blow air through a narrow channel. To produce the soft `sʲ`, raise the middle body of your tongue toward the hard palate while maintaining the narrow gap at the front. Keep the airflow steady without adding any vocal cord vibration unless voicing assimilation applies.

## Spelling and Behavior

Russian **С** looks exactly like the Latin uppercase and lowercase 'C', though it represents an 's' sound rather than a 'k' or 'ts'. In common consonant clusters such as **стн** (e.g., *грустный*) or **лнц** (as in our example *солнце*), the neighboring sounds often drop out in natural speech. Pay close attention to vowels following **С**, as soft vowels like **е**, **и**, and **я** will systematically soften it to `sʲ`.

## Variants and Common Traps

Native English speakers frequently misread **С** as a hard 'k' sound due to its visual similarity to Latin 'C'. Another common pitfall is failing to soften the letter in words like *семь* (`sʲemʲ`), pronouncing it as a flat English 's' instead of palatalized `sʲ`. Additionally, remember that prefix **с-** turns into a voiced `z` before voiced consonants, so *сделать* is pronounced /ˈzdʲeɫətʲ/ rather than with a voiceless hiss.
