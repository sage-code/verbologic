## The Sound of В

The letter **В** most commonly represents the voiced labiodental fricative `/v/`, very similar to the English sound in **voice** or **vine**. Unlike English, it regularly undergoes palatalization to become soft `/vʲ/` before soft-indicating vowels. Additionally, it devoices to `/f/` or `/fʲ/` whenever it appears at the end of a word or before a voiceless consonant.

## How to Pronounce В

To produce the hard `/v/`, lightly place your upper front teeth against the inner margin of your lower lip and breathe out while vibrating your vocal cords. For the soft `/vʲ/`, arch the middle of your tongue upward toward the hard palate simultaneously while maintaining light lip-to-teeth contact. Keep your lips relatively relaxed rather than excessively rounded to avoid sounding like a 'w'.

## Spelling and Rules

Uppercase **В** and lowercase **в** share the identical printed shape with the Latin capital letter 'B', which is a notorious pitfall for beginners. It never represents the 'b' sound in Russian, which is written with the distinct letter **Б**. The letter participates actively in Russian voicing assimilation, turning into `/f/` in clusters like **вход** `/fxot/`.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Notice how **вода** uses the standard hard `/v/`, while **вечер** softens into `/vʲeʨɪr/`, and **готов** drops voicing entirely to end in `/f/`. English speakers frequently confuse the glyph **В** with Latin **B**, reading it as `/b/` by accident. Another common error is substituting the English bilabial `/w/` sound for Russian `/v/`; ensure your teeth genuinely contact your lip.
