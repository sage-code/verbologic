## The Sound of Russian Х

The letter **Х** represents a voiceless velar fricative `/x/`, very similar to the *ch* in Scottish *loch* or German *Bach*. Unlike English `h`, it is not a smooth puff of air from the throat, but a rougher sound created in the back of the mouth. Before softening front vowels like **и** or **е**, it softens to a palatalized `/xʲ/`, sounding lighter and produced further forward.

## How to Pronounce It

Open your mouth slightly and raise the back of your tongue toward your soft palate, just as if you were preparing to make a `k` sound. Instead of stopping the airflow completely, leave a small gap and exhale forcefully to create audible friction. Keep your vocal cords relaxed without vibrating, ensuring you do not slip into an English `h` or a hard `k`.

## Spelling Rules and Script

**Х** looks identical to the Latin letter 'X', but you must never pronounce it as `ks`. It can appear anywhere in Russian words: at the beginning (**хлеб**), in the middle (**сахар**), and at the very end (**мох**). It is paired with softening vowels like **е** and **и** to yield the soft variant `/xʲ/`, but it never combines with **ы** in native roots.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers frequently substitute a soft glottal `h` (as in *hat*), which makes words like **хор** sound weak or indistinct to Russian ears. Pay attention to the difference between hard `/x/` in **муха** and soft `/xʲ/` in **мухи**, where the middle of the tongue rises toward the roof of the mouth. Avoid putting a hard `k` burst at the beginning; the airflow should remain continuous and turbulent.
