## The Sound of Ъ

The hard sign **ъ** makes no sound of its own. Instead, it serves as a silent separator that keeps the preceding consonant fully **hard** (non-palatalized) while triggering a distinct glide `/j/` before the following vowel. Unlike English, where compound boundaries often merge into smooth transitions, **ъ** creates a brief acoustic seam, as heard in the transition within words like *sub-unit* or *disjoin*.

## How to Pronounce It

Pronounce the preceding consonant firmly without raising the middle of your tongue toward the hard palate. Immediately follow with a crisp `/j/` glide, jumping right into the succeeding vowel. Do not let the consonant soften into a palatalized sound like `/bʲ/` or `/sʲ/`.

## Spelling Rules

The letter **ъ** appears almost exclusively after prefixes ending in a consonant before the iotated vowels **е**, **ё**, **ю**, and **я**. It never starts a native Russian word, so its uppercase form **Ъ** is rare and seen mainly in all-caps text or acronyms. Do not confuse it with the soft sign **ь**, which actively softens the preceding consonant.

## Common Pitfalls

Compare **объект** `/ɐbˈjekt/` with soft-sign contexts or simple vowel sequences without a separation mark. A frequent learner mistake is softening the consonant into `/bʲ/` as if it were written with **ь**, or omitting the `/j/` entirely to pronounce an English-style glide. Keep the prefix-final consonant deliberately hard and separate.
