## The Sound of Д

The letter **Д** most commonly represents the voiced dental stop `/d/`. Unlike English /d/, which is alveolar and pronounced behind the upper teeth, Russian `/d/` is dental, made with the tip of the tongue touching the back of the upper front teeth. Before front vowels like **е** or **и**, it softens into a palatalized `/dʲ/`.

## Pronunciation Guide

Press the broad tip of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front teeth rather than the gum ridge. Build up air pressure and release it with vocal cord vibration to produce a crisp, clean sound. To make the soft variant `/dʲ/`, flatten the middle of your tongue against the hard palate at the exact moment of release.

## Spelling and Alphabet

**Д** is the fifth letter of the Russian alphabet and originates directly from the Greek delta. In print, it features characteristic 'feet' hanging below the baseline, whereas in cursive it resembles a Latin handwritten *D* in uppercase and a handwritten *g* in lowercase. It pairs regularly with the soft sign **ь** to denote palatalization.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Learners frequently forget Russian final-obstruent devoicing, pronouncing words like **сад** as `*/sad/*` instead of the correct `/sat/`. Additionally, avoid pronouncing the letter too far back on the alveolar ridge as in English. Remember to soften the consonant into `/dʲ/` in words like **день**, ensuring your tongue contacts the palate.
