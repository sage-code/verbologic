## The Sound of Ж

The Russian letter **Ж** represents a voiced retroflex fricative `[ʐ]`, which sounds deeper and darker than the English 's' in *pleasure* `[ʒ]`. Unlike English, Russian **Ж** is inherently hard (non-palatalised) in standard speech, giving it an audible hollow, buzzing resonance. At the end of words or before voiceless consonants, it devoices to `[ʂ]`, sounding like a hard 'sh'.

## How to Pronounce It

Shape your mouth as if to say 'sh', but pull the body of your tongue slightly back and curl the tip upward toward the hard palate without touching it. Turn on your vocal cords to create a continuous voiced buzz while pushing air through the narrow gap. Keep your tongue flat and avoid raising the middle portion toward the roof of your mouth, which prevents unwanted softness.

## Spelling and Script

Uppercase **Ж** and lowercase **ж** resemble a six-legged insect or an asterisk with symmetrical curved outer legs around a central vertical stem. An essential orthographic rule to remember is that **Ж** is followed by **И**, never **Ы** (writing *жи*), yet it is always pronounced hard as `[ʐɨ]`. Similarly, *же* is pronounced hard as `[ʐɛ]` despite using the soft-indicating vowel letter.

## Common Traps and Contrasts

Learners often confuse Russian **Ж** with the soft English sound `[ʒ]` or French 'j', producing an overly soft, hiss-like acoustic. Make sure you devoice word-final **Ж** as in **муж** (`/muʂ/`), rather than letting it hum. Contrast the hard Russian **Ж** in **жар** with the soft affricate **Ч** in **чар** to avoid blurring basic word distinctions.
