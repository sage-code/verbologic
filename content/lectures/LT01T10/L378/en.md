## The Sound of Ё

The letter **Ё** represents the sound `[jo]` initially or after vowels, and `[ɵ]` (a central rounded vowel) when softening a preceding consonant. In English, it resembles the 'yo' in *yonder* or the 'y+aw' sequence, but when following a consonant it blends into a soft glide rather than a harsh separate consonant. Crucially, **Ё** is inherently stressed in almost every native Russian word.

## How to Pronounce Ё

Start by gliding from a palatal approximant `[j]` directly into a rounded mid-back or central vowel `[o]~[ɵ]`. If it comes after a consonant, press the middle of your tongue against the hard palate to soften that consonant, then round your lips firmly as you voice the vowel. Keep the vowel clear, resonant, and never reduce it to a neutral schwa.

## Spelling and Dots

In modern Russian print and digital media, the two dots above **Ё** are frequently omitted and replaced with standard **Е**, which can confuse learners. However, its presence is mandatory in children's books, dictionaries, and official documents to prevent ambiguity. Whenever you see **Ё**, you can be nearly certain that the syllable carries the primary stress of the word.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing **Ё** as a flat `[o]` or forgetting to palatalise the preceding consonant, turning *мёд* (honey) into *мод* (fashion gen. pl.). After unpaired retroflex consonants like **ж** or **ш**, the leading `[j]` disappears, leaving a pure stressed `[o]`, as in *жёлтый*. Watch out for loanwords and compounds like *трёхэтажный*, where a secondary stress might occur.
