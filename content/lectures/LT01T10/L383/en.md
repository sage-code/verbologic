## The Sound of К

The letter **К** represents a voiceless velar plosive, just like English `k` in **skate**, but without aspiration. When followed by fronting vowels like **е** or **и**, it softens into palatalized `[kʲ]`, somewhat similar to the `k` in English **cute**. Unlike in English word-initial stops, Russian **К** is never released with a puff of air.

## How to Pronounce К

To make the basic hard `[k]`, raise the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate, build up air pressure, and release it cleanly without breathing out a puff of air. For the soft `[kʲ]`, arch the middle of your tongue toward the hard palate at the exact moment of closure. Keep your vocal cords completely still during the closure unless it assimilates before a voiced consonant.

## Spelling Rules for К

The capital form **К** and lowercase **к** share the exact same shape, differing only in height. By Russian spelling rules, the letter **К** is never followed by **ы**, **я**, or **ю** in native stems; instead, you write **и**, **а**, and **у**. It frequently serves as a standalone preposition meaning 'to' or 'toward', which attaches phonetically to the following word.

## Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

Notice the stark contrast between hard **кот** (`/kot/`) and soft **кит** (`/kʲit/`). An English speaker's primary mistake is aspirating initial **К** as in English **cat**, which sounds excessively breathy and foreign in Russian. Additionally, remember that **К** assimilates into a voiced `[ɡ]` before voiced stops, as heard in **вокзал** (`/vɐɡˈzal/`).
