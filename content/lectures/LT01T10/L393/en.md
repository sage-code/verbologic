## The Sound of Ф

The letter **Ф** represents the voiceless labiodental fricative `/f/`, exactly like the English 'f' in *fact*. Before soft-indicating vowels or the soft sign, it becomes palatalized as `/fʲ/`, sounding lighter with a simultaneous 'y'-like coloring. Historically, native Slavic words lacked this sound, so most Russian words containing **Ф** are loanwords.

## How to Produce It

Gently place the edges of your upper front teeth against your lower lip and blow air through the small gap without vibrating your vocal cords. For the soft variant `/fʲ/`, arch the middle part of your tongue upward toward the hard palate while keeping the teeth-to-lip contact. Spread your lips slightly wider, as if preparing to smile.

## Spelling and Distribution

Capital **Ф** and lowercase **ф** look like an upright circle bisected by a central vertical stroke, resembling the Greek letter phi. It appears freely at the beginnings, middles, and ends of words. Unlike voiced consonants, **Ф** is already voiceless, meaning it does not change its voicing at the ends of words.

## Contrasts and Common Mistakes

Distinguish the hard `/f/` in **факт** (`/fakt/`) from the soft `/fʲ/` in **фильм** (`/fʲilʲm/`). English speakers often forget to soften the consonant before letters like **и** or **е**, pronouncing words like **кофе** with a fully hard `/f/`. Make sure the tongue actively arches upward when articulating the palatalized variant.
