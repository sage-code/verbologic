## The Sound of Щ

The letter **Щ** represents a long, soft voiceless alveolo-palatal fricative, transcribed as `/ɕː/`. It resembles the sound in English *fresh cheese* or *sheep*, but it is held significantly longer and pronounced with the blade of the tongue pressed much closer to the hard palate. Unlike standard English **sh**, which is retroflex or postalveolar, Russian **Щ** is distinctly palatalised and bright throughout its duration.

## How to Pronounce Щ

Start by pronouncing the English *sh* sound, but spread your lips slightly as if you are beginning to smile. Raise the flat middle blade of your tongue high up against the roof of your mouth, just behind your upper teeth ridge. Release a steady stream of air through the narrow channel while keeping your vocal cords relaxed, sustaining the hissing sound twice as long as an ordinary consonant.

## Spelling Rules and Behavior

**Щ** is inherently soft (palatalised) in Russian, meaning it never pairs with hard vowel indicators like **ы** or **я**. Under standard Russian spelling rules, it is followed by **и** (never **ы**), **у** (never **ю**), and **а** (never **я**), as seen in words like **щи** and **щука**. It can appear anywhere in a word, including word-final positions such as in **борщ**.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

A very frequent error among English speakers is treating **Щ** as a blend of *sh* and *ch* (like *shch*), which reflects an archaic spelling convention rather than modern speech. Avoid inserting an abrupt stop or plosive consonant into the sound; it must remain a smooth, uninterrupted fricative. Be careful not to confuse **Щ** with **Ш**, which is completely hard, hollow, and unpalatalised.
