## The Sound of the Letter X

In Italian, **X** is considered a foreign letter and is not part of the traditional native alphabet. When encountered in loanwords or Greek-derived terms, it is pronounced as the consonant cluster `/ks/`, exactly like the *x* in the English word *extra*. Unlike English, where word-initial *x* often softens to `/z/` (as in *xylophone*), Italian generally keeps both the `/k/` and `/s/` sounds audible, pronouncing *xilofono* as `/ksiˈlɔfono/`.

## How to Produce the Sound

To pronounce `/ks/`, start by pressing the back of your tongue against the soft palate to create a crisp, voiceless `/k/`. Immediately release the closure while sliding the tip of your tongue forward behind your upper teeth to push air through for a sharp `/s/`. Keep the transition smooth and energetic without inserting any vowel between the two consonants.

## Spelling Rules and Word Position

The letter **X** appears exclusively in borrowed vocabulary, scientific nomenclature, Latin prefixes like *extra-* or *ex-*, and international words like *taxi*. Native Italian words that historically contained *x* in Latin underwent assimilation centuries ago and are now written with a double *s* (for example, Latin *saxum* became *sasso*). You will rarely see it outside of modern technical or foreign contexts.

## Nuances and Common Pitfalls

English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing initial *x* as `/z/` as they would in English words like *xenophobia* or *xylophone*. In standard Italian, you must distinctly articulate the initial cluster as `/ks/` (or occasionally adapt it to `/s/` colloquially). Additionally, avoid softening intervocalic `/ks/` into `/ɡz/` unless the word is a direct borrowing from French where that voiced cluster is customary.
