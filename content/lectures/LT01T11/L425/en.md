## The Sound of Z

In Italian, the letter **Z** represents either a voiced affricate `/dz/` or an unvoiced affricate `/ts/`. Unlike English where 'z' is a soft humming sound like in 'zoo', Italian **Z** always involves a crisp dental stop release, similar to the 'ds' in 'beds' or 'ts' in 'cats'. In our example word **zaino**, it takes the voiced pronunciation `/dz/`.

## How to Pronounce It

To articulate the voiced `/dz/`, place the tip of your tongue against the upper teeth ridge to block the airflow completely as if making a 'd'. Then, release it gently into a buzzing 'z' while keeping your vocal cords vibrating. For the voiceless `/ts/`, make the exact same mouth shape, but release the air with a hiss without vibrating your vocal cords.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **Z** can appear as a single letter or doubled (**zz**). Interestingly, even a single intervocalic **Z** is pronounced long or doubled phonetically in standard Italian, as in **spazio** `/ˈspat.tsjo/`. The suffix **-zione** always uses the voiceless affricate `/ts/`.

## Voiced vs. Voiceless Variations

The main trap for English speakers is pronouncing **Z** like an English 'z' `/z/`. Always choose between `/dz/` (as in **zero** or **zaino**) and `/ts/` (as in **pizza** or **grazie**). Memorise common word patterns, and make sure you never pronounce it as a single buzzing fricative without the initial stop component.
