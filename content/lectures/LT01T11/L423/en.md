## The Sound of Italian U

The Italian letter **U** represents the close back rounded vowel `/u/` or the glide `/w/` when preceding another vowel. Unlike the English 'oo' in *boot*, which can glide towards a diphthong, Italian `/u/` is entirely pure, tense, and steady throughout its duration. It never takes on the lax quality heard in English *book* or *put*.

## Pronouncing Italian U

Round your lips tightly into a small, firm circular opening and protrude them forward slightly. Raise the back of your tongue high towards the soft palate without touching it. Maintain this firm position steadily without relaxing your jaw so the sound stays clear and resonant.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **U** always appears written as simple `u`, appearing at the start, middle, or end of words. When paired with `q`, it invariably forms `qu-` and sounds like the semi-vowel `/w/`, as in *questo*. In word-final position with an accent (`ù`), such as in *più* or *virtù*, it carries strong primary stress.

## Variants and Common Traps

Take care to distinguish the full vowel `/u/` in words like *fumo* from the quick glide `/w/` found in diphthongs like *buono* and *auto*. English speakers often make the mistake of reducing unstressed Italian **U** to a weak schwa or opening it to a lax sound like `/ʊ/`. Keep it consistently tense, rounded, and clean in every position.
