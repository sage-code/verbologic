## The Sound of Italian T

The letter **T** represents the voiceless dental plosive `/t/`. Unlike English *t*, which is alveolar and aspirated, Italian **T** is strictly dental and completely unaspirated, meaning no puff of air follows the release. It sounds crisper, sharper, and drier than an English 't'.

## How to Pronounce Italian T

Place the tip or blade of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front teeth, not on the alveolar ridge behind them. Build up air pressure behind the closure, then release it sharply without breathing out. Ensure your vocal cords do not vibrate and keep the release completely free of aspiration.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **T** appears in all standard word positions and combines with every vowel. When doubled as **tt**, as in *notte* or *gatto*, the closure is held for roughly twice as long before the sudden release. It consistently retains the sound `/t/` and never softens into an affricate before *i* or *e*.

## Mistakes to Avoid

Native English speakers often aspirate Italian **T**, producing a breathy sound like [tʰ], which sounds foreign in Italian. Another common error is tapping the sound or pronouncing it as a flap `[ɾ]` between vowels, as in American English *water*. Always keep the contact dental, sharp, and clean.
