## The Sound of Italian E

Italian **E** represents two distinct vowel sounds when stressed: the close-mid `/e/` (similar to the vowel in French *été* or a pure Scottish English *day* without the glide) and the open-mid `/ɛ/` (like the vowel in English *bed*). In all unstressed syllables, it is invariably pronounced as the closed `/e/`. Unlike English vowels, Italian **E** is always crisp, monophthongal, and never ends in an off-glide `/ɪ/`.

## How to Produce the Sounds

For the close-mid sound `/e/`, spread your lips slightly as if smiling gently and raise the body of your tongue high in the front of your mouth, keeping your jaw relatively closed. To produce the open-mid sound `/ɛ/`, drop your lower jaw noticeably further down and relax your tongue slightly lower. Ensure your lips remain completely unrounded and keep the vowel steady without sliding into a diphthong.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **E** appears in all word positions, whether initial, medial, or final. When accented at the end of a word, Italian spelling clearly distinguishes the sounds: an acute accent marks the closed sound (as in *perché*), while a grave accent indicates the open sound (as in *caffè* and the verb *è*). Inside a word, standard spelling does not mark the difference, so learners must memorize whether a stressed **E** is open or closed.

## Contrasting Open and Closed E

The distinction between `/e/` and `/ɛ/` can create minimal pairs: for instance, *pesca* with closed `/e/` means 'fishing', whereas *pesca* with open `/ɛ/` means 'peach'. A very common mistake for English speakers is turning final stressed `/e/` into the diphthong `/eɪ/` (as in *say*). Always ensure the vowel remains pure and abruptly clipped rather than drawn out.
