## The Sound of J

The letter **J** is not part of the traditional standard Italian alphabet and appears primarily in loanwords and historical place names. In English borrowings like *jeans*, it is pronounced as the voiced postalveolar affricate `/dʒ/`, identical to the English 'j' in 'judge'. In Latin expressions or geographical names like *Jesolo*, it is pronounced as the palatal approximant `/j/`, matching the English 'y' in 'yes'.

## How to Pronounce J

For the loanword sound `/dʒ/`, touch the tip and blade of your tongue to the roof of your mouth just behind your upper teeth, build pressure, and release with vocal fold vibration. For the glide `/j/`, arch the middle of your tongue close to the hard palate without touching it, letting your voice flow smoothly. Keep your lips relaxed or slightly spread depending on the following vowel.

## Spelling and Usage

Historically, **J** (*i lunga*) was used in Italian to represent a long or doubled 'i' at the end of plural words (such as *tempj*), but this practice is completely obsolete today. You will encounter uppercase **J** and lowercase **j** mainly in recent foreign borrowings such as *judo*, *jogging*, or *jeans*. It also persists in traditional regional place names like *Jesolo* or *Maraja* and certain formal surnames.

## Variants and Common Mistakes

English speakers often assume **J** always sounds like `/dʒ/`, but applying that rule universally in Italy leads to errors with toponyms like *Jesolo* (pronounced `/ˈjɛ.zo.lo/`, never `/ˈdʒɛ.zo.lo/`). Conversely, do not pronounce English loanwords with the French soft `/ʒ/` sound; Italians firmly use `/dʒ/` for terms like *jeans* or *jogging*. Always check whether a word originates from English or Latin/regional Italian to select the right sound.
