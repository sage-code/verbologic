## The Sound of Italian P

The Italian letter **P** represents the voiceless bilabial plosive `/p/`. Unlike English speakers who release a strong puff of air at the start of words, Italian speakers pronounce this consonant without aspiration. It sounds very crisp, much like the unaspirated 'p' heard in the English word *spin* rather than *pin*.

## How to Pronounce P

Bring both lips firmly together to stop the airflow completely, then release them quickly to let the air pop out. Make sure your vocal cords do not vibrate during the closure, as this is a voiceless sound. Hold back the breath so there is no lingering rush of air after your lips part.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **P** can appear in single form or doubled as **pp** between vowels, where it is held longer as a geminate consonant (`/pː/`). It occurs word-initially, word-internally, and in consonant clusters such as *pr*, *pl*, and occasionally *ps*. Italian words very rarely end in **p**, appearing solely in modern loanwords like *stop*.

## Aspiration and Doubling

English learners frequently make the mistake of aspirating Italian **P**, turning words like *pane* into *pʰane*, which sounds unnatural to native ears. Additionally, pay close attention to the contrast between single `/p/` and double `/pː/`. Failing to lengthen the closure in words like *cappa* will make them sound identical to *capa*.
