## The Sound of Italian V

The Italian letter **V** represents the voiced labiodental fricative `/v/`. It sounds virtually identical to the English 'v' in words like *vine* or *never*. Unlike Spanish, Italian preserves a crisp and clear friction without softening into an approximant or merging with 'b'.

## How to Pronounce It

Rest your upper front teeth lightly against your lower lip, leaving enough space for air to stream through continuously. Engage your vocal cords to create a voiced buzz as the breath escapes. Ensure your lips do not close completely, keeping the friction sharp and steady.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **V** appears at the beginning, middle, and occasionally doubled between vowels as in *avvocato* (`/av.voˈka.to/`). When written as a double consonant, prolong the labiodental contact to give it distinct geminate length. It never occurs at the end of native Italian words.

## Contrasts and Common Traps

Spanish speakers must be careful not to confuse `/v/` with `/b/` or soften it into `[β]`. In Italian, minimal pairs like *bere* ('to drink') and *vere* ('true') depend entirely on distinguishing a full bilabial stop from a labiodental fricative. German and English speakers generally find this sound straightforward, but must remember to pronounce double **vv** noticeably longer.
