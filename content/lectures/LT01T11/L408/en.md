## The Sound of the Letter D

The Italian **D** represents a voiced dental plosive, transcribed as `/d/`. Unlike the English alveolar 'd', where the tongue contacts the gum ridge behind the teeth, the Italian sound requires direct contact with the back of the upper front teeth. It is crisp, fully voiced, and never aspirated like English stops can be.

## How to Pronounce D

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the inner surface of your upper front teeth rather than the gum ridge above them. Allow vocal cord vibration to build up behind the closure, then release the air cleanly without a puff of breath. Keep your lips relaxed and shaped according to the adjacent vowel.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **D** appears across all positions in Italian words, commonly starting syllables or appearing between vowels. When written double as **dd**, as in **freddo**, the closure is held noticeably longer before release. It can also undergo syntactic doubling (*raddoppiamento fonosintattico*) after certain stressed monosyllables.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

The main variation is length: a short `/d/` in **dado** versus an elongated geminate `/dː/` in **cadere** vs. **cadde**. English speakers often make the mistake of articulating **D** too far back on the alveolar ridge, which sounds unnatural in Italian. Avoid releasing the consonant with an audible puff of aspiration, keeping the sound strictly dental and voiced throughout.
