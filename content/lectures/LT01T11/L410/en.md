## The Sound of Italian F

The letter **F** represents the voiceless labiodental fricative `/f/`, identical to the sound in the English word *father*. It is produced consistently and clearly regardless of whether it appears at the beginning, middle, or end of a syllable. Unlike some consonants, it never softens or voices into a `/v/` sound.

## How to Pronounce F

Gently rest your upper incisors against the inner edge of your lower lip. Blow air continuously through the narrow gap without vibrating your vocal cords. When pronouncing the doubled **FF** as in *caffè*, hold this friction slightly longer to create a distinct geminate consonant.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **F** is written identically in uppercase (*F*) and lowercase (*f*). In Italian orthography, it frequently appears single or geminated as **ff**, but it never combines into digraphs like the English *ph*. Italian exclusively uses **f** for this sound, adapting Greek roots into spellings like *filosofia* rather than *philosophia*.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

Because `/f/` exists identically across English and Italian, native English speakers rarely mispronounce the basic sound. The main challenge lies in distinguishing single **f** from double **ff**, where the double version requires a held, tense closure. Take care not to shorten double consonants in words like *fatto* compared to *fato*.
