## The Sound of Italian A

The letter **A** in Italian consistently represents an open central unrounded vowel `[ä]` (broadly transcribed as `/a/`). It is much clearer and brighter than the typical English schwa `/ə/` or the vowel in 'cat'. Unlike English, it never reduces to an obscure murmur in unstressed positions.

## How to Pronounce Italian A

Open your mouth comfortably wide and keep your tongue resting flat on the floor of the mouth, with the tip touching behind your lower front teeth. Relax your lips without rounding them. Produce a clean, resonant vowel sound similar to the 'ah' in 'father', keeping it stable throughout.

## Spelling and Rules

Italian **A** appears in all positions: word-initial, medial, and final. When an accented **à** appears at the end of a word (such as in *città* or *sarà*), it indicates that the final syllable carries the word's primary stress. It forms diphthongs easily with gliding vowels, as in *aria* or *auto*.

## Nuances and Common Pitfalls

The vowel `/a/` sounds naturally lengthened in open stressed syllables (like the first `a` in *casa*), but stays short in unstressed positions (like the final `a` in *casa*). A frequent mistake for English speakers is reducing unstressed Italian **A** into a weak schwa, especially at the end of words. Always keep every instance of **A** fully open, crisp, and pure.
