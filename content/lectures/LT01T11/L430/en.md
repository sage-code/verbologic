## The Sound of Y

In Italian, **Y** is considered a foreign letter known as *ipsilon* or *i greca*. When placed before vowels, it represents the glide sound `/j/`, identical to the English 'y' in *yes*. When followed by consonants or at the end of loanwords, it is pronounced as the clear vowel `/i/`, exactly like the Italian letter **i**.

## How to Say It

To articulate `/j/`, raise the body of your tongue high toward your hard palate without creating frictional air turbulence. Spread your lips slightly, just as you would for a tense English 'ee', and transition smoothly and quickly into the succeeding vowel. For the vowel variant `/i/`, maintain that high, front tongue position with steady, unconstricted voicing.

## Spelling and Usage

**Y** is not part of the standard 21-letter traditional Italian alphabet. It appears solely in loanwords, brand names, and modern international borrowings such as **yogurt**, **yoga**, or **spray**. Consequently, native Italian vocabulary never uses this letter, always relying on **i** to produce glide and vowel sounds.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Depending on the origin of the borrowed term, **Y** flips between a glide `/j/` as in **yogurt** and a pure vowel `/i/` as in **hobby**. A common mistake for learners is trying to give it a French or German rounded 'u' sound (`/y/`). Always keep the sound fully unrounded, using either `/j/` or `/i/`.
