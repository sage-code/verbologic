## The Sound of Italian S

The letter **S** has two primary values in Italian: a voiceless alveolar fricative `/s/` like in English 'sun', and a voiced alveolar fricative `/z/` like in 'zebra'. Unlike English, an initial **s** before a vowel is crisp and always voiceless, never blurred. Before voiced consonants such as **b**, **d**, or **g**, it automatically becomes voiced as `/z/`.

## How to Pronounce S

Place the tip of your tongue close to the alveolar ridge behind your upper front teeth, leaving a narrow groove for air to hiss through. For voiceless `/s/`, keep your vocal cords still so only the sharp friction of escaping air is heard. For voiced `/z/`, engage your vocal cords while maintaining the exact same mouth position to add a clear buzzing vibration.

## Spelling Rules and Contexts

The letter **S** is written identically in lowercase and uppercase print as `s` and `S`. When doubled as **ss** between vowels, it is always voiceless and pronounced with lengthened duration, as in *rosso*. Combined with **c** before **e** or **i** (`sc`), it forms the postalveolar fricative `/ʃ/`, sounding like English 'sh' in *scena*.

## Comparing Voiced and Voiceless S

In standard Italian and especially northern accents, intervocalic single **s** is voiced as `/z/` in words like *rosa* (`/ˈrɔ.za/`), but voiceless as `/s/` in central-southern usage. In contrast, word-initial position before a vowel is strictly voiceless, as in *sera* (`/ˈse.ra/`). A frequent mistake for learners is voicing initial **s** as `/z/` or failing to pronounce doubled **ss** distinctly longer than single **s**.
