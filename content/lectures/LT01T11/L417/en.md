## The Sound of the Italian O

The Italian letter **O** represents two distinct back vowels: close-mid `/o/` and open-mid `/ɔ/`. Unlike English vowels, neither sound ever glides into an off-glide dipthong like the English 'go' or 'boat'. Both sounds remain pure, clear, and steady throughout their entire duration.

## How to Produce the Sound

For the closed `/o/`, raise the back of your tongue toward the soft palate and round your lips tightly into a small circle. For open `/ɔ/`, drop your jaw slightly lower and relax the lip rounding so the opening is wider. Keep your tongue stable without letting it shift into a 'w' sound at the end.

## Spelling and Distribution

Standard Italian orthography writes both sounds simply as **o**, without indicating openness in unmarked spelling. When a word carries an accent on a final stressed syllable, a grave accent (**ò**) marks open `/ɔ/` as in *però*, while an acute accent (**ó**) occasionally marks closed `/o/`. In unstressed syllables, standard Italian always neutralizes the letter to closed `/o/`.

## Comparing Closed and Open O

The contrast creates minimal pairs such as *bótte* (`/ˈbot.te/`, wine barrel) versus *bòtte* (`/ˈbɔt.te/`, blows or beating). English speakers tend to turn Italian closed `/o/` into the diphthong `/oʊ/`, which sounds foreign to native ears. Practice keeping your lips and tongue perfectly immobile while sounding out pure vowels.
