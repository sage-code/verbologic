## The Italian R Sound

The Italian letter **R** represents an alveolar trill or tap `/r/`, completely different from the English postalveolar approximant `/ɹ/`. Between vowels it is lightly tapped with a single flap of the tongue tip, while at the start of words or when doubled (`rr`) it is strongly rolled. English speakers must avoid curling the tongue backwards or producing a throat-based sound.

## How to Pronounce R

Place the tip of your tongue loosely against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth. Exhale a steady stream of air so that the tip of the tongue vibrates rapidly against the roof of the mouth. Keep your lips relaxed and do not tense the back of your throat.

## Spelling and Positions

The letter **R** appears in all standard word positions, frequently clustering with stops like in *treno* or *prato*. When written double as **RR** (e.g., *terra*), the trill must be held noticeably longer and with greater vibration to distinguish it from single **R** (e.g., *caro* vs. *carro*). Capital **R** follows standard Italian capitalization rules at the beginning of sentences and proper names.

## Variants and Common Traps

Unlike French or German, standard Italian never uses a uvular sound (`/ʁ/` or `/ʀ/`), an accent known in Italy as *erre moscia*. A single intervocalic **R** produces a quick tap `/ɾ/`, whereas doubled **RR** requires multiple rapid contacts `/rː/`. English learners frequently make the mistake of using the soft, rhotic English `/ɹ/`, which makes words sound heavily accented and difficult to understand.
