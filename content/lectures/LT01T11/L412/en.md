## The Sound of the Letter H

In Italian, the letter **H** (*acca*) is completely **silent** (`∅`). Unlike in English, it is never aspirated or pronounced as a puff of breath like `[h]`. Instead, it serves purely orthographical and grammatical functions.

## How to Pronounce H

Do not produce any sound or friction in your throat when you see this letter. Simply pronounce the following vowel directly with a clear, uninterrupted vocal onset. Relax your throat completely and move straight to the next sound.

## Spelling Rules and Use

The letter **H** appears initially in four forms of the verb *avere* (*ho*, *hai*, *ha*, *hanno*) to distinguish them from other homophones like *o*, *ai*, *a*, and *anno*. It is also used after **C** and **G** before *e* and *i* (*che*, *chi*, *ghe*, *ghi*) to indicate a hard velar stop (`[k]` or `[ɡ]`). Finally, it appears in interjections like *ah!* or *boh!* and in modern loanwords like *hotel*.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

English speakers frequently attempt to aspirate the **H** in Italian words like *hotel* or *hanno*, which sounds unnatural to native speakers. Remember that *hanno* is pronounced identically to *anno* (`/ˈan.no/`). Never exhale or whisper an `[h]` sound in standard Italian.
