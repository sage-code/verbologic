## The Sound of Italian K

The letter **K** is not part of the traditional Italian alphabet and represents the voiceless velar plosive `/k/`. It sounds virtually identical to the English 'k' in 'skill' or the Italian hard **C** before 'a', 'o', or 'u'. Unlike in English, it is pronounced without aspiration (a puff of air) in natural Italian speech.

## How to Pronounce It

Press the back of your tongue against your soft palate (velum) to completely block the airflow. Release the air sharply while keeping your vocal cords completely still. Ensure you do not add an English-style breathy puff of air after the release.

## Spelling and Usage

In Italian, **K** appears exclusively in loanwords, scientific terminology, foreign proper nouns, and informal modern slang (like 'k' replacing 'ch'). Standard Italian native words invariably use **C** or **CH** to represent the `/k/` sound. It can appear at the beginning or in the middle of imported words, such as *kayak* or *bikini*.

## Comparisons and Common Pitfalls

Native English speakers often aspirate `/k/`, producing a noticeable puff of air as in 'kite'. In Italian, keep the consonant crisp and unaspirated, just like the 'k' in 'sky' or the Italian letter **C** in *casa*. Avoid voicing it into a `/ɡ/` sound, and do not confuse its modern slang usage with formal orthography.
