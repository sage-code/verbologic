## The Sound of Italian M

The letter **M** in Italian represents the voiced bilabial nasal `/m/`, identical to the sound in English 'mother'. It remains pure and fully vocalized in all phonetic environments, never becoming muffled or silent. When doubled as **mm**, it is held noticeably longer with sustained vocal vibration.

## Pronunciation Technique

Press your upper and lower lips gently but completely together to block the airflow through the mouth. Lower the velum at the back of your throat so the air streams freely through your nose while your vocal cords vibrate. Release the lips cleanly into the following vowel without adding an extra puff of air.

## Orthographic Rules

Italian **M** appears at the beginning, in the middle, and doubled within words, but it is rarely found at the very end except in borrowed terms. Before the bilabial plosives **p** and **b**, Italian spelling strictly requires **m** rather than **n**, as in *bambino* or *sempre*. Double **mm** indicates a distinct geminate consonant that lengthens the closure.

## Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

Single `/m/` contrasts sharply with double geminate `/mː/`, as heard between *fumo* (smoke) and *fummo* (we were). English speakers often shorten double **mm** into a regular single consonant, which flattens the rhythm of Italian. Ensure you sustain the lip closure to make double consonants clearly audible.
