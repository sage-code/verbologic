## The Sound of Q

In Italian, the letter **Q** represents the voiceless velar stop `/k/` and is virtually always paired with the letter **u**, forming the sequence `/kw/`. It sounds exactly like the English **qu** in *quick* or *quality*, though without any aspiration on the initial consonant. Unlike English, this letter never appears at the end of a word or before a consonant.

## How to Pronounce It

Press the back of your tongue firmly against the soft palate at the roof of your mouth to stop the airflow, exactly as you would for a crisp **k**. Simultaneously round your lips in preparation for the gliding **w** sound. Release the air cleanly from the back of the mouth without puffing extra breath as your lips transition into the following vowel.

## Spelling Rules and Context

The letter **Q** is always written in the combination **qu-** followed by a vowel, as in *questo* or *quando*. The doubled version of this sound is almost always written as **cq**, seen in words like *acqua* or *nacque*, rather than *qq* (with the sole exception of *soqquadro*). Words where the `/kw/` sequence is spelled with **cu-** (such as *cuore* or *scuola*) are historical exceptions that must be memorised.

## Common Traps and Comparison

Learners often confuse when to write **qu-** versus **cu-**, since both represent the identical phonetic cluster `/kw/`. Native English speakers must also be careful to avoid aspirating the `/k/`, keeping it sharp and clean as in Spanish or French. Take care not to pronounce **qu** as a French-style single `/k/`; the **u** is always pronounced as a glide `/w/` in Italian.
