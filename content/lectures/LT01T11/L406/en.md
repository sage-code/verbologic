## The Sound of B

The Italian letter **B** represents a voiced bilabial plosive, transcribed as `/b/`. It sounds almost identical to the English *b* in words like *boat* or *table*, but it is pronounced with slightly firmer lip contact and without aspiration. Unlike Spanish, Italian never softens this letter into a fricative between vowels.

## How to Produce It

Press both lips firmly together to completely block the airflow from your lungs. Build up slight air pressure behind the lips while engaging your vocal cords to create voicing. Then, release the lips crisply and cleanly without blowing extra air out.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **B** appears at the start of words, internally between vowels, and in clusters before liquids such as *br* or *bl*. When doubled as **bb**, as in *babbo* or *nebbia*, it is held longer to produce a distinct geminate consonant. It is never silent in native Italian words.

## Nuances and Common Pitfalls

Pay close attention to single versus double **b**: a single `/b/` in *bacio* is short, while the double `/bː/` in *abbadia* requires pausing briefly on the lip closure. Spanish and Portuguese speakers must be careful not to soften `/b/` into a weak `[β]` sound between vowels. English speakers should ensure they do not add an aspirating puff of air.
