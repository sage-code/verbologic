## The Sound of Q

In Spanish, the letter **Q** represents the voiceless velar plosive `/k/`. Unlike English, Spanish **Q** is never followed by an audible 'w' sound; the digraph **qu** represents a pure `/k/` identical to the sound in *skip* without puffing air.

## Pronouncing Q

Raise the back of your tongue firmly against the soft palate (velum) to block the airflow completely. Release the contact sharply with your vocal cords silent, making a crisp sound. Ensure you do not add aspiration (a puff of breath) as in the English word *kite*.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **Q** almost never appears alone; it is virtually always written in the digraphs **que** and **qui**, where the **u** is entirely silent. It serves to produce the `/k/` sound before front vowels, complementing **c** which handles **ca**, **co**, and **cu**.

## Common Mistakes

English speakers often instinctively pronounce **qu** as `/kw/`, saying *kweso* instead of `/ˈke.so/`. Remember that the **u** is purely an orthographic marker and must remain completely silent before **e** and **i**.
