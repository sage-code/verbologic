## The Sound of the Letter D

In Spanish, the letter **D** has two primary sounds: a hard stop `[d]` and a soft approximant `[ð]`. The stop `[d]` is dental rather than alveolar, meaning the tongue touches the upper teeth rather than the gum ridge as in English. The softer sound `[ð]` resembles the voiced *th* in English "feather", occurring between vowels.

## How to Pronounce It

For the stop sound, place the tip of your tongue directly against the back of your upper front teeth and release with a gentle burst of voice. For the softer sound between vowels, let the tongue approach the upper teeth without fully sealing the airflow, allowing the sound to flow smoothly. Keep your lips relaxed and vocal cords vibrating throughout.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **D** consistently represents dental sounds in Spanish and never takes accents of its own. It appears at the beginning of words like **día**, medially like **todo**, and at the end of words such as **verdad**. In word-final position, it is often pronounced very softly or even dropped in casual speech.

## Sound Variations and Common Pitfalls

Avoid using the English alveolar *d*, which creates an unnatural percussive sound to Spanish ears. Pay close attention to intervocalic contexts where **D** softens to `[ð]`, as in **nada**; pronouncing a crisp English `[d]` here is a frequent learner mistake. In central Spain, a final **D** as in **Madrid** may sound close to a faint voiceless `[θ]`.
