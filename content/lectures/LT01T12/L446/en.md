## The Sound of the Spanish O

The Spanish letter **O** represents a pure close-mid back rounded vowel, transcribed as `/o/`. Unlike English vowels in words like *go* or *boat*, it is completely stable and never glides into a 'w' sound. It is crisp, relatively short, and maintains its distinct quality whether stressed or unstressed.

## How to Produce /o/

Round your lips moderately into an oval shape without pursing them too tightly. Pull the body of your tongue back toward the soft palate to a mid-height position. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and hold the articulators completely still throughout the vowel so it does not turn into a diphthong.

## Spelling Rules and Written Accent

The letter **O** regularly corresponds to the sound `/o/` in all positions. When an acute accent is placed over it (**ó**), as in *camión*, it indicates irregular lexical stress rather than a shift in vowel quality. In modern orthography, the accent is no longer used between numerals (e.g., *1 o 2*).

## Avoiding Typical Mistakes

English speakers frequently make the error of diphthongising `/o/` into `[oʊ]`, turning words like *no* into *noh-oo*. Always cut the vowel off cleanly without moving your lips or jaw toward the end of the sound. Unlike English vowels, Spanish unstressed **o** never reduces to a schwa `[ə]` and must remain fully articulated.
