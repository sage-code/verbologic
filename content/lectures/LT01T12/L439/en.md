## The Sound of Spanish I

The letter **I** in Spanish primarily represents the close front unrounded vowel `/i/`, very similar to the 'ee' in English *meet*, but tenser and shorter. Unlike English, Spanish never reduces an unstressed **I** to a lax sound like the 'i' in *bit*. When placed next to another vowel in the same syllable, it functions as a glide, pronounced like the English 'y' in *yes* or the offglide in *boy*.

## How to Produce the Sound

Spread your lips slightly into a relaxed smile and arch the front part of your tongue high up toward the hard palate. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and hold the tongue position firm without letting it drop toward a relaxed position. The sound should feel crisp, forward, and bright in the mouth.

## Spelling and Accents

The letter is written as lowercase **i** and uppercase **I**. When it carries the word's primary stress to break a diphthong or avoid irregular stress rules, it takes an acute accent mark as **í** (for example, in *país* or *difícil*). Note that the dot on lowercase **i** is completely replaced by the accent mark (*í*).

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

In full syllables, **I** sounds pure as in *cine* (`/ˈθi.ne/`), while in diphthongs it compresses into a glide as in *bien* (`/bjen/`) or *aire* (`/ˈai̯.ɾe/`). English speakers frequently make the mistake of pronouncing unstressed Spanish **i** as lax `/ɪ/`, turning a word like *visita* into something sounding like English *visit-uh*. Always keep the vowel tense, sharp, and consistent regardless of stress.
