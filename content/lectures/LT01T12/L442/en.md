## The Sound of Spanish L

The Spanish letter **L** represents an alveolar lateral approximant, written as `/l/`. Unlike the English 'dark L' heard at the end of syllables in words like *ball* or *milk*, the Spanish **L** is always 'clear' and bright. It maintains this fronted, crisp articulation regardless of whether it begins a word, sits between vowels, or closes a syllable.

## How to Pronounce L

Press the tip of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge, right behind your upper front teeth. Keep the back of your tongue low and relaxed without pulling it toward your throat, allowing the air to flow smoothly around the sides of your tongue. Engage your vocal cords to produce a clean, resonant voice without any hollow or velarized resonance.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **L** is very frequent and can appear in word-initial, medial, and final positions, such as in *leche*, *calor*, and *fácil*. It regularly combines with consonants to form clusters like *bl-*, *cl-*, *fl-*, *gl-*, and *pl-*. Do not confuse single **L** with the digraph **LL**, which historically represented a palatal lateral `/ʎ/` and is widely pronounced as `/ʝ/` in modern Peninsular Spanish.

## Clear Articulation vs. Dark L

A primary mistake English speakers make is velarizing **L** at the end of syllables in words like *alto* or *sol*, turning it into an English dark sound. Compare the clear flow in Spanish *mil* with the muffled quality of English *mill*. Always ensure the back of the tongue stays down so the sound stays bright, forward, and crisply defined.
