## The Sound of M

The letter **M** in Spanish represents the voiced bilabial nasal `[m]`, identical to the English sound in *mother*. It is a clear, resonant nasal produced by completely closing both lips while vibrating your vocal cords. At the very end of loanwords like *álbum*, standard Peninsular Spanish naturally neutralises this sound into an alveolar `[n]`.

## How to Produce It

Press your upper and lower lips together gently to form a complete seal. Lower your soft palate so that air flows entirely out through your nose. Engage your vocal cords to create voice, then release your lips cleanly into the following vowel without any puff of aspiration.

## Spelling and Distribution

Written as uppercase **M** and lowercase **m**, it appears predominantly before vowels and before the bilabial consonants **p** and **b** (as in *campo* or *cambio*). Standard Spanish spelling rules strictly require **m** instead of **n** before **b** and **p**. The letter almost never occurs at the end of native Spanish words, appearing finally only in Latin borrowings like *ídem* or *currículum*.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers generally find Spanish **m** very straightforward because `[m]` is practically identical across both languages. The main pitfall occurs at word boundaries: English speakers often force a hard bilabial `[m]` at the end of words like *álbum*, whereas native Spaniards almost universally pronounce it as `[n]`. Additionally, keep adjacent vowels crisp and avoid overly nasalising them before the lips part.
