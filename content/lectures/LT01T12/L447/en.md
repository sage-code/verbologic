## The Sound of Spanish P

The Spanish letter **P** represents the voiceless bilabial plosive `/p/`. Unlike the English 'p', which is strongly aspirated with a puff of air at the beginning of words, Spanish `/p/` is always completely **unaspirated**. It sounds much closer to the 'p' in English *spot* rather than the one in *pot*.

## How to Pronounce P

Press your upper and lower lips firmly together to completely block the airflow from your lungs. Release your lips cleanly and abruptly without letting out an audible breath of air. Keep your vocal cords silent during the closure, letting them vibrate only when moving into the following vowel.

## Spelling Rules and Occurrence

The letter **P** consistently corresponds to the sound `/p/` wherever it appears in Spanish words. It frequently occurs at the beginning of words and between vowels, as in **padre** or **sopa**. In rare Greek-origin prefixes like *ps-* or *pn-* (e.g., **psicología**), the **p** is frequently silent in everyday speech, though keeping the written letter is standard.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

English speakers most commonly make the mistake of aspirating Spanish **P** at the beginning of syllables, producing an unnatural puff of air. Practice holding a sheet of paper or your hand before your mouth while saying **pan** or **poco**; the paper should barely move. Always ensure the sound is crisp, dry, and tight.
