## The sound of the Spanish A

The Spanish letter **A** represents an open central unrounded vowel, transcribed as `/a/`. Unlike English vowels, it is pure, bright, and never glides into a diphthong like the vowel in 'cat' or 'father'. It remains clear and fully articulated even when unstressed, never weakening into a schwa.

## How to pronounce Spanish A

Open your mouth comfortably wide and keep your tongue completely relaxed, resting flat in the bottom of your mouth. Your lips should be relaxed and unrounded, neither smiling excessively nor pushed forward. Voice the sound continuously from the throat without letting your jaw move or tongue shift.

## Spelling and written forms

The letter is written as uppercase **A** and lowercase **a**. When it carries the word's primary stress against standard accentuation rules, it takes an acute accent mark as **á** (e.g., *está*, *árbol*). The pronunciation of **á** is acoustically identical to plain **a**, denoting only stress rather than vowel quality.

## Variants and common mistakes

In Spanish, `/a/` has essentially no meaningful positional variants; it sounds remarkably stable across all phonetic environments. English speakers often make the mistake of reducing unstressed **a** at the end of words to a weak, muffled schwa `/ə/`. Always keep every **a** crisp, open, and resonant, as in *casa* (`/ˈka.sa/`), ensuring both vowels sound alike.
