## The Sound of C

In European Spanish, the letter **C** represents two entirely distinct sounds depending on what follows it. Before the vowels **e** and **i**, it is pronounced as the voiceless dental fricative `/θ/`, identical to the 'th' in English *think*. Before **a**, **o**, **u**, or consonants, it sounds like the hard `/k/` in *cat*.

## How to Pronounce It

For the `/θ/` sound before **e** or **i**, place the tip of your tongue gently between your upper and lower front teeth and blow air through without vibrating your vocal cords. For the `/k/` sound before other letters, press the back of your tongue against your soft palate and release the trapped air sharply. Unlike in English, Spanish `/k/` is crisp and produced without a puff of breath.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **C** never takes an accent mark on its own, but it frequently anchors common Spanish syllable structures. When a hard `/k/` sound needs to be written before **e** or **i**, Spanish changes the spelling to **qu** instead of **c** (as in *queso*). Conversely, the soft `/θ/` sound is written with **z** before **a**, **o**, and **u** (as in *zapato*).

## Dialects and Common Pitfalls

In central and northern Spain, pronouncing **c** before **e** or **i** as an English 's' is considered a dialectal feature called *seseo*, typical of Latin America and parts of Andalusia. In standard Castilian, you should keep `/θ/` distinct from `/s/` to avoid merging pairs like *casa* (house) and *caza* (hunt). Take care also to keep the hard `/k/` unaspirated to sound natural.
