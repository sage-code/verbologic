## The Sound of K

In Spanish, the letter **K** represents the voiceless velar plosive `/k/`. It sounds very similar to the English 'k' in *skin*, but unlike standard English initial 'k', it is completely **unaspirated**—meaning you should release no puff of air. The letter is rare in Spanish and occurs almost exclusively in loanwords and scientific terms.

## How to Pronounce It

Place the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate (velum) to block the airflow entirely. Build up slight air pressure behind the closure, then release it sharply without breathing out extra air. Keep your vocal cords completely still, as this is a voiceless stop.

## Spelling Rules

The letter **k** is relatively uncommon in native Spanish vocabulary, which traditionally uses **c** (before *a*, *o*, *u*) or **qu** (before *e*, *i*) for the `/k/` sound. You will see **k** in borrowed vocabulary such as *kilo*, *karma*, or *anorak*. In everyday writing, capital **K** is used at the start of proper nouns and sentences just like in English.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Spanish **K** has only one invariant sound, `/k/`, regardless of whether it appears at the start, middle, or end of a word. The most frequent pitfall for English speakers is **aspiration**; pronouncing *kilo* with a heavy breath of air sounds distinctly foreign. Focus on making the release clean, dry, and crisp.
