## The Sound of B

In Spanish, the letter **b** does not always sound like the English 'b'. While it is pronounced as a firm stop `[b]` after a pause or after nasal consonants, in most other positions—especially between vowels—it softens into a voiced bilabial approximant `[β]`. Crucially, in standard Spanish from Spain, the letters **b** and **v** represent the exact same sounds.

## How to Produce It

For the stop variant `[b]`, press your lips firmly together to completely block the airflow, build slight pressure, and release with vocal fold vibration. For the soft variant `[β]`, bring your lips close together without letting them touch completely, allowing the voice to flow continuously through the narrow slit. Make sure your teeth do not touch your bottom lip, as this sound is never produced like an English 'v'.

## Spelling and Rules

The letter **b** appears frequently across all syllable positions in Spanish, such as in prefixes like *sub-* or endings like *-aba*. Because **b** and **v** are phonetically indistinguishable, spelling bees and orthography rules heavily test which letter to use. It is traditionally called **be** or **be alta** to distinguish it orally from **uve**.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Listen to the difference between the hard `[b]` in *bueno* and the softened `[β]` in *lobo* or *beber*. The most frequent mistake for English speakers is using the English 'v' sound `[v]` involving the upper teeth and lower lip, which does not exist in standard Spanish. Another common error is using a harsh, explosive English 'b' between vowels where native speakers use a gentle glide.
