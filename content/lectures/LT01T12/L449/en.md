## The Sound of R

In Spanish, the letter **R** represents two distinct sounds: a single tap `[ɾ]` or a vibrant trill `[r]`. Unlike English, which uses a smooth, retroflex approximant `[ɹ]`, Spanish **never** pulls the tongue backward without contact. At the start of words like **rojo**, it is always a strong alveolar trill.

## How to Pronounce It

Place the tip of your tongue loosely against your alveolar ridge, just behind your upper front teeth. Exhale firmly so the stream of air forces the tongue tip to vibrate rapidly against the ridge two or three times. Keep your tongue relaxed; excessive tension will prevent the trill from occurring.

## Spelling and Distribution

A single **r** is pronounced as a strong trill `[r]` at the beginning of a word or immediately following consonants like **n**, **l**, or **s** (e.g., *alrededor*). Between vowels, a single **r** is a quick tap `[ɾ]`, whereas the double letter **rr** is required to spell the full trill `[r]`.

## Crucial Distinctions

Distinguishing the tap `[ɾ]` from the trill `[r]` is vital because it changes meaning, as in *pero* (but) versus *perro* (dog). English speakers commonly substitute their native curled-back 'r' sound, which sounds noticeably foreign and indistinct in Spanish.
