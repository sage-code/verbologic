## The Sound of V

In European Spanish, the letter **V** is pronounced identically to the letter **B**, represented in phonetic transcription as either `[b]` or `[β]`. Unlike English, Spanish never uses a labiodental sound like the English 'v' in *vine*. Depending on its position, it is either a firm stop like English 'b' or a soft, continuous sound made by narrowing the lips.

## How to Produce It

Never place your top teeth against your lower lip, as you would in English. At the start of an utterance or after `m` and `n`, simply press both lips firmly together to make a clean `[b]`. Anywhere between vowels or after other sounds, bring both lips close together without touching, allowing the voiced air to slip smoothly through the gap to make `[β]`.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **V** occurs frequently throughout Spanish vocabulary, appearing at the beginning of words (*vino*) and between vowels (*avión*). In Spain, its name is **uve**, which distinguishes it clearly from the letter **B** (*be*). Because both letters share the exact same pronunciations, orthographic spelling bees and dictations require careful memorisation of roots and etymologies.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

The hard stop `[b]` appears after a pause or nasal, as in **verde** (`/ˈbeɾ.de/`), whereas the soft fricative `[β]` occurs in intervocalic contexts such as **uva** (`/ˈu.βa/`). The single biggest mistake English speakers make is producing a true English `[v]` with the teeth on the lower lip. Doing so sounds noticeably foreign; always remind yourself to use only your two lips.
