## Sound of the Letter

In European Spanish, the letter **Z** represents a voiceless dental fricative, transcribed as `[θ]`. It is virtually identical to the soft **th** sound in English words like *think* or *path*. It is never pronounced as an English buzzing **z** in standard Castilian Spanish.

## How to Pronounce It

Place the tip of your tongue lightly between your upper and lower teeth, or against the back of your top front teeth. Blow air gently through the gap without activating your vocal cords. Keep your lips relaxed and ensure no buzzing vibration occurs.

## Spelling Rules

The letter **Z** typically appears before the vowels **a**, **o**, and **u** (as in *zapato*, *zorro*, *zumo*), as well as at the very end of words (like *feliz*). Before **e** and **i**, the sound is almost always written with the letter **C** instead. When forming plurals, word-final **z** regularises to **c**, such as *pez* becoming *peces*.

## Contrasts and Common Mistakes

English speakers often default to a voiced /z/ sound like in *zebra*, which does not exist in standard Spanish and sounds foreign. Pay special attention to word-final positions like *luz* or *paz*, where learners often drop the sound or turn it into an **s**. Remember that in Spain, **z** and the soft **c** sound identical, contrasting clearly with the standard /s/.
