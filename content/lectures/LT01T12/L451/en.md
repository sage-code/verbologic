## The Sound of T

The Spanish **T** represents a voiceless dental plosive, transcribed as `[t̪]` or phonemically `/t/`. Unlike the English 't', which is alveolar and pronounced with a puff of air, the Spanish sound is strictly dental and completely **unaspirated**. It produces a much crisper, drier tap against the upper teeth.

## How to Say It

Place the tip of your tongue directly against the back of your upper front teeth, rather than the gum ridge above them. Build up slight air pressure behind the tongue, then release it cleanly without letting out a burst of breath. Keep your vocal cords completely still, ensuring the release is sharp and instantaneous.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **T** appears freely at the beginning and in the middle of words, as in **tiempo** or **gato**. Double 'tt' does not naturally exist in native Spanish words, appearing only in recent loanwords like *pizza* or *ballet*. It rarely ends native Spanish words, except in a handful of borrowings like *robot* or *zenit*.

## Comparing and Mistakes

English speakers frequently make the mistake of aspirating Spanish **T** at the start of words like **tío** or **tiempo**, making it sound like an English 't' with a hiss of breath. Another common error is placing the tongue too far back on the alveolar ridge, which softens the characteristic dental snap. Practice keeping the tongue firmly behind the top teeth to avoid this.
