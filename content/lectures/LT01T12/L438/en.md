## Silent in Native Words

In standard European Spanish, the letter **h** is completely silent (`/∅/`) in almost every native word. Unlike English, where 'h' represents a breathy sound `/h/`, Spanish **h** contributes no sound at all. Words like **hola** begin directly with the vowel sound `/o/`.

## Producing No Sound

Do not expel any air or constrict your throat when you see the letter **h**. Simply prepare your mouth, tongue, and lips for whichever vowel immediately follows it. Read the word as though the initial or internal letter did not exist at all.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **h** serves an etymological role, often tracing back to Latin 'f-' (such as **hijo** from Latin *filius*). It is commonly found before diphthongs like **hue-** (**huevo**) and **hie-** (**hierro**). Note that combined with **c**, it forms the distinct digraph **ch**, representing the sound `/tʃ/`.

## Pronunciation Nuances

The main trap for English speakers is trying to pronounce an audible 'h' in words like **hotel** or **hola**, which sounds unnatural in Spanish. The only rare exceptions are modern loanwords like **hámster** or foreign names, where it is pronounced like the Spanish 'j' (`/x/`). Always default to keeping it completely silent.
