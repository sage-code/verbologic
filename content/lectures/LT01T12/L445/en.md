## The Sound of Spanish Ñ

The letter **Ñ** represents the palatal nasal sound `/ɲ/`. It is very similar to the *canyon* sound in English or the *gn* in Italian and French. Unlike an ordinary `n` followed by a `y`, it is articulated as a single, smooth nasal consonant against the roof of the mouth.

## How to Produce /ɲ/

Press the flat middle body of your tongue firmly against the hard palate, right behind the gum ridge. Keep the tip of your tongue lowered behind your bottom front teeth. Let the air flow out entirely through your nose as your vocal cords vibrate, then release cleanly into the following vowel.

## Spelling Rules and Characteristics

The **Ñ** is an independent letter of the Spanish alphabet and has its own alphabetical section in dictionaries. It features a wavy diacritical mark called a *tilde* (`~`) above the base letter N. It never appears before consonants and occurs almost exclusively between vowels or at the beginning of words like *ñandú*.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Learners commonly split the sound into two separate segments, pronouncing words like *niño* as if they were written with an English `n` plus `y` (`/nj/`). This leaves the tip of the tongue touching the upper teeth ridge, which sounds unnatural. Ensure the contact is made entirely by the broad surface of the tongue against the palate.
