## The Sound of the Spanish 'U'

The letter **U** typically represents the close back rounded vowel `/u/`, similar to the 'oo' in English *moon*, but shorter, purer, and without any gliding diphthong. Unlike English, Spanish vowels are tense and stable regardless of whether they are stressed. In diphthongs next to another vowel, it reduces to the semivowel glide `/w/` as in *water*.

## How to Pronounce 'U'

Pull your tongue back towards the soft palate and raise the back of your tongue high in your mouth. Tightly round and protrude your lips forward into a small, tense circular shape. Keep the sound crisp and steady without letting your tongue or lips relax during pronunciation.

## Spelling Rules and Silent 'U'

The letter **U** is completely silent when placed between **g** or **q** and the vowels **e** or **i**, as in *queso* and *guerra*. If the 'u' needs to be pronounced in combinations with **g**, it carries a diaeresis (trema) as in *pingüino* or *vergüenza*. Additionally, an accent mark is used (*ú*) to break a diphthong into two separate syllables, as in *baúl*.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

English speakers often make the mistake of pronouncing **U** as a diphthong `/juː/` (like *cue*) or with the lax sound `/ʊ/` found in *book*. In Spanish, **U** is never preceded by an inserted 'y' sound unless explicitly written, so words like *Cuba* must start cleanly with `/ku/`, not `/kju/`. Remember also not to pronounce the 'u' in words like *quiero* or *guitarra*.
