## The Sound of the Letter W

In European Spanish, the letter **W** is not native and appears exclusively in foreign loanwords. In English borrowings like *web*, it is pronounced as the glide `[w]`, almost identical to the English 'w'. In German loanwords or proper names, however, it is traditionally adapted as the Spanish `[b]` or `[β]` sound.

## How to Pronounce W

For English loanwords, round your lips tightly into an 'oo' shape and glide smoothly into the following vowel without touching your teeth to your lips. For words of Germanic origin, treat the letter as a standard Spanish **B** or **V**, bringing both lips together rather than using your upper teeth. Ensure you do not add a strong hard 'g' sound before the glide.

## Spelling and Usage of W

The letter **W** is the least frequent consonant in Spanish orthography and exists solely in borrowed vocabulary. In Spain, it is officially named *uve doble*, though *doble uve* is also recognized. Capital **W** is commonly seen in foreign brand names, surnames, and technological acronyms.

## Comparing Pronunciations of W

Compare the English-style glide in *web* `[web]` with the Germanic bilabial stop in *wolframio* `[bolˈfɾa.mjo]`. A common mistake for native English speakers is attempting to pronounce German loanwords with a `[w]` sound instead of the naturalized Spanish `[b]`. Conversely, avoid replacing English-derived `[w]` with an overly harsh fricative like `[ɡw]`.
