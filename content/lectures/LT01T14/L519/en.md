## The Sound of Hungarian Ú

The letter **Ú** represents the long close back rounded vowel `/uː/`. It is nearly identical in quality to the vowel in the English word *flute* or *moon*, but it is held distinctly longer than the short Hungarian **u**. English speakers tend to diphthongize long back vowels, but Hungarian **ú** remains purely monophthongal and steady from start to finish.

## How to Pronounce Ú

Pull your tongue back and raise the back of it toward the soft palate, just as you do for a regular /u/. Round your lips tightly into a firm, puckered circle. Hold this exact tongue and lip posture while voicing for roughly twice the duration of a short **u**, ensuring that your lips and jaw do not move.

## Spelling and Distribution of Ú

The letter **Ú** is spelled with an acute accent over the Latin vowel *u* to signify its long duration. It can appear in all positions: word-initially (as in *új*), medially (as in *hús*), or word-finally (as in *bosszú*). The acute accent is phonemic in Hungarian, meaning omitting it often produces an entirely different word or a misspelling.

## Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

Be careful never to substitute the short vowel **/u/** for the long **/uː/**, as vowel length changes meaning in Hungarian. For instance, compare **húsz** (/ˈhuːs/, 'twenty') with **huszár** (/ˈhusaːr/, 'hussar'). Foreign speakers frequently shorten this sound in unstressed syllables; ensure you maintain its long, rounded quality wherever it appears in a word.
