## Sound of the Letter

The letter **G** represents the voiced velar plosive `[ɡ]`, identical to the hard 'g' in English words like *go* or *get*. Unlike in English, Hungarian **G** is never pronounced as an affricate like the 'g' in *gem* or *giant*. It retains this solid, voiced stop quality across almost every position.

## Pronunciation Guide

Raise the back of your tongue until it firmly seals against your soft palate (velum). Build up slight air pressure behind the closure while engaging your vocal cords to create vibration. Release the tongue cleanly to let the sound burst out without any added breathiness.

## Spelling and Distribution

Hungarian **G** appears frequently at the beginning, middle, and end of native words and loanwords. When doubled as **gg**, it denotes an elongated consonant sound `[ɡː]`, as in **reggel** ('morning'). It also combines with 'y' to form the distinct palatal letter **Gy**, which should not be confused with plain **G**.

## Contextual Variations and Common Pitfalls

Before voiceless consonants, such as in compound prefixes like **meg-** followed by a voiceless sound (e.g., **megkér**), **G** naturally assimilates and sounds like `[k]`. English speakers must be careful never to soften **G** into a 'j' sound before front vowels like *e* or *i*. In Hungarian, words like **gép** always start with a hard `[ɡ]`.
