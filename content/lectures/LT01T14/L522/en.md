## The Sound of V

The Hungarian letter **V** represents the voiced labiodental fricative `/v/`. It sounds virtually identical to the English **v** in *vine* or *river*. Hungarian `/v/` is unique in phonology because it can undergo voicing assimilation (becoming unvoiced before voiceless consonants in colloquial fast speech, though strictly standard Hungarian resists this), but it never causes preceding voiceless consonants to become voiced.

## How to Pronounce V

Lightly place your upper front teeth against your lower lip, just as you do for English **v**. Gently blow air through the narrow gap between your teeth and lip while vibrating your vocal cords. Ensure your lips do not round or protrude, avoiding an English **w** sound.

## Spelling and Usage

The letter **V** is written as **V** uppercase and **v** lowercase. It appears freely at the beginning, middle, and end of words. Double **vv** indicates a lengthened consonant, as seen in words like *orvosi* versus *hívva* (`/ˈhiːvvɒ/`).

## Comparisons and Pitfalls

Native speakers of languages like German must remember that Hungarian **v** is always voiced `/v/`, never unvoiced `/f/` like German *Vogel*. Slavic or Spanish speakers must take care not to confuse `/v/` with a bilabial approximant or `/b/`. Always maintain clear contact between your upper teeth and lower lip to keep the sound crisp.
