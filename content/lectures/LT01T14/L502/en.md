## The Sound of Hungarian L

The Hungarian letter **L** represents an alveolar lateral approximant `/l/`. Unlike the dark or velarised English 'l' heard in words like *ball* or *milk*, Hungarian **L** is always 'clear' and bright across all positions in a word. It closely matches the light 'l' sound heard at the beginning of English words such as *light* or *lemon*.

## How to Pronounce L

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth. Let the air flow smoothly around both sides of your tongue without adding any friction or throat tension. Keep the back of your tongue low and relaxed so that the sound never becomes dark or muffled.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **L** can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of words. When doubled as **LL**, it represents a geminate or lengthened consonant `/lː/`, as in *toll* (pen) or *áll* (to stand). In hyphenated compound words where three identical consonants would meet, the cluster is written out with a hyphen, such as *toll-léc*.

## Common Traps and Comparisons

The most common mistake English speakers make is replacing word-final or preconsonantal Hungarian **L** with a dark, hollow [ɫ], pronouncing words like *bál* or *zöld* incorrectly. Always keep the sound forward and crisp, maintaining the exact same clear quality whether the letter starts a syllable or ends it. Be careful also to hold double **ll** for roughly twice as long as a single **l**.
