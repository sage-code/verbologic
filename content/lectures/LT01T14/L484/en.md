## The Sound of the Letter

The Hungarian letter **A** represents an open back rounded vowel, transcribed as `/ɒ/`. It sounds very close to the vowel in British English **lot** or **thought**, but it is distinctively rounded and slightly deeper than the standard American open vowel. Unlike in English, this vowel is always short and never shifts into a schwa in unstressed syllables.

## How to Pronounce It

Drop your jaw wide as if you are going to say a standard 'ah', but pull your tongue down and slightly back into your throat. At the same time, slightly round your lips into a loose oval shape rather than leaving them spread. Keep the vocal tract open and produce a short, resonant sound without gliding into any other vowel.

## Spelling and Rules

The letter **A** appears in all word positions, including word-initially, medially, and very commonly as an inflectional ending in word-final position. When vowel-lengthening suffixes are added to words ending in **a**, it regularly changes into its long counterpart **á** (for example, *alma* becomes *almát*). It is always written as a plain Latin **A** or **a** without any diacritics.

## Common Pitfalls

The most common mistake English speakers make is pronouncing **a** as an unrounded `/ɑː/` or `/a/` like the *a* in *father*, or substituting a neutral schwa when the syllable is unstressed. Be careful never to confuse short **a** (`/ɒ/`) with long **á** (`/aː/`), as they differ not only in duration but completely in vowel quality. Words like *hat* ('six') and *hát* ('back/well') are entirely different words solely because of this contrast.
