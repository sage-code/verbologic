## The Sound of Ű

The letter **Ű** represents a long, close front rounded vowel, transcribed as `/yː/`. It sounds exactly like a prolonged French *u* in *lune* or German *ü* in *Tür*. English lacks this specific vowel, so learners must avoid substituting standard English sounds like `/uː/` (as in *moon*) or `/juː/` (as in *few*).

## How to Pronounce Ű

Start by producing a continuous long **ee** sound as in *see*, keeping your tongue pushed high and forward against your lower teeth. Without shifting your tongue's position at all, tightly round your lips into a small, tense 'O' circle. Hold this rounded vowel sound steady for roughly twice as long as the short **Ü**.

## Spelling and Length Mark

The letter **Ű** is uniquely Hungarian, distinguished by its double acute accent (*hosszú hungaricum*), which signifies that the vowel is strictly long. The lowercase form is **ű**. It contrasts directly with **ü**, which bears a regular umlaut to show a short duration.

## Crucial Contrasts

Ensure you hold **ű** significantly longer than short **ü**, as duration alone can change the meaning of words. For example, compare **tűz** (`/tyːz/`, meaning 'fire') with **tüzet** (`/ˈtyzɛt/`, 'fire' in the accusative case), or **öt** ('five') versus **őt** ('him/her'). A common mistake for native English speakers is relaxing the rounded lips, which creates a diphthong like /juː/; keep the lips firmly rounded and static.
