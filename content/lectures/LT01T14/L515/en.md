## Sound of Sz

The digraph **sz** represents the voiceless alveolar fricative `[s]`, precisely matching the English *s* sound in *sun* or *bus*. Learners are often confused by Hungarian orthography because a plain letter *s* represents an *sh* sound `[ʃ]`, whereas **sz** is simply *s*. It remains crisp and unvoiced in nearly all contexts.

## How to Say Sz

Position the tip of your tongue close to the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth without touching it. Keep your teeth close together and blow a steady stream of air through the central groove of the tongue. Do not engage your vocal cords; the resulting sound should be a sharp, clear hiss.

## Spelling and Rules

Hungarian treats **sz** as a single distinct letter of the alphabet. At the beginning of a sentence or a proper noun, only the first letter is capitalised (**Sz**), while fully uppercase text uses **SZ**. When doubled for a long sound, it is spelled **ssz** (as in *hosszú*), rather than repeating both letters.

## Comparing and Mistakes

The most frequent error English speakers make is misreading **sz** as a *z* sound or confusing it with plain Hungarian *s* (`[ʃ]`). Remember the inverse rule: Hungarian **s** is *sh*, while **sz** is *s*. Additionally, before voiced consonants like *b*, *d*, or *g*, **sz** undergoes voice assimilation and is pronounced as `[z]`, as seen in *részben*.
