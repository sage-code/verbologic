## The Sound of Q

The letter **Q** is not part of the traditional native Hungarian alphabet, but it is officially included in the extended 44-letter alphabet for foreign loanwords and proper names. It is almost exclusively found in the sequence **qu**, which is pronounced exactly like `kv` in Hungarian (the sound `/k/` followed by `/v/`). Unlike in English, the letter **u** that follows does not form a `w` glide, but a clear Hungarian `v` sound.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce the **qu** combination, raise the back of your tongue against the soft palate to make a crisp, unaspirated Hungarian `k`. Immediately release it into a voiced labiodental fricative `v` by bringing your upper teeth against your lower lip while vibrating your vocal cords. Ensure you do not round your lips into an English `w` sound.

## Spelling and Usage

**Q** only appears in Latin loans, historical family names, and foreign terminology such as **Aquincum** or **quasi**. In fully integrated loanwords, Hungarian orthography almost always adapts the spelling to **kv**, as seen in **kvartett** (quartet) or **kvíz** (quiz). You will rarely encounter **q** outside of formal or specialized contexts.

## Comparing and Pitfalls

The most common mistake English speakers make is pronouncing **qu** with an English `/kw/` diphthong or glide. In Hungarian, the sequence represents a strict `kv` where the second consonant is a sharp labiodental `/v/`. Compare borrowed spellings like **quasi** with native adaptations like **kvaterkázik** to hear the identical consonant cluster.
