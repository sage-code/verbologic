## The Sound of Ny

The digraph **Ny** represents a voiced palatal nasal consonant, transcribed as `[ɲ]`. It sounds virtually identical to the Spanish *ñ* in *señor*, the French/Italian *gn* in *oignon* or *gnocchi*, or roughly like the *ny* in English *canyon*. Unlike English *ny*, it is produced as a single merged palatal sound rather than an alveolar `[n]` followed by a distinct `[j]`.

## How to Pronounce Ny

Press the broad flat body of your tongue firmly against your hard palate, just behind the alveolar ridge. The tip of your tongue should rest gently behind your lower front teeth or lightly touch the gumline without bearing pressure. Lower your soft palate to let the air stream freely out through your nose while your vocal cords vibrate.

## Spelling and Rules

**Ny** is treated as a single letter in the Hungarian alphabet and can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of words. When capitalised at the start of a sentence or proper noun, only the first element is uppercase (**Ny**), but in full acronyms or all-caps text, both letters become uppercase (**NY**). When doubled, it is written as **nny** (e.g. *mennyi*), which represents a long `[ɲː]`.

## Common Pitfalls

Native English speakers often mistakenly pronounce **ny** as two distinct sounds, `[n]` plus `[j]`, as in *ten years*. Ensure that your tongue body makes broad contact with the roof of the mouth in one seamless gesture. Be careful not to confuse **ny** with plain **n**, as pairs like *bán* ('to regret') and *bány* ('mine', archaic) or *anya* ('mother') and *Anna* have completely distinct meanings.
