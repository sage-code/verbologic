## The Sound of the Letter T

The letter **T** represents a voiceless dental stop, transcribed as `/t/`. Unlike in English, it is pronounced with the tip of the tongue touching the upper front teeth rather than the alveolar ridge behind them. Most importantly, it is **unaspirated**, meaning there is no puff of breath released when saying it, sounding much like the 't' in the English word *stop*.

## How to Pronounce T

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front teeth to completely block the airflow. Build up a small amount of air pressure behind the closure, then release the tongue sharply without breathing out extra air. Ensure your vocal cords do not vibrate during the initial contact.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter is written as **T** in uppercase and **t** in lowercase. In Hungarian, it can appear anywhere in a word, including initially, internally, and finally. When doubled as **tt**, it represents a lengthened or geminate sound `/tː/`, which must be held distinctly longer than a single **t**.

## Sound Variations and Common Pitfalls

While usually pronounced as a clean `/t/`, it assimilates to a voiced `/d/` before voiced consonants, as heard in **hatvan** (`/ˈhɒdvɒn/`). English speakers frequently make the error of adding aspiration at the start of words like **tél**, which sounds unnatural to Hungarian native speakers. Be careful to keep the release crisp, clean, and strictly unaspirated.
