## The Sound of Hungarian D

The Hungarian letter **D** represents the voiced dental plosive `/d/`. Unlike English `/d/`, which is pronounced with the tongue tip against the alveolar ridge behind the teeth, Hungarian `/d/` is strictly dental, with the tongue tip making direct contact with the back of the upper front teeth. It is fully voiced and never followed by a puff of air (aspiration).

## How to Pronounce D

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the back of your upper front teeth rather than the gum ridge. Build up a brief burst of air pressure behind the seal while vibrating your vocal cords. Release the tongue cleanly to let the sound pop out smoothly without any breathy friction.

## Spelling Rules and Behavior

The letter **D** occurs freely at the beginning, middle, and end of words. When doubled as **dd**, it denotes a long consonant `/dː/`, as in *adni* vs. *add*. Note that unlike German or Russian, Hungarian does not undergo word-final devoicing; a final **d** remains voiced unless followed by a voiceless consonant in the next word.

## Sound Variations and Common Pitfalls

Before voiceless consonants, **d** undergoes regressive voicing assimilation and turns into `[t]`, as heard in *adta* `[ˈɒttɒ]`. English speakers should avoid producing an alveolar sound or aspirating it at the beginning of words. Do not confuse plain **d** with the digraph **gy** `/ɟ/`, which is palatal and has a much softer, flatter tongue position.
