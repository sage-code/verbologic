## The Sound of the Letter X

The letter **X** is not native to Hungarian and is used exclusively in loanwords, representing the consonant cluster `/ks/`. Unlike in English, where an initial *x* often softens to `/z/` (as in *xylophone*), Hungarian pronounces an initial **x** crisply as `/ks/` (sounding like *ksilofon*). In intervocalic positions or through voice assimilation, it occasionally surfaces as `/gz/`.

## How to Pronounce It

To articulate this letter, produce a sharp, voiceless velar stop `/k/` by pressing the back of the tongue against the soft palate, then immediately release the air into an unvoiced alveolar sibilant `/s/`. Keep your lips relaxed and slightly spread. Do not let vocal fold vibration sneak in at the beginning of words, even if your native tongue normally voices initial *x*.

## Spelling and Usage

The letter **X** appears primarily in classical Greco-Latin borrowings and modern internationalisms such as **taxi** or **oxigén**. In purely integrated or assimilated vocabulary, Hungarian often spells out the cluster phonetically as **ksz** or **gz** (for example, *szex* vs. *egzotikus*). Both lowercase **x** and uppercase **X** follow standard Latin script forms.

## Variants and Common Mistakes

English speakers often make the error of pronouncing words like *xilofon* with an English `/z/` sound; in Hungarian, it must start with a distinct `/ks/`. Additionally, ensure you do not replace the crisp alveolar `/s/` with the postalveolar Hungarian *sz* vs *s* sound; it should always be the whistling `/s/` (as in English *sit*), never Hungarian *s* (`/ʃ/`). In voiced contexts, as in loanwords related to *egzakt*, native assimilation may shift the sound naturally to `/gz/`.
