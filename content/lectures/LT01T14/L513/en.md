## The Sound of R

Hungarian **R** represents an alveolar trill or tap, transcribed as `/r/`. Unlike the English smooth retroflex approximant or the French/German uvular throat sound, Hungarian **R** is produced by rolling the tip of the tongue against the gum ridge behind the upper teeth. In everyday speech it often consists of a single brisk tap, while double **RR** is distinctly prolonged with multiple vibrations.

## How to Pronounce R

Place the tip of your tongue loosely against the alveolar ridge, right above your upper front teeth. Exhale a steady stream of air across the tip so that it flutters or taps briskly against the ridge. Keep the back and root of your tongue relaxed so you do not accidentally produce a throat sound.

## Spelling and Usage

The letter **R** appears in uppercase as **R** and lowercase as **r**. It is extremely common in Hungarian and can occur at the beginning, middle, or end of words. When geminated as **rr** (as in *forró*), the tongue trills for a longer duration with multiple audible vibrations.

## Tips and Common Pitfalls

The most common mistake for English speakers is using the English postalveolar approximant `[ɹ]`, which sounds very muffled and foreign in Hungarian. German and French speakers must also avoid using the uvular trill or fricative `[ʁ]`. Aim for an Italian- or Spanish-style rolled or tapped `/r/` using strictly the tongue tip.
