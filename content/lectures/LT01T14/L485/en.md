## The Sound of Á

The letter **á** represents an open central unrounded long vowel, transcribed as `/aː/`. It sounds very similar to the vowel in British English **father** or American English **spa**, but held for roughly twice as long. Unlike standard Hungarian **a**, which is short and rounded (`/ɒ/`), **á** is fully unrounded and distinctly brighter.

## How to Pronounce Á

Open your mouth comfortably wide and keep your tongue low and flat on the floor of the mouth. Ensure your lips remain relaxed and completely **unrounded**, with the corners slightly apart. Produce a clear, steady voice sound from the chest, maintaining the vowel quality steadily without letting it glide into a diphthong.

## Spelling and Distribution

Written as an **a** with an acute accent (**á**, capital **Á**), this letter occurs in all positions in Hungarian words: initial, medial, and final. The accent mark is not merely a stress indicator, but fundamentally changes the vowel's identity and length. In inflectional endings like the possessive or accusative, a base **a** often shifts to an **á** before suffixes (e.g. *fa* becoming *fát*).

## Comparing and Common Pitfalls

The most critical error for learners is confusing **á** (`/aː/`) with short **a** (`/ɒ/`). Failing to differentiate them completely changes word meanings, as in *hat* ('six') versus *hát* ('back'). English speakers must also be careful to hold the sound as a pure monophthong, resisting the urge to turn it into an off-glide diphthong like /aʊ/ or /aɪ/.
