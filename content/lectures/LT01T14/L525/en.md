## Sound of Y

The letter **Y** (*ipszilon*) is not traditionally used on its own for native vocabulary, appearing mainly in foreign loanwords and historical family names. In loanwords like *yacht*, it represents the palatal glide `/j/`, identical to the English **y** in *yes*. In historical surnames, it typically represents the vowel sound `/i/`.

## How to say it

To produce the `/j/` sound heard in modern loanwords, raise the middle body of your tongue close to the hard palate. Keep your vocal cords vibrating while allowing air to glide smoothly over the tongue without friction. Your lips should be relaxed and spread, matching the vowel that follows immediately.

## Spelling and usage

In modern Hungarian orthography, **Y** is used almost exclusively in foreign borrowings, brand names, and traditional aristocratic family names. It also serves as a crucial component of native digraphs like **gy**, **ly**, **ny**, and **ty**, where it historically indicated palatalization rather than a distinct independent letter sound. When standing alone in an old surname such as *Báthory*, it is pronounced as a simple short Hungarian *i*.

## Pronunciation nuances

Learners often confuse whether to pronounce a lone **Y** as a consonant or a vowel. Treat it as `/j/` in loanwords like *yoga* or *yacht*, but switch to the vowel `/i/` at the end of traditional Hungarian surnames like *Vörösmarty*. Avoid pronouncing it as a rounded vowel like French *u* or German *ü*, which does not occur for this letter in Hungarian.
