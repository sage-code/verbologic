## The Sound of Í

The Hungarian letter **Í** represents a long close front unrounded vowel, transcribed as `/iː/`. It sounds very similar to the "ee" in English *see* or *fleece*, but it is held distinctly longer and remains pure throughout its duration. Unlike in English, Hungarian vowels never glide into a diphthong, so your tongue must stay completely stationary.

## How to Pronounce Í

To make this sound, raise the front of your tongue high towards the hard palate, keeping the tip resting just behind your lower front teeth. Spread your lips wide in an unrounded, smiling posture. Hold the vocal sound steady for roughly double the length of the short Hungarian **I**, without letting your pitch or tongue position drift.

## Spelling and Distribution

Written as **Í** in uppercase and **í** in lowercase, this letter carries an acute accent to mark its long vowel length. It can appear anywhere in a word: initially as in *így* ('thus'), medially as in *víz* ('water'), or finally as in *sí* ('ski'). It is an independent letter in the Hungarian alphabet and must never be written as plain **I**.

## Contrasting Sounds and Common Mistakes

The most critical contrast is between long **Í** `/iː/` and short **I** `/i/`, which creates distinct word pairs like *irt* ('to eradicate') versus *írt* ('wrote'). Many learners tend to shorten **Í** in unstressed syllables or diphthongize it into an English-style glide. Make sure you actively sustain the vowel duration, as shortening it can confuse native listeners or alter the grammatical meaning.
