## The Sound of Ő

The letter **Ő** represents a long, close-mid front rounded vowel, written as `/øː/` in IPA. It is the long counterpart of the short Hungarian **Ö** (`/ø/`), similar to the vowel in French *deux* or German *schön*, but sustained longer. English lacks this sound entirely; it sounds somewhat like the vowel in *bird*, but pronounced with tensely rounded lips and no rhotic coloring.

## How to Pronounce Ő

Shape your tongue as if you were pronouncing a long 'ay' sound as in *say* (`/eː/`). Without moving your tongue, round your lips firmly into a tight, pursed circle as if you were going to whistle. Hold this sound steady and long, ensuring your tongue stays high and fronted without gliding into an 'ee' or 'oo' sound.

## Spelling and Length Markings

In Hungarian orthography, the double acute accent (hungarumlaut) marks a vowel as both rounded and long. **Ő** contrasts strictly with **Ö**; failing to write or pronounce the length properly can change the meaning of a word completely. It can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of words, such as in **ő** ('he/she') or **mező** ('field').

## Contrasts and Common Pitfalls

The most critical distinction is between short **Ö** (`/ø/`) and long **Ő** (`/øː/`), as seen in pairs like **öt** ('five') and **őt** ('him/her'). English speakers frequently make the mistake of reducing it to an unrounded central vowel like the 'er' in *butter* or turning it into a diphthong. Keep the lips tensely pursed and extend the duration so that native speakers do not hear the short vowel instead.
