## The Sound of H

Hungarian **H** represents the voiceless glottal fricative `/h/`, very similar to the English *h* in *house*. Unlike in French or Spanish, it is never automatically silent at the beginning of words. Between voiced sounds it often becomes voiced `[ɦ]`, and in a few words word-finally it surfaces as a raspy velar fricative `[x]` or is completely silent.

## How to Pronounce H

Open your mouth slightly and keep your tongue resting in a neutral position on the floor of the mouth. Exhale gently through your open vocal cords without engaging your voice, producing an audible puff of air. Make sure the friction comes from the throat rather than the tongue touching the palate.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter is written as **H** in uppercase and **h** in lowercase. It appears initially, medially, and occasionally finally. When doubled as **hh**, it represents an intensified or geminate fricative sound, found mostly in loanwords or compound boundaries like *méhhas*.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers often drop **h** in connected speech, but Hungarian requires a crisp `/h/` initially and medially (e.g., *ház* `/haːz/`, *haza* `/hɒzɒ/`). A critical trap is word-final position: some words drop the **h** entirely (*cseh* is pronounced `/t͡ʃɛ/`), while others retain a rough German-style `[x]` sound (*düh* is `/dyx/`). Do not pronounce silent final **h** unless dictionary norms specifically require `[x]`.
