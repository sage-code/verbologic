## The Sound of Dz

The Hungarian letter **dz** represents an alveolar voiced affricate `/d͡z/`, similar to the sound of *ds* in English *kids* or *cards*, but pronounced as a single unified consonant. When followed by **s** to form the trigraph **dzs**, it sounds instead like the English *j* in *jump* (`/d͡ʒ/`). Unlike in English where `/d͡z/` only appears at word boundaries, in Hungarian it often appears medially and can be pronounced either short or geminated.

## How to Say It

Place the tip of your tongue against your alveolar ridge just behind the upper front teeth, exactly as you would for a `/d/`. Build up slight breath pressure, then release the tongue smoothly into a voiced `/z/` hiss while keeping your vocal cords vibrating. Ensure the transition is instantaneous rather than two distinct consonants separated by air.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **dz** is a digraph treated as an independent letter in the Hungarian alphabet, occurring mainly in native words and naturalised loanwords. When lengthened in writing, only the first element is doubled as **ddz** (e.g., *edz* becomes *eddz* in the imperative). Note that the trigraph **dzs** also begins with this sequence, though it denotes a completely different sound (`/d͡ʒ/`).

## Comparing Sounds

Do not confuse the affricate **dz** (`/d͡z/`) with a simple voiced **z** (`/z/`) or the voiceless affricate **c** (`/t͡s/`). For instance, English speakers often accidentally drop the initial stop element, turning words like *bodza* ('elderberry') into an incorrect *boza*. Always maintain vocal fold vibration so that it does not sound like the unvoiced **c** in *cica*.
