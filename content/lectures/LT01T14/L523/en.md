## Sound of the Letter

The letter **W** is not part of the traditional native Hungarian alphabet and appears strictly in foreign loanwords, acronyms, and historical family names. In almost every case, it is pronounced identically to the Hungarian letter **V**, sounding like the voiced labiodental fricative `/v/`. Unlike in English, where it produces a smooth glide `/w/`, Hungarian speakers naturally replace this with a standard English 'v' sound.

## Pronunciation Guide

Place your upper teeth gently against the inner edge of your lower lip, just as you do for the English 'v'. Exhale steadily while activating your vocal cords to produce a continuous vibration. Do not round your lips into a circle as you would for an English 'w'; keep the dental-to-lip contact firm and buzzy.

## Spelling and Usage

In contemporary Hungarian, **W** is treated as an 'extended' alphabet letter and alphabetised alongside **V**. Fully assimilated everyday borrowings typically swap it out for standard **v**, as seen in *vattacukor*. However, it is preserved in international terms like **WC**, SI units like *watt*, and aristocratic historic surnames such as *Wesselényi*.

## Nuances and Pitfalls

The most frequent error for English speakers is pronouncing **W** as a rounded glide `/w/`. Even in English borrowings like *whisky* or *western*, Hungarians predominantly say `/ˈviski/` and `/ˈvɛstɛrn/`. Remember that the letter's Hungarian name is *dupla vé* (double V), which directly reinforces its standard `/v/` pronunciation.
