## The Sound of B

The Hungarian letter **B** represents a voiced bilabial plosive, identical to the standard English `[b]` sound in words like *baby* or *boat*. Unlike English, Hungarian **B** maintains strong voicing throughout its articulation, even at the beginning or very end of a word. However, when it directly precedes a voiceless consonant, regressive assimilation causes it to be pronounced as an unvoiced `[p]`.

## How to Produce B

Press your upper and lower lips firmly together to completely block the airflow from your lungs. Build up slight air pressure behind your lips while activating your vocal cords to create voicing. Then, release your lips quickly so the trapped air bursts out cleanly.

## Spelling and Gemination

Hungarian **B** appears in all word positions: word-initial, medial, and final. When prolonged or doubled, it is spelled **bb** (such as in *jobb* meaning 'better'), which indicates that you must hold the lip closure for twice as long before releasing it. In compound words, a double **b** can also arise naturally across morpheme boundaries.

## Variations and Common Pitfalls

The standard pronunciation is `[b]`, as in *baba*, but remember that regressive assimilation turns it into `[p]` before voiceless consonants, as heard in *dobtam* (`/ˈdoptɒm/`). A common pitfall for English speakers is under-voicing initial or final `[b]`, making it sound too close to `[p]`. Always ensure the vocal cords vibrate actively from the very start of the lip closure.
