## The Sound of Hungarian Ty

The Hungarian letter **Ty** represents a voiceless palatal plosive, transcribed in IPA as `[c]`. It sounds somewhat like the *t* in English *tune* or the *c* in *cue*, but pronounced as a single, neat sound rather than two separate elements. Unlike the English /tʃ/ sound in *chair*, **Ty** is a true stop consonant articulated flat against the hard palate.

## How to Pronounce Ty

Place the flat body of your tongue firmly against the hard roof of your mouth, just behind the alveolar ridge. Keep the tip of your tongue pressed down behind your lower front teeth to prevent turning it into an English *ch* sound. Build up slight air pressure and release it cleanly without a noisy whistling or friction.

## Spelling and Rules

**Ty** is treated as a single letter in the Hungarian alphabet and alphabetised under its own heading. When capitalised at the beginning of a word or sentence, only the initial consonant is uppercase (**Ty**), while fully capitalised words write both letters as capitals (**TY**). When doubled, it is contracted to **tty**, as seen in words like *hattyú* (swan).

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Learners frequently substitute the English /tʃ/ (as in *chin*) or an explicit /tj/ sequence (as in *stew*) for Hungarian **Ty**. Notice how *tyúk* differs sharply from *csuk* or a two-step *t-yúk*. Practice keeping the airflow completely stopped and releasing smoothly without buzzing or hissing.
