## The sound of Ó

The letter **Ó** represents a long, close-mid back rounded vowel `[oː]`. It sounds very similar to the vowel in British English *law* spoken with rounded lips, or more accurately like a prolonged French *eau* or German *Boot*. Unlike in English, this vowel is a pure, stable monophthong and must never glide into a `[w]` sound.

## How to pronounce Ó

Round your lips firmly into an 'o'-shape and pull the body of your tongue back toward the soft palate. Keep your jaw relatively closed, higher than for English *hot*. Maintain the position steadily for roughly twice as long as the short Hungarian **O** without moving your lips or tongue at the end.

## Spelling rules for Ó

The acute accent mark (*hosszú ékezet*) always indicates length in Hungarian vowels. **Ó** can appear at the beginning, in the middle, or at the end of words, frequently functioning as a distinct word on its own (meaning *ancient*). In uppercase, the accent must be maintained as **Ó**.

## Comparing Ó with similar sounds

The most critical distinction is between short **o** and long **ó**, which changes word meanings entirely, as in *kor* (age) versus *kór* (disease). English speakers commonly make the mistake of turning **ó** into a diphthong `[oʊ]`; ensure your lips remain locked in a static position until phonation ends completely.
