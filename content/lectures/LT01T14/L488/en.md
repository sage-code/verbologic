## The Sound of Cs

The digraph **Cs** represents the voiceless postalveolar affricate `[tʃ]`. It sounds essentially identical to the English **ch** in words like *cheese* or *match*. Unlike some Slavic languages, Hungarian has only this single voiceless postalveolar affricate, making it straightforward for English speakers.

## How to Pronounce Cs

Place the tip and blade of your tongue against the back of your alveolar ridge, just behind your upper front teeth. Build up slight air pressure as if starting to make a `[t]`, then release it into a narrow channel to produce a sharp `[ʃ]` (sh-sound) all in one fluid movement. Keep your vocal cords completely still, as this sound is fully voiceless.

## Spelling Rules for Cs

**Cs** is treated as a distinct single letter in the Hungarian alphabet and alphabetised under its own heading. When geminated (lengthened), it is abbreviated as **ccs** rather than written out twice (for example, *öccse*). At the beginning of sentences or proper nouns, only the first letter is capitalised (**Cs**), while in all-caps text, both letters are capitalised (**CS**).

## Nuances and Common Pitfalls

Hungarian **cs** always maintains its crisp affricate sound `[tʃ]` and must never soften into a simple fricative like `[ʃ]` (the Hungarian **s**). English speakers often struggle with the geminated form **ccs**, where the closure must be held roughly twice as long before the friction release (as in *loccsan*). Be careful not to confuse **cs** `[tʃ]` with **c** `[ts]`, which corresponds to the *ts* sound in *cats*.
