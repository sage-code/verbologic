## The Sound of Z

The letter **Z** in Hungarian represents the voiced alveolar fricative `/z/`, which is identical to the English 'z' in *zebra* or *buzz*. Unlike in German or Italian, where this letter often represents an affricate like `ts`, Hungarian **Z** is always a continuous, smooth buzzing sound. However, when it stands immediately before a voiceless consonant, it undergoes regressive voicing assimilation and becomes a voiceless `/s/`.

## How to Pronounce Z

Place the tip of your tongue very close to the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth, leaving a tiny gap for air to pass. Keep your teeth close together and blow air through the channel while vibrating your vocal cords. If your throat does not vibrate, you are producing an 's' sound instead of a proper voiced **Z**.

## Spelling and Positions

**Z** can appear in all word positions: word-initially as in *zene* (music), medially as in *búza* (wheat), and word-finally as in *méz* (honey). It can be doubled as **ZZ** to represent a geminate long consonant `/zː/`, as heard in *izzad* (to sweat). It also forms digraphs such as **SZ** (`/s/`) and **ZS** (`/ʒ/`), which must be treated as completely separate single letters.

## Variants and Pitfalls

The primary variant occurs before voiceless consonants like *t*, *k*, or *h*, where `/z/` turns into `/s/`, such as in *húzta* (`/ˈhuːstɒ/`). German speakers must be especially careful not to pronounce **Z** as `/ts/`, which is written as **C** in Hungarian. Additionally, take care never to confuse single **Z** (`/z/`) with the digraph **SZ** (`/s/`), as swapping them changes word meanings entirely.
