## Sound of the Letter

The letter **N** generally represents the alveolar nasal `[n]`, very similar to the English 'n' in *net*. When it occurs immediately before the velar consonants `[k]` or `[ɡ]`, it automatically assimilates into the velar nasal `[ŋ]`, exactly like the sound in English *sing* or *bank*. Unlike English, Hungarian consonants do not soften or reduce in unstressed positions.

## How to Pronounce It

For the standard sound `[n]`, press the tip of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth while lowering your soft palate so air flows freely through your nose. For the variant before `k` or `g`, raise the back of your tongue against the soft palate instead, allowing the nasal resonance to shift backward. Keep your vocal cords vibrating throughout the sound.

## Spelling and Distribution

Written as uppercase **N** and lowercase **n**, it appears in all word positions including beginnings, middles, and word-finally. A doubled **nn** represents a phonemically distinct long consonant `[nː]`, which must be held for roughly twice the duration of a single 'n'. Be careful not to confuse **n** with the digraph **ny**, which represents an entirely distinct palatal sound.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Listen to the contrast between the standard dental/alveolar sound in *nap* (`/ˈnɒp/`) and the assimilated velar sound in *bank* (`/ˈbɒŋk/`). A frequent mistake for learners is failing to hold geminate consonants, turning words like *font* (pound) into a confusion with *fonn* (to spin). Additionally, ensure you do not add a palatal 'y-glide' after plain **n** before front vowels like *e* or *i*.
