## The Sound of M

The Hungarian letter **M** represents the voiced bilabial nasal sound `/m/`. It sounds exactly like the English **m** in *mother* or *summer*. It is a voiced nasal consonant that remains completely consistent across almost every phonetic environment in Hungarian.

## How to Pronounce It

Close both lips firmly together while keeping your soft palate lowered so that airflow escapes freely through your nose. Engage your vocal cords to create voice as the air resonates through the nasal cavity. When opening your lips to release into the next vowel, do so smoothly without adding any puff of air.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter is written as **M** (uppercase) and **m** (lowercase). When doubled as **mm**, it indicates a long consonant sound `[mː]`, as seen in *semmi* ('nothing') or *elem* versus *ellemmel*. Unlike in English where double letters usually just affect preceding vowels, in Hungarian **mm** must be distinctly held for roughly twice as long as a single **m**.

## Accents and Pitfalls

Native speakers of English will find the basic single `/m/` completely straightforward. The primary pitfall is ignoring length distinctions: failing to hold double **mm** long enough can sound unnatural or obscure word boundaries. Additionally, when followed by labiodental sounds like /f/ or /v/, it naturally assimilates into the labiodental nasal `[ɱ]` (as in *hamvas*), which happens instinctively.
