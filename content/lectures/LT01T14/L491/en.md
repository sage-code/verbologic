## Sound

The digraph **dzs** represents the voiced postalveolar affricate `[d͡ʒ]`. It sounds virtually identical to the English **j** in *jam* or the **g** in *gem*. While it is an official letter of the Hungarian alphabet, it appears almost exclusively in loanwords.

## How to say it

Place the tip and blade of your tongue against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper teeth, exactly as if preparing to say `[d]`. Release the air with friction through rounded lips to produce a voiced `[ʒ]` sound, gliding smoothly between the two elements. Keep your vocal cords vibrating throughout the sound.

## Spelling

Although written with three characters as a trigraph, **dzs** functions as a single consonant unit. When capitalized at the beginning of a sentence, only the first letter is capitalized (**Dzs**), but in full acronyms or all-caps text, all three are capitalized (**DZS**). When doubled in writing, it is shortened to **ddzs** rather than repeated fully.

## Compare

Do not confuse **dzs** with the unvoiced sound **cs** `[t͡ʃ]`, which sounds like the *ch* in *chip*. English speakers usually find this sound very natural, but beware of devoicing it at the end of words or before voiceless consonants. Compare *dzsem* `[ˈd͡ʒɛm]` (jam) with *csempe* `[ˈt͡ʃɛmpɛ]` (tile).
