## The Sound of I

The letter **I** represents a short close front unrounded vowel `/i/`. Unlike English short *i* in *bit* (which is lax `[ɪ]`), Hungarian **I** is a pure, tense sound very similar to the *ee* in *beet*, but held for a much shorter duration. Avoid centralizing or relaxing it into a lax vowel.

## How to Pronounce I

Spread your lips slightly into a relaxed smile and raise the front of your tongue high up toward your hard palate. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and your vocal tract tense without letting the tongue drop. Produce a clean, brisk sound without gliding into any other vowel.

## Spelling and Distribution

Hungarian **I** occurs freely at the beginning, middle, and end of words. It is strictly short; its long counterpart is written with an acute accent as **Í** (`/iː/`). Keep in mind that vowel length distinguishes entirely different words in Hungarian, so do not stretch a plain **i**.

## Comparing and Common Mistakes

English speakers often substitute the lax vowel `/ɪ/` as in *sit*, which sounds foreign and muffled to native Hungarian ears. Another common error is confusing short **i** with long **í**, as in pairs like *irt* (extirpates) versus *írt* (wrote). Always keep short **i** crisp, tense, and brief.
