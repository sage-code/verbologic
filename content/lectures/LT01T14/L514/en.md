## The Sound of S

In Hungarian, the single letter **s** always represents the sound `/ʃ/`, exactly like the **sh** in English *shoe* or *fish*. It never sounds like the English /s/ sound; learners frequently confuse this with the letter **sz**, which actually produces /s/. Additionally, before voiced consonants like *b*, *d*, or *g*, it assimilates and is pronounced voiced as `/ʒ/` (like the *s* in *measure*).

## How to Pronounce It

Round your lips slightly and push them forward into a gentle trumpet shape. Lift the blade and front of your tongue toward the roof of your mouth, just behind the alveolar ridge. Expel air smoothly through the channel without vibrating your vocal cords to produce a clear, rushing **sh** sound.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **s** appears freely at the beginning, middle, and end of native and loan words. When prolonged or doubled, it is written as **ss** (for example, *lassú* meaning slow). Remember that whenever you see a solo **s** in a Hungarian text, you must read it as an English 'sh'.

## Comparing Sounds and Common Mistakes

The most critical mistake English speakers make is pronouncing **s** as `/s/`, which entirely changes words or makes them unrecognizable. Compare **só** (`/ʃoː/`, salt) with **szó** (`/soː/`, word); confusing the two completely alters the meaning. Also watch out for regressive voicing in compound words such as **kisbaba** (`/ˈkiʒbɒbɒ/`), where the /ʃ/ softens into `/ʒ/`.
