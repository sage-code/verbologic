## The Sound of F

The Hungarian letter **F** represents the voiceless labiodental fricative `/f/`, identical to the English 'f' sound in words like *fish* or *coffee*. Unlike some other Hungarian consonants that assimilate across word boundaries, this sound remains remarkably crisp and consistent in almost all contexts. English speakers will find it completely familiar and natural to pronounce.

## How to Pronounce It

Rest your upper front teeth lightly against your lower lip, leaving a slight gap for airflow. Blow air continuously out of your mouth across this contact point without engaging your vocal cords. Keep the friction clear and unvoiced throughout the sound.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **F** can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of words. When doubled as **ff** (such as in *huff* or *felfed*), it indicates a prolonged, geminate `/fː/` consonant. In hyphenation across line breaks, doubled letters remain unchanged as **f-f**.

## Tips and Common Pitfalls

Take care to keep `/f/` strictly voiceless; do not accidentally voice it into a `/v/` before vowels or sonorant consonants. Contrast pairs like **férfi** (`/ˈfeːrfi/`, man) and **vér** (`/ˈveːr/`, blood) demonstrate why maintaining clear unvoicing is crucial for meaning. The primary variation is simply length: single `/f/` versus geminate `/fː/`.
