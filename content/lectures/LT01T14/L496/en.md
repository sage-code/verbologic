## The Sound of Gy

The Hungarian letter **Gy** represents a voiced palatal plosive, transcribed as `/ɟ/`. It is somewhat similar to the 'd' sound in the English word *duty* or the 'j' in *jeep*, but it is a pure stop made further back along the hard palate rather than an affricate. English speakers should be careful not to pronounce it like the English 'j' (`/d͡ʒ/`), as it lacks the hissing friction of that sound.

## How to Pronounce Gy

Place the flat body of your tongue firmly against your hard palate, just behind the alveolar ridge. The tip of your tongue should point downward behind your lower front teeth rather than touching the roof of your mouth. Build up slight air pressure, then release the seal smoothly while vibrating your vocal cords to create voice.

## Spelling Rules for Gy

**Gy** is considered an independent letter of the Hungarian alphabet and alphabetises separately after **G**. When writing in capital letters, only the first letter is capitalised in sentence case (**Gy**), though both are capitalised in all-caps texts (**GY**). If geminated (doubled), it is written as **ggy** (for example, in *meggy*), rather than repeating both letters.

## Sound Variations and Common Pitfalls

Hungarian **Gy** has a single standard value, `/ɟ/`, though it automatically undergoes voicing assimilation to its voiceless counterpart `/c/` (written as **ty**) before voiceless consonants. The most frequent pitfall for learners is substituting the English affricate `/d͡ʒ/` (as in *jar*) or the cluster `/dj/`. Practice contrasting minimal pairs like *gyár* and a hypothetical *djár* to keep the sound completely smooth and palatal.
