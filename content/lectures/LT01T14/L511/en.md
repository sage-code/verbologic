## The Sound of P

The Hungarian letter **P** represents the voiceless bilabial plosive `/p/`. Unlike in English, it is strictly **unaspirated**, meaning you release it without any audible puff of air, similar to the 'p' in English *spin* rather than *pin*. When it precedes a voiced consonant such as **d**, **g**, or **b**, it undergoes voicing assimilation and turns into a soft `/b/` sound.

## How to Pronounce P

Close both lips firmly together to completely block the airflow from your lungs. Let a small amount of air pressure build up behind your lips without vibrating your vocal cords. Then, part your lips crisply to release the sound without blowing extra breath out afterwards.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **P** can appear at the beginning, middle, or end of words. When doubled as **pp**, as in *csöpp* (tiny drop), it represents a **geminate** or long consonant `/pː/`, held for roughly twice the duration of a single **p**. In compound words, the combination of two **p** letters is written out fully.

## Sound Variations and Common Pitfalls

The regular sound is pure `/p/` (as in *piros*), but in words like *népdal* (folk song), it sounds like `/b/` due to the following voiced **d**. The most common mistake for English speakers is **aspirating** this consonant at the start of words; make sure to keep it tight and clean, holding back the breath.
