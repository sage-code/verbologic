## The Sound of K

The Hungarian letter **k** represents the voiceless velar plosive `/k/`. Unlike in English, it is completely **unaspirated**, meaning you release the sound cleanly without a burst of air. It closely resembles the crisp 'k' sound in the English word *sky* rather than the breathy 'k' in *kite*.

## How to Pronounce K

Raise the back of your tongue against the soft palate at the roof of your mouth to completely block the airflow. Release the contact sharply while keeping your vocal cords still. Ensure that no puff of breath escapes after the release, especially before front vowels.

## Spelling and Gemination

The letter **k** occurs frequently in all word positions, including prefixes like *ki-* and suffixes like the accusative marker *-t* after stems. When doubled as **kk**, it is held longer as a geminate consonant (as in *sokk*). Before voiced obstruents, it assimilates phonetically to `[ɡ]`, though the spelling remains **k**.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers often make the mistake of heavily aspirating **k**, pronouncing *kék* as `[kʰeːkʰ]`, which sounds distinctly foreign to Hungarian ears. Listen to the difference between normal `/k/` in *kék* and the voiced assimilatory variant `[ɡ]` in compound phrases like *kék bálna*. Practice keeping your throat tension sharp and breath release completely suppressed.
