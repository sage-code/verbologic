## Sound of Ly

Historically, **Ly** represented a palatal lateral approximant like Italian *gli*, but in modern standard Hungarian it has completely merged with **j** to sound like `/j/`. It is identical to the English consonant **y** in *yes* or *yellow*. While it looks like an *L*-combination, there is absolutely no *l*-sound in its contemporary pronunciation.

## How to Say Ly

Raise the middle body of your tongue towards the hard palate, keeping the tongue tip down behind the lower front teeth. Let your vocal cords vibrate as air flows smoothly across the surface of the tongue without creating friction. Do not touch the tip of your tongue to the upper gum ridge, or you will accidentally introduce an English *l* sound.

## Spelling and Rules

Hungarian orthography retains **Ly** entirely for historical and etymological reasons, making it a famous spelling hurdle even for native speakers. When doubled, it is written as **lly** (or **Lly** at the beginning of a capitalized sentence), pronounced as a long `/jː/` as in *süllyed*. Aside from the root word *lyuk* (hole) and its derivatives, very few modern words begin with this letter.

## Comparison and Pitfalls

The main challenge is that **Ly** sounds completely indistinguishable from the letter **J** in standard Hungarian; *folyó* (river) and *hajó* (ship) use the exact same sound `/j/`. Foreign learners often make the mistake of pronouncing it like an *l* followed by a *y* (as in *million*) or like Spanish *ll*. Remember that in every single context today, it functions simply as an ordinary `/j/`.
