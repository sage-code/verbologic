## The Sound of Ü

The Hungarian letter **Ü** represents a close front rounded vowel, transcribed as `/y/`. It is essentially identical to the German short **ü** in *fünf* or the French **u** in *tu*. English lacks this native sound, so learners should take care not to substitute an English 'oo' `/u/` or 'ee' `/i/`.

## How to Pronounce Ü

Start by producing a clear, continuous English 'ee' sound as in *see*, keeping your tongue arched high at the front of your mouth. Without moving your tongue at all, tightly round your lips into an 'o' or whistling shape. Keep the vowel short and crisp without sliding into a diphthong.

## Spelling and Distribution

Written as lowercase **ü** and uppercase **Ü**, this letter carries two dots (an umlaut or trema). It can appear in any position within a word, including initially, medially, and finally. Always distinguish it from the long version **Ű**, which uses double acute accents to mark duration.

## Comparisons and Pitfalls

The most critical distinction is between short **ü** `/y/` and long **ű** `/yː/`, as in *öt* (which has **ö**) vs. words like *fül* (ear) and *fű* (grass). English speakers often unround the lips to sound like **i** or pull the tongue back to sound like **u**. Practice contrasting *tükör* (mirror) with *tik* or *tuk* to keep the tongue forward and lips rounded.
