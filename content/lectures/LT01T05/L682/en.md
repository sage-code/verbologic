## Fast Connected Speech

In everyday Romanian, words connect together smoothly. Small words like pronouns and short negatives lose their own stress. They link directly to the next word in the sentence. We call these reduced words weak forms. A hyphen often shows this link in writing. When you speak at normal speed, do not treat each word like an isolated unit. Let the little words join the rhythm of the main words.

## Mouth, Stress, and Tone

Keep your tongue and lips relaxed during weak words. Do not pause where you see a hyphen. Put your main breath effort and loudness on the primary word. The weak word uses very little air. Your pitch should remain steady and flat on the weak word. Then, raise your pitch slightly on the stressed syllable of the core word. Release the entire phrase on a single breath.

## The Example in Action

Look at the phrase **n-am timp** /nam tʲimp/. Here, the negative word links with the verb to make one syllable /nam/. Close your lips quickly for the /m/ sound. Do not put heavy stress on /nam/. Move your tongue immediately toward the word **timp**. Notice the soft /tʲ/ sound right at the start. Stress the vowel in **timp** much more clearly than the vowel in **n-am**.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

English speakers often pause at hyphens. Never stop your voice between the letter n and the word am. Another mistake is pronouncing weak words too loudly. In English, you might emphasize every small word for clarity. In Romanian, doing that disrupts the natural rhythm. Keep your airflow continuous. Always keep the first syllable noticeably lighter and shorter than the main noun that follows.
