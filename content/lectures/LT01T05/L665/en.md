## The sound
Words break naturally into small beats called syllables.
Listen to the beats in (ajutor) /a.ʒuˈtor/, which means help.
Each beat carries a vowel sound.
Your voice stays smooth across every beat.
The rhythm guides your breath.
You hear three clear beats in this word.

## How to say it
Open your mouth slightly for the first beat.
Push air forward gently for the second beat.
Stress the last beat firmly with a higher pitch.
Do not stop your breath between beats.
Let each sound flow into the next smoothly.
Say (ajutor) /a.ʒuˈtor/ as one flowing melody.

## Reading rules
Sometimes a line ends in the middle of a word.
You place a hyphen between two complete syllables.
Never break a single syllable apart.
For example, you write (ajutor) /a.ʒuˈtor/ across lines as a-ju-tor.
Another example is (carte) /ˈkar.te/, which splits as car-te.
A vowel always remains in each part.

## Common mistakes
Do not cut inside a single syllable.
Never leave a single consonant alone at the line edge.
Do not pause unnaturally when you read a broken word aloud.
Keep the rhythm steady and natural.
Read the whole word without a gap.
Treat it just like any regular word.
