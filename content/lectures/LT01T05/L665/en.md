## The syllable break rule
In Romanian, words do not split at random letters when you reach the end of a line. You must divide them strictly between syllables. A syllable is a single beat of sound built around a vowel. Romanian spelling mirrors speech very closely. When you run out of space on a notebook line, place a hyphen after a full syllable. The rest of the syllables move to the next line smoothly.

## Mouth and voice rhythm
To find where to split, say the word out loud and clap the beats. Every syllable requires one open pulse of your vocal cords. Your mouth opens wider on the vowel and closes slightly on consonants. The musical tone stays level, but the stressed syllable sounds slightly louder and higher. When reading a split word, your voice pauses for a tiny instant at the hyphen without losing the word rhythm.

## The word a-ju-tor
A great example is the Romanian word for help: **a-ju-tor** /a.ʒuˈtor/. This word has three spoken beats. The first syllable is simply the vowel **a**. The second syllable is **ju**, pronounced with vibrating lips like the sound in measure. The final syllable is **tor**, where your tongue taps behind your upper teeth. The main stress lands strongly on **tor**. At line end, you write **a-** and move **jutor** down, or write **a-ju-** and move **tor** down.

## Common learner mistakes
English speakers often try to break words based on written roots or prefixes. In Romanian, the natural spoken beat always takes priority over spelling history. Do not isolate a single consonant between two vowels, because consonants usually attach to the following vowel. Never split two letters that create one blended vowel sound. Always let your breath pulses guide your pencil when you divide a word.
