## The pattern
In Romanian, the word "nu" means "not". The word "a" is an auxiliary verb used to build the past tense. When these two words appear together, they combine into one shorter form. The letter "u" disappears completely. A hyphen joins the remaining "n" to the letter "a". People write this combined form as **n-a**. You will hear and see this contraction everywhere in daily communication. It creates a single, fluent syllable.

## How to say it
Start by placing the tip of your tongue behind your upper front teeth. Make a quick /n/ sound with your voice. Immediately drop your jaw down to open your mouth wide. Let your tongue rest flat on the floor of your mouth. Produce a clear and open /a/ sound without pausing. The syllable /na/ is spoken without heavy stress. Your voice stays light and relaxed. The main stress of your sentence lands on the verb that follows.

## Examples in speech
Native speakers always connect these sounds smoothly. A very common phrase is **n-a venit** /na veˈnit/, which means "he did not come". The word "n-a" links directly to the verb without any break. The musical tone of your voice stays even on /na/. Then your pitch rises slightly on the final stressed syllable of "venit". Another common sentence is **n-a văzut**, meaning "she did not see". Both words sound like one continuous flow of breath.

## Common mistakes
Many English speakers try to pronounce "nu" and "a" as two distinct words. Saying "nu a" sounds stiff and unnatural to native listeners. Another common error is adding a glottal stop between the letters. Do not stop the air in your throat before the vowel /a/. Let the sound flow directly from the tongue tap into the open vowel. Finally, remember not to emphasize "n-a". Keep the stress on the main action verb instead.
