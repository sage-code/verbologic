## Quick words in invitations

When you invite a friend to do something in Romanian, you often use two small words: *hai* and *să*. In fast everyday speech, you do not pause between them. These two little words are not meant to stand out on their own. Instead, they act like a quick run-up to the main action. The real weight of the sentence falls on the verb that follows right after them.

## Mouth and voice movements

Start with **hai** by dropping your jaw slightly for the vowel and quickly closing your mouth into a glide. Next comes **să**. For this sound, keep your tongue resting in the middle of your mouth and your lips relaxed. Do not push extra air or raise your voice on these two words. Keep your pitch low and flat. Save your voice energy and pitch peak for the first syllable of the verb.

## Pronouncing the phrase

Listen to the standard phrase: **hai, să mergem** /haj sə ˈmer.dʒem/. Notice how smoothly the speaker glides through the first two words. You barely hear a break between them. The voice quickly steps forward until it hits the syllable **mer**. That main syllable is louder, longer, and slightly higher in pitch. Everything before it is just a swift launchpad for the full invitation.

## Common mistakes for English speakers

English speakers often stress every single word when they see a comma. In writing, you often see a comma between these words, but your voice must not stop. Do not say "HAI... SĂ...". Also, avoid turning the vowel in **să** into a bright, open "ah" sound. Keep that vowel neutral and relaxed. If you keep both words soft and fast, your invitation will sound completely natural.
