## One Quick Syllable
When speaking Romanian, two words often fuse together into a single sound. The question word *ce* and the verb form *ai* combine directly. In writing, a hyphen connects them as **ce-ai** /tʃaj/. Instead of two separate beats, your voice treats them as one unit. The original "e" sound disappears completely in this combination. This merger makes everyday speech fast, fluid, and natural.

## Mouth and Voice Action
Your mouth begins with the sharp "ch" sound as in "cheese". Next, your jaw drops open quickly to make the strong vowel "a". Then, your tongue rises slightly toward the roof of your mouth for the short "y" glide. The whole sound **ce-ai** /tʃaj/ has only one beat of voice. The stress lands firmly on the central "a" sound. Your pitch stays steady and does not jump between syllables.

## What It Means
The blended phrase **ce-ai** /tʃaj/ is very common in everyday conversation. It usually means "what do you have" or "what did you". For example, you hear it in questions asking someone what they ate or what is wrong. Because it is a single unit, you should never pause in the middle. Native speakers say it in a split second. Treating it as a single word helps people understand you immediately.

## Mistakes to Avoid
English speakers often make the mistake of reading the words separately as two beats. If you say "che" and then "eye", your speech will sound robotic and slow. Another common mistake is trying to pronounce an extra "e" sound in the middle. Remember that the "e" vanishes entirely in **ce-ai** /tʃaj/. Keep your lips relaxed and let the entire cluster flow out in one quick breath.
