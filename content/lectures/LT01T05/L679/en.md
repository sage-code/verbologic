## Short words share their beat
Romanian uses a musical rhythm where not every word receives equal strength. Little helper words like prepositions carry almost no stress. Words like *în* or *la* do not stand alone in speech. Instead, they attach their sound to the main word that follows them. Think of the little word as a light stepping stone before the louder word. When you look at a sentence, these words do not get their own independent beat. They belong to the musical unit of the larger noun right behind them.

## How your voice moves
Keep your voice relaxed and lower in pitch when starting the little word. Do not stop breathing between the small word and the main noun. Your lips and tongue should glide straight into the first sound of the next word. When you say **în casă** /ɨn ˈka.sə/, your tongue starts back for the vowel in **în**. Then your tongue quickly taps behind the upper teeth for /n/ and releases into the strong /k/ of **casă**. You pronounce them together like one single word.

## Listening to the rhythm
Listen carefully to how native Romanian speakers pronounce common pairs of words. Notice the phrase **în casă** /ɨn ˈka.sə/, which means at home. The word **în** sounds very short, quiet, and fast. The first syllable of **casă** sounds louder, higher, and slightly longer. Another common example is the phrase *la masă* /la ˈma.sə/, which means at the table. Here again, the small word *la* stays light and soft. The voice leaps smoothly onto the stressed syllable *ma* without any pause.

## Common mistakes to avoid
English speakers often treat every written word as a separate block. If you pause after small prepositions, your Romanian will sound stiff and robotic. Another frequent mistake is putting too much volume or stress on words like *în* or *la*. Never say the words with a heavy gap or two loud beats. Instead, keep the small word quiet and quick, like a soft pickup note in music. Let your voice rush naturally toward the main stressed syllable of the following noun.
