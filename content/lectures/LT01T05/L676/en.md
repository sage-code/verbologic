## The Spoken Pattern
In formal Romanian, the standard word for thank you is **mulțumesc** /mult͡suˈmesk/. It has three full syllables. In fast daily speech, people like to save effort. Romanian speakers often drop the first syllable entirely in casual conversations. You will regularly hear **'țumesc** instead. Another common informal option is **mersi**, borrowed from French. Both short forms appear constantly among friends and family. Learning this pattern helps you understand fast native speakers.

## Mouth and Stress Details
The word **mulțumesc** /mult͡suˈmesk/ carries its main stress on the last syllable. Your voice must emphasize the final part, **-mesc**. When speakers shorten the word to **'țumesc**, the stress stays on that same syllable. Place the tip of your tongue against the back of your upper teeth for the **ț** sound. Release the air with a brisk hiss, like the end of the English word *cats*. Then round your lips briefly before opening them for the ending.

## Pitch and Melody
Keep your melody friendly and polite. In Romanian, expressions of gratitude usually follow a gentle rise and fall in pitch. Start at your normal speaking tone. Raise your pitch slightly as you reach the stressed final syllable. Do not drop your volume at the end of the word. Let your jaw relax so the consonant cluster flows without extra pauses. A smooth and energetic delivery sounds the most natural to local ears.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid
English speakers often put stress on the first syllable. Saying *MUL-țu-mesc* sounds stiff and incorrect in Romanian. Always keep the vocal weight at the end of the word. Another common mistake is pronouncing every sound too rigidly in informal settings. When a close friend helps you, an overly careful **mulțumesc** /mult͡suˈmesk/ can feel too distant. Use the shortened form **'țumesc** or **mersi** to sound warm, but keep the full word for formal occasions.
