This chapter practises short Romanian questions and their possible answers, built from the vocabulary and sentence patterns you already know. Every question comes with two answers, so you practise both **asking** and **replying** in realistic mini-exchanges.

## From statement to question

A yes/no question in Romanian usually keeps the **same words** as the statement. What changes is the **intonation** — the voice rises at the end — and, in writing, the question mark:

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| *Ești student.* — You are a student. | *Ești student?* — Are you a student? |
| *El este prietenul tău.* — He is your friend. | *El este prietenul tău?* — Is he your friend? |

## Answering: da and nu

Answer with **da** (yes) or **nu** (no), followed by a short full sentence:

| Question | Answer 1 | Answer 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *Ești student?* | *Da, sunt student.* | *Nu, sunt profesor.* |

::term{addresses="question_esti-student,question_da-sunt-student,question_nu-sunt-profesor"}
::

::callout{type="tip"}
Repeat the verb in your answer (*Da, **sunt** student.*). It sounds natural and trains the verb forms at the same time.
::

## Questions with a question word

Some questions ask for information instead of yes/no. They start with a question word — *care* (which), *ce* (what), *cine* (who), *unde* (where), *când* (when), *cum* (how):

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| *Care este numele tău?* — What is your name? | *Numele meu este Andrei.* — My name is Andrei. |

::term{addresses="question_care-este-numele-tau,question_numele-meu-este-andrei"}
::

## How the tables work

The tables are grouped by area of life: personal basics, home and food, study and work, city and travel, health and environment. Each question is followed by its two answers. Play the question, answer aloud yourself, then check both answers.
