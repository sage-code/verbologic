In Romanian spelling, digraphs and trigraphs are sequences of two or three letters that represent **a single sound**, not the separate sounds of each letter. Watch the video first, then use the tables for guided practice.

::youtube{id="CEyYGL_umtI" title="Romanian digraphs and trigraphs"}
::

## Digraphs

These combinations make **c** and **g** soft when an **e** or **i** follows:

| Digraph | Sound (IPA) | English approximation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ce / ci** | /tʃ/ | `ch` as in "cheese" / "chin" | *ceas* (watch), *ciocan* (hammer) |
| **ge / gi** | /dʒ/ | `j` as in "jump" / "gin" | *geam* (window), *girafă* (giraffe) |

::term{addresses="letter_ce-ci,letter_ge-gi"}
::

## Trigraphs

Adding an **h** keeps **c** and **g** hard before e and i. The three letters work together as one sound unit:

| Trigraph | Sound (IPA) | English approximation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **che / chi** | /kʲ/ | hard `k` followed by a front-vowel glide | *cheie* (key), *chip* (face) |
| **ghe / ghi** | /ɡʲ/ | hard `g` as in "get" before front vowels | *gheată* (boot), *ghiocel* (snowdrop) |

::term{addresses="letter_che-chi,letter_ghe-ghi"}
::

::callout{type="tip"}
The whole system is one rule: **e** or **i** after **c/g** softens them; an **h** in between keeps them hard. *ceai* (tea) vs *chei* (keys), *ger* (frost) vs *gheară* (claw).
::
