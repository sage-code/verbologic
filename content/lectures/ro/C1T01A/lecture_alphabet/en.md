Romanian does something English never dared: **it spells exactly what it speaks.** Every letter has one sound, every sound has one letter. There are no silent letters waiting to ambush you, no "though / through / tough" traps. Read the word and you have spoken it — correctly — the first time.

That is the single most useful fact about this language, and it changes how you should study it: **learn the 31 letters once, and the entire dictionary becomes pronounceable forever.**

## The five letters English doesn't have

Five letters carry everything that makes Romanian look foreign. They are not exotic — each is a small, fixed modification of a letter you already know:

- **ă** — the *uh* in English "again". It only ever appears unstressed, in the middle or at the end of a word: *măr* (apple), *casă* (house).
- **â** and **î** — the same sound, two spellings: the *i* in "bird", said with the tongue pulled back. **î** starts words; **â** lives inside them: *învăț* (I learn), *cât* (how much).
- **ș** — plain English *sh*: *școală* (school).
- **ț** — *ts*, the end of English "cats", standing proudly at the front of a word: *țară* (country).

::callout{type="tip"}
The diacritics are not decoration. *patru* (four) and *pătru* are different words; dropping ă/â/î is the fastest way to be misunderstood in writing.
::

## Two Latin letters doing double duty

Romanian kept the Latin alphabet but reused two letters for sounds English keeps separate:

- **c** is *ch* before e and i (*cel* → "chel"), hard *k* everywhere else (*casa* → "kah-sa").
- **g** follows the same logic: *girofar* starts with a *j* sound, *gust* with a hard *g*.

That e/i rule is the only conditional spelling rule in the entire language — two letters, one line of logic, and you are done.

## Why this pays for itself immediately

Because spelling mirrors sound, the dictionary becomes a pronunciation trainer. Pick any word — *bibliotecă*, *permis*, *sprânceană* — and you can already say it. Compare that with English or French, where the same confidence takes years.
