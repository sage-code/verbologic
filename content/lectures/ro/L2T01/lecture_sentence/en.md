A sentence is a **complete unit of meaning**. In writing, it begins with a capital letter and ends with punctuation. In speech, it expresses one complete idea: a fact, a question, a command, or a strong feeling.

## The four sentence types

| Type | Purpose | Ends with | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Declarative** | states information | `.` | *Eu sunt student.* — I am a student. |
| **Interrogative** | asks a question | `?` | *Ești student?* — Are you a student? |
| **Imperative** | gives a command or instruction | `.` or `!` | *Spune bună ziua.* — Say good day. |
| **Exclamative** | expresses strong emotion | `!` | *Ce frumos!* — How beautiful! |

The next chapters follow this order: **statements** first, then **questions with their answers**, then **commands**.

## Why statements come first

Declarative sentences are the foundation of basic communication. With them you identify people, describe objects, explain routines, and report simple facts. Once this pattern is solid, questions and commands are small changes to it.

A Romanian statement usually follows the same order as English — **subject, verb, rest**:

| Subject | Verb | Rest | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Eu | sunt | student. | I am a student. |
| El | este | profesor. | He is a teacher. |
| Ea | este | femeie. | She is a woman. |

::callout{type="note"}
The subject pronoun is often dropped, because the verb ending already says who acts: *Sunt student.* means the same as *Eu sunt student.*
::
