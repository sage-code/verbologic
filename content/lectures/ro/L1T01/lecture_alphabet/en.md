Romanian does something English never dared: **it spells exactly what it speaks.** Every letter has one sound, every sound has one letter. There are no silent letters waiting to ambush you, no "though / through / tough" traps. Read the word and you have spoken it — correctly — the first time.

That is the single most useful fact about this language, and it changes how you should study it: **learn the 31 letters once, and the entire dictionary becomes pronounceable forever.**

## Overview

- **Structure:** Romanian uses 31 letters based on the Latin alphabet, including 5 letters with diacritics: ă, â, î, ș, ț.
- **Phonetic consistency:** a nearly transparent spelling — each letter maps to one sound, so Romanian text is easy to read.
- **Vowels:** 7 core vowel values. The central vowels (ă, â/î) need the most practice.
- **Consonants:** the standard Latin consonants plus dedicated sounds such as *sh* (ș) and *ts* (ț).
- **Inheritance:** evolved directly from the Latin alphabet, with Slavic and regional sound changes.

## The five letters English doesn't have

Five letters carry everything that makes Romanian look foreign. They are not exotic — each is a small, fixed modification of a letter you already know:

- **ă** — the *uh* in English "again". It only ever appears unstressed, in the middle or at the end of a word: *măr* (apple), *casă* (house).
- **â** and **î** — the same sound, two spellings: the *i* in "bird", said with the tongue pulled back. **î** starts words; **â** lives inside them: *învăț* (I learn), *cât* (how much).
- **ș** — plain English *sh*: *școală* (school).
- **ț** — *ts*, the end of English "cats", standing proudly at the front of a word: *țară* (country).

::callout{type="tip"}
The diacritics are not decoration. *patru* (four) and *pătru* are different words; dropping ă/â/î is the fastest way to be misunderstood in writing.
::

## Two Latin letters doing double duty

Romanian kept the Latin alphabet but reused two letters for sounds English keeps separate:

- **c** is *ch* before e and i (*cel* → "chel"), hard *k* everywhere else (*casa* → "kah-sa").
- **g** follows the same logic: *girofar* starts with a *j* sound, *gust* with a hard *g*.

That e/i rule is the only conditional spelling rule in the entire language — two letters, one line of logic, and you are done.

## Pronunciation rules

| Letter | IPA | English approximation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A a | /a/ | Like the **a** in **father**; open and clear, not clipped. |
| Ă ă | /ə/ | Like the unstressed **u** in **but**; short central vowel. |
| Â â | /ɨ/ | No exact English match; a central, unrounded vowel with the tongue higher in the mouth. |
| B b | /b/ | Like the **b** in **bed**; voiced and direct. |
| C c | /k/ or /tʃ/ | Like **c** in **cat** before a/o/u, and like **ch** in **chin** before e/i. |
| D d | /d/ | Like the **d** in **dog**; clean voiced stop. |
| E e | /e/ | Like the **e** in **bed**, but steadier and more closed. |
| F f | /f/ | Like the **f** in **fan**. |
| G g | /g/ or /dʒ/ | Like **g** in **go** before a/o/u, and like **g** in **gem** before e/i. |
| H h | /h/ | Like the **h** in **hat**; simple breathy sound. |
| I i | /i/ or /j/ | Like the **i** in **machine** as a vowel, or like **y** in **yes** as a glide. |
| Î î | /ɨ/ | Same sound as **â**; a central Romanian vowel with no direct English equivalent. |
| J j | /ʒ/ | Like the **s** in **measure** or **vision**. |
| K k | /k/ | Like the **k** in **kite**; mainly in loanwords. |
| L l | /l/ | Like the **l** in **leg**; clear, not darkened. |
| M m | /m/ | Like the **m** in **map**. |
| N n | /n/ | Like the **n** in **net**. |
| O o | /o/ | Like the **o** in **more**, but shorter and cleaner. |
| P p | /p/ | Like the **p** in **pen**; unvoiced and crisp. |
| R r | /r/ | Rolled or tapped **r**, closer to Spanish than English. |
| S s | /s/ | Like the **s** in **sit**. |
| Ș ș | /ʃ/ | Like the **sh** in **shoe**. |
| T t | /t/ | Like the **t** in **top**; short and unvoiced. |
| Ț ț | /ts/ | Like the **ts** in **cats**; a clear affricate, not a plain t. |
| U u | /u/ | Like the **oo** in **boot**; rounded and steady. |
| V v | /v/ | Like the **v** in **van**. |
| W w | /w/ | Mostly in loanwords; like English **w** in **wind**. |
| X x | /ks/ or /gz/ | Like **x** in **box** or in **exact**, depending on position. |
| Y y | /j/ or /i/ | Mostly in loanwords; often like **y** in **yes**. |
| Z z | /z/ | Like the **z** in **zoo**. |

## Why this pays for itself immediately

Because spelling mirrors sound, the dictionary becomes a pronunciation trainer. Pick any word — *bibliotecă*, *permis*, *sprânceană* — and you can already say it. Compare that with English or French, where the same confidence takes years.
