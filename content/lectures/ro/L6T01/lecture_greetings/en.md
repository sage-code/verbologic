This lecture prepares you for real first-contact conversations with native speakers. You will learn how to choose the right formula based on context, relationship, and formality level, then apply everything in short, verifiable dialogs.

## Why greetings matter

In Romanian, the first 3–5 seconds of a conversation set the social tone. An unsuitable greeting is not only a vocabulary issue but also a register issue: it may sound too distant, too familiar, or disrespectful.

**Formal vs informal register.** Start formal when you do not know the person, then move to informal only if the other person clearly signals it. Address formulas are closely tied to pronouns: **dumneavoastră** (formal) versus **tu** (informal).

**Micro-dialog structure.** A complete minimal exchange has three parts: greeting, short social question, and polite closing. Model: *Salut + Ce mai faceți? + Mulțumesc, o zi bună!*

## Essential formulas

### Casual greetings

| Formula | Context |
| --- | --- |
| Salut | friends, close colleagues |
| Bună | daily informal situations |
| Ceao | very close friends |

### Polite greetings

| Formula | Context |
| --- | --- |
| Bună dimineața | morning, formal or neutral |
| Bună ziua | daytime, formal or neutral |
| Bună seara | evening, formal or neutral |
| Noapte bună | end of day, before sleep |

### Conversation closings

| Formula | Note |
| --- | --- |
| Pe curând | Warm formula used when you expect to meet again soon. |
| La revedere | Standard polite form, suitable for formal and neutral contexts. |
| Să fii sănătos | Personal wish, more intimate; conveys care and respect. |
| Noroc bun | Rare/regional formula; may sound archaic, mainly used in traditional tone. |
| Să auzim de bine | Friendly closing with a positive future meaning. |
| Să ne vedem sănătoși | Affectionate wish, suitable when separation may be longer. |
| Adio și noroc | Very strong; may suggest a final goodbye. Use carefully. |

## Executable examples

Read each dialog, then watch the video and repeat every line aloud. Try to speak clearly and calmly.

**Example 1 — interaction with an unknown person**

```text
Emil: Bună ziua!
-Ana: Bună ziua!
Emil: Ce mai faceți?
-Ana: Foarte bine, mulțumesc. Dumneavoastră?
Emil: Bine, mulțumesc. O zi frumoasă!
-Ana: La revedere!
```

**Example 2 — colleague or friend**

```text
Emil: Ceao! Ce faci?
-Ana: Bine, tu?
Emil: Super. Mersi!

Emil: Da bine. De-abia aștept!
```


## Pronunciation and rhythm

::callout{type="tip"}
Diacritics are essential in Romanian. For example, the letter "a" can appear as "â" or "ă". These are not optional accents, but distinct letters with their own phonetic values that can change word meaning.
::

Short social questions usually have moderate rising intonation at the end: *Ce mai faceți?* Avoid exaggerated pitch rise, or the sentence may sound unnatural or hesitant.

**Repeat each group three times in a row:**

- Salut, salut, salut
- Bună, bună, bună
- Ceau, ceau, ceau
- Noroc, noroc, noroc
- Sănătate, sănătos, sănătoși

::

## Common mistakes

**False friends.** Romanian has words that look familiar to English speakers but have different meanings. For example, "hai" means "come on." Using it as a direct greeting can sound odd and may be misunderstood.

**Confusing *tu* and *dumneavoastră*.** Typical error: mixing both in one sentence, for example *Ce faci dumneavoastră?* Choose one register per interaction.

**Literal translation from English.** *How do you do?* should not be translated mechanically. Natural Romanian greeting forms include *Bună ziua*, *Ce mai faceți?*, or *Îmi pare bine*, depending on context.

::term{addresses="word_imi-pare-bine"}
::

**Politeness omissions.** Without *scuze*, *vă rog*, or *mulțumesc*, a message may sound imperative. Especially at the beginning, intentionally use polite formulas until the register feels natural.
