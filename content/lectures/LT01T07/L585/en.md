## The Sh Sound of Letter C

In certain specific environments, the letter **C** produces the voiceless postalveolar fricative `/ʃ/`. This is identical to the familiar English 'sh' sound heard in words like *ship* or *shoe*. It occurs primarily when **C** sits in an unstressed syllable right before vowel combinations beginning with 'e' or 'i'.

## How to Produce /ʃ/

Round your lips slightly and flare them outward gently. Raise the blade of your tongue close to the roof of your mouth just behind the alveolar ridge without making full contact. Expel continuous air smoothly across the tongue to create a soft, friction-filled rushing sound.

## Spelling and Patterns

This palatalized pronunciation of **C** appears almost exclusively in Latin-derived suffixes such as **-cean** (*ocean*), **-cial** (*special*), and **-cious** (*delicious*). Outside these suffix environments, **C** is typically read as `/k/` or `/s/`. You will not encounter `/ʃ/` for standalone **C** at the start or end of native Germanic roots.

## Contrasting Sounds and Pitfalls

Be careful not to pronounce words like *ocean* or *musician* with a crisp `/s/` or hard `/k/`. Learners often attempt to pronounce the vowels following the **C** separately, turning *ocean* into three syllables instead of two. Remember that the combination merges into a single clean `/ʃ/` sound followed by a schwa.
