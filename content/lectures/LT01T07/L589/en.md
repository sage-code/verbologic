## The Sound of S

The letter **S** most typically represents the voiceless alveolar fricative `/s/`, heard as a sharp hissing sound as in **sun**. However, it very commonly represents the voiced sound `/z/`, especially between vowels or at the end of plural words. Unlike some languages where 's' is softened before certain vowels, English `/s/` remains crisp and unpalatalized.

## How to Pronounce S

Place the tip of your tongue near the alveolar ridge right behind your top front teeth without actually touching it. Leave a narrow central groove along the blade of your tongue to channel the airstream forward. Force air continuously through this narrow channel while keeping your vocal cords completely still for `/s/`.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **S** appears anywhere in a word, from starts like **sea** to grammatical endings like plurals and third-person verbs. Doubled **ss** almost universally guarantees a voiceless `/s/` sound, as in **class** or **lesson**. When combined with 'h', it forms the digraph **sh**, representing `/ʃ/` as in **ship**.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

The most frequent error is failing to voice **S** as `/z/` in plural endings following vowels or voiced consonants, such as **dogs** (`/dɒɡz/`) or **eyes** (`/aɪz/`). Learners also occasionally confuse the voiced `/z/` and voiceless `/s/` in minimal pairs like **loose** (`/luːs/`) versus **lose** (`/luːz/`). Make sure to check whether your vocal cords vibrate in grammatical endings.
