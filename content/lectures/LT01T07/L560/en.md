## The Sound of H

The letter **H** represents an unvoiced glottal fricative, written as `/h/`. It is essentially a brief, audible puff of breath through open vocal cords, taking on the mouth shape of the vowel that follows it. Unlike harsher guttural sounds in other languages, the English `/h/` should sound soft, smooth, and completely unconstricted.

## How to Pronounce H

Part your lips naturally and relax your tongue in the middle of your mouth. Exhale gently from your throat as if you are trying to fog up a mirror with your breath. Make sure your vocal cords do not vibrate, and immediately transition into the next vowel sound.

## Spelling and Patterns

**H** appears frequently at the beginning of words like *hat* and *home*, as well as in common digraphs such as *th*, *sh*, and *ch*. In a small handful of words borrowed from French, such as *hour*, *honest*, and *heir*, the initial letter is entirely silent. Additionally, **H** is often dropped in natural, connected speech within weak function words like *him* or *her*.

## Variants and Common Pitfalls

Be careful not to pronounce the silent **H** in words like *honour* (`/ˈɒn.ər/`) or omit it where it must be voiced, such as in *house* (`/haʊs/`). Speakers of Romance languages often tend to drop all **H** sounds entirely, turning *heart* into *art*. Conversely, avoid scraping the back of your throat with a harsh raspy friction like the German `[x]`.
