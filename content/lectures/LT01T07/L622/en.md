## The Sound of -tion

The suffix **-tion** is most commonly pronounced as `/ʃən/`, blending the soft 'sh' sound into a reduced, neutral schwa and an `/n/`. It never carries primary stress, which instead falls on the syllable immediately preceding it. After the consonant **s**, the sound changes slightly to `/tʃən/`, as heard in words like **question**.

## How to Produce -tion

To make the standard `/ʃ/` sound, round your lips slightly and push air between the blade of your tongue and the roof of your mouth. Immediately relax your vocal tract into a very short, neutral vowel before touching the tip of your tongue to the alveolar ridge for the `/n/`. Keep the whole syllable light, brief, and completely unstressed.

## Spelling and Patterns

This four-letter ending is one of the most common noun-forming suffixes in English, derived from Latin via French. It almost always forms abstract nouns from verbs, such as **act** turning into **action**. Whenever you see **-tion**, you can reliably place the primary word stress on the syllable right before it.

## Variants and Pitfalls

The primary contrast occurs between the standard `/ʃən/` in **nation** and the affricate `/tʃən/` found after **s** in **digestion**. Learners often make the mistake of pronouncing the vowel as a full, clear 'o' or reading the 't' literally as `/t/`. Remember to reduce the vowel completely to a schwa and let the ending glide by softly.
