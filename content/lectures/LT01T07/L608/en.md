## The Sound of '-lk'

In classic common words ending in '-lk' following the vowels 'a' or 'o', the letter 'l' is completely silent, leaving only the voiceless velar stop `/k/`. Learners often mistakenly articulate the 'l', but in words like 'walk' or 'talk', only the final `/k/` is heard. Unlike in many other languages where every written consonant is pronounced, English completely drops the 'l' in these historical vowel combinations.

## How to Produce It

When pronouncing words like 'walk', do not raise the tip of your tongue to touch the roof of the mouth for an 'l'. Instead, shape your mouth for the preceding vowel and then press the back of your tongue directly against the soft palate. Release the air abruptly with a clean, unvoiced puff to form a crisp `/k/`.

## Spelling Patterns

The combination '-lk' appears exclusively at the ends of morphemes and root words. In words with 'alk' (such as 'walk', 'chalk', 'stalk') and 'olk' (such as 'yolk', 'folk'), the 'l' is silent. However, after vowels like 'i' or 'u' (in 'milk', 'silk', 'bulk'), the 'l' is fully pronounced alongside `/k/`.

## Silent 'l' vs. Sounded 'l'

Be careful to distinguish words with a silent 'l' like 'talk' (`/tɔːk/`) and 'folk' (`/foʊk/`) from words where 'l' must be articulated, such as 'milk' (`/mɪlk/`) or 'elk' (`/ɛlk/`). The most frequent error is trying to pronounce a literal 'l' sound in 'walk' or 'talk', which makes speech sound unnatural to native ears.
