## The Sound of '-eigh'

The spelling pattern **-eigh** most commonly represents the diphthong `/eɪ/`, exactly like the name of the letter **A**. It glides smoothly from a mid-front vowel toward a higher front position. Learners should avoid pronouncing the silent letters **gh** as a guttural or friction sound.

## How to Pronounce It

Start with your mouth moderately open and the front of your tongue in a mid position, as in the vowel of **bed**. Then, glide your jaw slightly upward as your tongue raises toward the roof of your mouth to finish near `/ɪ/`. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and do not pronounce any consonant sound for the **gh**.

## Spelling and Patterns

This combination is a historical spelling pattern where the letters **gh** have become completely silent in modern English. It typically occurs in native Germanic roots either before a final **t** (as in **weight** and **eight**) or at the very end of a base word (such as **sleigh** and **weigh**). It rarely appears at the beginning of words except in numerals like **eight** and its derivatives.

## Contrasting Sounds and Pitfalls

While `/eɪ/` is the standard value, a crucial exception occurs in words like **height** and **sleight**, where **-eigh** is pronounced `/aɪ/`. A frequent error among non-native speakers is attempting to articulate the letters **gh** as a rasping sound `/x/` or a hard `/g/`. Remember that the entire cluster **-eigh** simply functions as a single long vowel or diphthong.
