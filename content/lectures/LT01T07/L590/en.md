## The Sound of S as /z/

In this role, the letter **S** represents the voiced alveolar fricative `/z/`. It is acoustically identical to the sound of the letter **Z** in English words like *zebra*. Unlike the voiceless `/s/`, this sound requires your vocal cords to actively vibrate throughout the articulation.

## How to Say It

Place the tip or blade of your tongue very close to your upper gum ridge without fully touching it. Force air through the narrow channel to create a hissing friction. Simultaneously engage your vocal cords to add voice, producing a steady buzzing sound like a bee.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **S** frequently sounds like `/z/` when it appears between two vowels, as in *rose*, *busy*, or *music*. It is also the standard pronunciation for regular plural and third-person suffixes following voiced sounds, such as in *dogs* or *reads*. At the start of native English words, however, **S** is almost never pronounced as `/z/`.

## Common Traps and Variations

Learners often mistakenly use the voiceless `/s/` whenever they see the letter **S**, pronouncing *rose* as *ross*. Remember that intervocalic **S** and plural endings after voiced consonants regularly switch to `/z/`. Pay close attention to word pairs like *use* (noun, with `/s/`) and *use* (verb, with `/z/`).
