## The Sound of /ɑː/

In the keyword **father**, the letter **A** represents the open back unrounded vowel `/ɑː/`. This is a deep, relaxed sound pronounced further back in the mouth than the bright vowel in *cat*. In British Received Pronunciation, it is typically held longer than standard short vowels, whereas General American pronounces it with a similar back quality but slightly varied duration.

## How to Produce /ɑː/

Drop your jaw comfortably wide open while keeping your lips completely unrounded and relaxed. Flatten your tongue and pull the body of the tongue slightly toward the back of your mouth, ensuring it stays low. Let your vocal cords vibrate freely, creating a clear, resonance-filled sound similar to what a doctor asks you to make when inspecting your throat.

## Spelling Patterns

The `/ɑː/` pronunciation of **A** commonly appears in words like *father*, *bra*, and *spa*, as well as before silent **L** in words like *calm* and *palm*. In British English, it also frequently occurs before voiceless fricatives such as in *fast*, *path*, and *after*. Capital **A** consists of two diagonal strokes joined at the peak with a horizontal crossbar, while lowercase **a** features a rounded bowl with a vertical stem.

## Comparing Variations and Avoiding Traps

Do not confuse the broad `/ɑː/` in *father* with the short `/æ/` in *fat* or the diphthong `/eɪ/` in *fate*. A frequent error among learners is rounding the lips into an `/ɒ/` or `/ɔː/` sound, which can make *calm* sound like *call*. Keep the oral cavity wide open and avoid raising the tongue blade toward the hard palate.
