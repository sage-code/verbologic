## The Sound of '-ough'

The letter combination **-ough** is infamous for having several distinct pronunciations, but in words like *thought* it represents the long open vowel `/ɔː/` (or `/ɑː/` in American English). It sounds like the vowel in *caught* or *law*. Learners should note that the 'gh' is completely silent here, serving only as an historical spelling marker.

## How to Pronounce It

Drop your jaw moderately and round your lips slightly into a relaxed oval shape. Pull the back of your tongue backward and upward toward the soft palate without touching it. Produce a steady, resonant voiced sound from the throat while keeping the tongue still.

## Spelling Patterns

The spelling **-ough** is a classic historical relic of Middle English, where the 'gh' originally represented a guttural sound. When followed by a **t**, as in *thought*, *bought*, and *brought*, it almost always produces the `/ɔː/` sound. It rarely appears at the beginning of words and is predominantly found in verb forms and adjectives.

## Common Pitfalls

The greatest danger is trying to pronounce **-ough** phonetically letter by letter or assuming it always sounds like `/oʊ/` or `/ʌf/`. Compare *thought* (`/θɔːt/`) with *though* (`/ðoʊ/`) and *tough* (`/tʌf/`). Avoid inserting an 'f' sound where it does not belong, such as saying 'thorft' instead of **thought**.
