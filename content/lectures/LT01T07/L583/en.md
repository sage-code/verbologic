## The Sounds of 'C'

The letter **C** does not represent a single unique sound in English; it most commonly makes the hard `/k/` sound, as in *cat*, or the soft `/s/` sound, as in *city*. Unlike languages where **C** represents an affricate like `/ts/` or `/tʃ/`, English **C** relies entirely on the vowel that follows it. In word-initial positions before stressed vowels, the hard `/k/` is pronounced with a noticeable puff of air called aspiration.

## How to Pronounce the Hard 'C'

To produce the hard `/k/` sound, raise the back of your tongue until it firmly presses against the soft palate at the roof of your mouth. Allow air pressure to build briefly behind this seal, then release it abruptly with a crisp burst of air. Keep your vocal cords completely still, as this consonant is entirely unvoiced.

## Spelling and Context Rules

As a general rule, **C** is pronounced `/k/` before the vowels **A**, **O**, and **U**, as well as before most consonants and at the ends of words like *music*. When followed by **E**, **I**, or **Y**, it softens to `/s/`, as seen in *center* and *cycle*. Furthermore, combinations like **-cial** or **-cious** shift the sound to `/ʃ/`, as in *delicious*.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

A frequent mistake for learners from Slavic or Central European language backgrounds is pronouncing **C** as `/ts/`, turning *city* into *tsity*. Remember that English never uses `/ts/` for a single **C**; it alternates strictly between `/k/` and `/s/`. Take care with minimal spelling pairs like *call* `/kɔːl/` versus *cell* `/sɛl/` to ensure your pronunciation aligns with the following vowel.
