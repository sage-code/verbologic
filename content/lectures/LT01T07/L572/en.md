## The Sound of T

The letter **T** is a voiceless alveolar plosive, written in IPA as `/t/`. At the beginning of stressed syllables, it is pronounced with a distinct puff of air known as **aspiration** (`[tʰ]`). Unlike dental consonants in many Romance languages, English `/t/` touches the ridge behind the teeth, giving it a crisp, percussive quality.

## How to Pronounce T

Place the tip of your tongue firmly against the **alveolar ridge**—the bumpy gum area just behind your upper front teeth. Build up air pressure behind your tongue without vibrating your vocal cords. Release the tongue sharply to let a clean burst of air escape.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **T** appears in both lowercase `t` and uppercase `T`, commonly doubled as in *butter* or *kitten*. It also forms key digraphs like **th** (`/θ/` or `/ð/`), and when paired with vowels in suffixes such as *-tion*, it represents the sound `/ʃ/` (*nation*). In clusters like *stle* (*castle*, *listen*), the **t** is completely silent.

## Accents and Common Pitfalls

Be careful not to press your tongue against the back of your front teeth, which creates a softer dental sound rather than the crisp English `/t/`. In North American English, a single **t** between vowels often turns into a voiced flap `/ɾ/`, making *writer* sound identical to *rider*. In casual British English, syllable-final **t** is frequently replaced by a glottal stop `/ʔ/`.
