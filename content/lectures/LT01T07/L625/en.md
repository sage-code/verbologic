## Sound Value

The spelling pattern **-ough** is notoriously unpredictable in English, but in words like *though* it represents the long diphthong `/oʊ/` (or `/əʊ/` in British English). It sounds identical to the 'o' sound in *go* or *boat*. The combination **gh** is completely silent here, leaving only the vowel sound to be pronounced.

## How to Say It

Start with your tongue positioned in the mid-back of your mouth while keeping your lips slightly rounded. Glide your tongue upward and further back toward the velum while tightening your lips into a closer, smaller circle. Ensure you do not pronounce any consonant sound like `/g/` or `/f/` at the end.

## Spelling Conventions

This four-letter cluster appears most commonly in the middle or at the very end of native Germanic roots. In words like *though* and *dough*, the entire quadgraph acts solely as a long 'o' vowel sound. Because spelling reform attempts have mostly failed, you will still see standard full forms in formal writing, though informal American variants like *donut* or *thru* exist.

## Comparisons and Common Mistakes

Learners often struggle because **-ough** can represent up to nine different pronunciations in English, including `/ʌf/` in *enough*, `/uː/` in *through*, and `/ɔː/` in *thought*. The most frequent mistake is assuming the consonant letters produce an audible sound in *though*, causing learners to wrongly say `/ðɒf/` or add a guttural `/x/`. Memorize each vocabulary item individually rather than trying to apply a single universal rule.
