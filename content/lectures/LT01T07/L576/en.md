## The Sound of X

In English, the letter **X** almost always represents a consonant cluster rather than a single sound. Most frequently it is pronounced as voiceless `/ks/` as in *box*, or as voiced `/ɡz/` as in *example*. When found at the very start of rare loanwords, it softens to a simple `/z/` sound.

## How to Pronounce X

To make the common `/ks/` sound, raise the back of your tongue to touch the soft palate to stop the airflow for `/k/`, then immediately release it into a hissing `/s/` behind your teeth. For the voiced `/ɡz/`, use the exact same tongue positions while vibrating your vocal cords. Keep your lips neutral and relaxed throughout both sounds.

## Spelling and Positions

**X** is unique because a single printed letter represents two sequential consonant phonemes. It is very common in word-final position like *mix* and internally in prefixes like *ex-* (*explain*, *exact*). It rarely appears at the beginning of native English words, mostly featuring in Greek-derived vocabulary.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Learners often mistakenly voice **X** in all positions or fail to voice it before stressed vowels. Contrast voiceless `/ks/` in *exercise* `/ˈek.sə.saɪz/` with voiced `/ɡz/` in *exert* `/ɪɡˈzɜːt/`. Avoid pronouncing initial **X** as `/ks/` in words like *xylophone*; it must start cleanly with `/z/`.
