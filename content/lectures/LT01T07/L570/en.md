## The Sound of the Letter R

In English, the letter **R** represents a postalveolar approximant `/ɹ/`. Unlike the tapped or trilled sounds found in many other languages, the English `/ɹ/` never involves the tongue tapping or vibrating against the roof of the mouth. In rhotic varieties like General American, it also colours following or preceding vowels, whereas in British Received Pronunciation it is silent after vowels unless another vowel follows.

## How to Produce the Sound

Curl the tip of your tongue slightly backward toward the roof of your mouth without actually touching anything. Let the sides of your tongue rest gently against your upper back teeth to anchor it. Slightly round your lips and voice the sound continuously without any vibration or trilling.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **R** appears in uppercase as **R** and lowercase as **r**. It occurs widely at the beginning, middle, and end of words, as well as in common clusters like **br**, **tr**, and **str**. It can also appear doubled, as in *mirror* or *carry*, usually keeping the single consonant sound.

## Regional Variants and Common Mistakes

A key contrast lies between rhotic accents like General American, which pronounce `/ɹ/` or an r-coloured vowel `/ɚ/` in words like *car* `/kɑːr/`, and non-rhotic accents like British RP, which drop it to produce `/kɑː/`. Learners often make the mistake of rolling or tapping the sound as in Spanish or Russian. Make sure your tongue floats freely without striking the palate.
