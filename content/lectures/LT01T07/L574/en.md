## The Sound of V

The letter **V** represents the voiced labiodental fricative sound `/v/`. It sounds exactly like the buzzing friction heard in words like *vine* or *river*. Unlike the unvoiced `/f/`, your vocal cords must vibrate continuously while producing this consonant.

## How to Pronounce V

Lightly rest your upper front teeth against the inner edge of your lower lip. Force air smoothly through the narrow opening while actively engaging your vocal cords to create voice. Avoid rounding or pressing your lips together so the sound does not turn into a `/w/`.

## Spelling Patterns

In English, **V** almost always represents `/v/` and is remarkably consistent. A standard rule of English orthography is that native words rarely end in a bare **V**; they are almost always followed by a silent **E**, as in *give*, *have*, and *love*. It rarely doubles, appearing doubled mainly in informal slang like *savvy* or *revv*.

## Common Traps and Comparisons

The most critical distinction for many learners is separating `/v/` from `/w/` or `/b/`. Confusing *vine* with *wine* or *vest* with *best* alters the meaning entirely. Always ensure your top teeth make firm contact with your lower lip rather than rounding both lips forward.
