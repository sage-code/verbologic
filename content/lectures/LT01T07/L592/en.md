## The Voiced Postalveolar Fricative

Here, the letter **S** represents the voiced postalveolar fricative `/ʒ/`. It sounds exactly like the *s* in *measure* or the *ge* in the French-derived word *beige*. Unlike the more common voiceless `/s/` sound of **S**, this sound requires full vocal cord vibration while gently hissing behind the teeth.

## How to Produce the Sound

Shape your mouth as if you are going to make the English `/ʃ/` sound (as in *ship*), flaring your lips slightly outward. Place the blade of your tongue close to the roof of your mouth just behind the gum ridge without touching it. Force air through the narrow channel while engaging your voice to create a smooth, buzzing hiss.

## Spelling and Distribution

The letter **S** produces `/ʒ/` primarily in the middle of words before vowel-initial endings such as **-ion**, **-ure**, and **-ual**. Common words include *vision*, *decision*, *pleasure*, and *casual*. It virtually never occurs in this phonetic value at the beginning or absolute end of native English words.

## Contrasting Sounds and Pitfalls

Avoid confusing `/ʒ/` with the voiceless sound `/ʃ/` found in *mission* or the voiced affricate `/dʒ/` heard in *jump*. Non-native speakers often over-pronounce this as a hard `/dʒ/` or de-voice it into `/ʃ/`. Remember that in *vision* and *measure*, the airflow must remain continuous and distinctly voiced throughout.
