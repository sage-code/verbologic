## The Voiceless Postalveolar Fricative

The digraph **sh** consistently represents the voiceless fricative `/ʃ/`, as in **ship** or **wish**. It is identical to the quiet *shh* sound used to hush someone. In English, it is articulated slightly further back than `/s/`, with a soft, rushing stream of air.

## How to Produce /ʃ/

Round your lips slightly and flare them gently forward. Raise the blade of your tongue close to the roof of your mouth, just behind the tooth ridge, without touching it. Blow air steadily over the tongue to create a broad, soft hissing sound without vibrating your vocal cords.

## Spelling Patterns for /ʃ/

While **sh** is the default spelling at the start or end of words (such as **shop** or **cash**), this sound is also frequently spelled with **ti**, **ci**, or **ssi** before endings like *-tion* or *-cious* (e.g., **station**, **delicious**). Unlike in German, English never uses *sch* for this single sound in native words. It rarely changes value based on neighboring vowels.

## Comparing /ʃ/ and Common Pitfalls

Take care not to confuse `/ʃ/` with the sharp hiss of `/s/` or the voiced variant `/ʒ/` found in **measure**. Avoid placing the tongue tip too far back into a retroflex curl, as heard in some Slavic languages, or flattening it forward into an alveolar sound. Practice minimal pairs like **sip** versus **ship** and **save** versus **shave** to keep the airflow broad and clean.
