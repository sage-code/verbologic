## The Sound of X

In words like **exam**, the letter **x** represents the voiced cluster `/ɡz/`. Unlike the more frequent voiceless sound `/ks/` found in **box**, your vocal cords vibrate through both the stop and the fricative. It combines a voiced velar stop `/ɡ/` smoothly into a voiced alveolar sibilant `/z/`.

## How to Produce /ɡz/

Start by pressing the back of your tongue against the soft palate to briefly block the airflow, humming gently to make the voiced `/ɡ/`. Release the air as your tongue tip moves close to the ridge behind your upper front teeth, continuing the voice to produce a buzzing `/z/`. Ensure that the transition is seamless without an intervening vowel.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **x** is written as uppercase **X** and lowercase **x**. The voiced pronunciation `/ɡz/` typically occurs when the prefix **ex-** or an internal **x** is immediately followed by a stressed vowel, as in **exist** or **executive**. In contrast, syllable-final and word-final positions almost universally yield the voiceless cluster `/ks/`.

## Contrasting Sounds and Pitfalls

Be careful not to default to the voiceless cluster `/ks/` in every position; saying `/ɪkˈsæm/` instead of `/ɪɡˈzæm/` is a very common foreign accent marker. Compare the voiced `/ɡz/` in **exam** with the voiceless `/ks/` in **exit** (often `/ˈek.sɪt/`) and **expert**. Remember also that at the very beginning of loanwords like **xylophone**, initial **x** is pronounced simply as `/z/`.
