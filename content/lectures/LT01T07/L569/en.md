## Sound of the Letter

In English, the letter **Q** almost never appears alone and is usually followed by **U**. Together, **QU** represents the consonant cluster `/kw/`, which sounds like a crisp `/k/` gliding immediately into a rounded `/w/`. In loanwords from French, it sounds like a simple, unvoiced `/k/`.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce the standard sound, raise the back of your tongue to touch your soft palate to block the airflow for `/k/`. As you release the breath, quickly round your lips tightly into a small circle to glide smoothly into the `/w/` sound. Ensure your vocal cords vibrate only once you hit the `/w/` transition.

## Spelling and Rules

Virtually every native English word spells this letter with an accompanying **U**, as in *quick* or *quiet*. It rarely appears at the end of native words; word-final instances typically appear as **-que** in French borrowings like *boutique*. Capital **Q** is written as a large circle with an intersecting tail stroke at the bottom right.

## Comparison and Common Mistakes

Do not pronounce the **U** after **Q** as a full vowel like `/uː/`; it must be a swift glide `/w/`. A common mistake for learners whose native languages lack `/w/` is substituting a `/v/` sound, making *queen* sound like *kveen*. Be careful also with borrowings like *technique* where the sequence sounds merely like `/k/` without any lip rounding.
