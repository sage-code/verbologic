## Silent 't' in Clusters

In certain historical consonant clusters, the letter **t** represents no sound at all, denoted phonetically as `/∅/`. It has been completely dropped from spoken modern English in these words, meaning you hear only the surrounding consonants. Learners often overpronounce it because the written letter appears in the spelling.

## How to Pronounce Words with Silent 't'

To say words like *listen*, glide smoothly from the `/s/` directly into the `/ən/` syllable without making any stop. Do not let your tongue tip press firmly against the alveolar ridge to form a `/t/`. Simply release the friction of the 's' straight into the schwa or syllabic nasal.

## Spelling Patterns with Silent 't'

This silent **t** appears almost exclusively in specific native Germanic clusters such as **-sten-** (*listen*, *fasten*), **-stle-** (*castle*, *whistle*), and **-ften-** (*often*, *soften*). It is also found in some French loanwords like *ballet* and *gourmet*, where the final 't' is mute. In standard writing, the spelling must retain the letter despite its total absence in speech.

## Silent vs. Sounded 't'

Compare the completely silent **t** in *listen* (`/ˈlɪs.ən/`) with the fully articulated `/t/` in related or similar forms like *list* (`/lɪst/`). A very common mistake for foreign learners is pronouncing the **t** in *castle* or *fasten*, which makes speech sound unnatural and archaic. Note that while *often* is occasionally pronounced with a `/t/` by some speakers, omitting it is the standard historical pattern.
