## The Sound of Soft G

In words like **gym**, the letter **G** represents a voiced postalveolar affricate, transcribed as `/dʒ/`. This is the identical sound heard at the beginning of *judge* or *joy*. It begins like a `/d/` and quickly releases into a voiced friction sound like `/ʒ/`.

## How to Produce /dʒ/

Place the tip and blade of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper teeth, blocking the airflow. Slightly round your lips and vibrate your vocal cords as you release the tongue into a rush of air. Ensure the sound remains voiced throughout the entire release.

## Spelling and Patterns

The letter **G** most commonly adopts the soft `/dʒ/` value when followed directly by the vowels **e**, **i**, or **y**. While native Germanic words occasionally retain a hard `/ɡ/` before these vowels (such as *get* and *give*), words of Latin or Romance origin reliably use `/dʒ/`. At the end of short-vowel words, this affricate is typically written as **dge**, as in *badge*.

## Contrasts and Common Mistakes

Do not confuse the soft **G** (`/dʒ/`) with the hard **G** (`/ɡ/`) found in *go* or *game*. A frequent error among learners is de-voicing it into an unvoiced `/tʃ/` (like the *ch* in *chair*) or softening it entirely into a simple fricative `/ʒ/`. Keep the initial stop firm and maintain active vocal cord vibration throughout.
