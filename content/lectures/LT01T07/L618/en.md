## The Sound of Greek ‘ch’

When found in words borrowed from ancient Greek, the digraph **ch** is pronounced as a voiceless velar plosive `/k/`. It sounds identical to the hard English **k** or **c** in words like *cat* or *skin*. Non-native speakers often mistakenly try to pronounce it with an affricate `/tʃ/` as in *church*.

## How to Produce the Sound

Raise the back of your tongue firmly against your soft palate (velum) at the back of your mouth to completely block the airflow. Build up air pressure behind this seal, then release it sharply with an unvoiced burst of breath. Ensure your vocal cords do not vibrate during the burst.

## Spelling Patterns for ‘ch’ as /k/

This hard pronunciation of **ch** almost exclusively occurs in scientific, medical, and cultural vocabulary of Greek descent. You will encounter it at the beginning of words (*chorus*, *character*, *chaos*), in the middle (*anchor*, *mechanic*), and sometimes at the end (*monarch*, *stomach*). It represents the Greek letter chi (Χ/χ).

## Comparing Pronunciations of ‘ch’

The digraph **ch** most commonly represents the affricate `/tʃ/` in native words like *children*, but shifts to `/k/` in Greek terms like *chorus* and `/ʃ/` in French loans like *chef*. Learners frequently err by saying `/tʃ/` for *chorus* or *mechanic*. Pay close attention to word origins to avoid mixing these three distinct sounds.
