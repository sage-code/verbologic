## The Sound of 'wr-'

The digraph **wr-** represents a silent letter combination where the letter 'w' is not pronounced at all. It is articulated simply as the standard English postalveolar approximant `/r/`, completely identical to words beginning with a lone 'r'. In Modern English, there is no lingering trace of a 'w' sound.

## How to Say It

Ignore the initial letter 'w' completely when speaking. Curl the tip of your tongue slightly backward toward the roof of your mouth without touching it, and tense the sides of your tongue against your upper molars. Voice the sound through your vocal cords as you release into the subsequent vowel.

## Spelling and Origins

The spelling combination **wr-** is an archaic Germanic vestige appearing almost exclusively at the beginning of native English root words. Historically, speakers pronounced both the 'w' and the 'r', but the initial labial consonant was dropped centuries ago. Common examples include everyday words such as **write**, **wrap**, **wreck**, and **wrong**.

## Common Pitfalls

The single most frequent mistake made by learners is attempting to pronounce the letter 'w', producing an unnatural `/w/` or `/v/` before the `/r/`. Remember that pairs like **write** and **right**, or **wrap** and **rap**, are perfect homophones. Treat every initial **wr-** precisely as if it were written with a simple 'r'.
