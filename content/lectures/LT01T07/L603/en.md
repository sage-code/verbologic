## The sound of silent 'k' in 'kn-'

In modern English, the digraph **kn-** is always pronounced simply as the voiced alveolar nasal `/n/`. The letter **k** is completely silent, leaving no trace in modern standard speech. Native speakers hear no difference between words like **know** and **no**.

## How to articulate /n/

To make this sound, place the tip of your tongue firmly against the **alveolar ridge** right behind your upper front teeth. Keep your vocal cords vibrating and lower your soft palate so air flows smoothly out through your nose. Do not attempt to form a `/k/` click in the back of your throat before releasing the sound.

## Spelling patterns with 'kn-'

This spelling combination occurs almost exclusively at the **beginning of Germanic root words**, such as **knee**, **knot**, and **knight**. It survives from Old and Middle English, where both the /k/ and /n/ were originally pronounced. You will also see it preserved inside compound words like **unknown** or **penknife**.

## Avoiding common mispronunciations

Unlike in German or other related languages where both consonants are articulated, English never permits a sounding `/k/` before `/n/`. Foreign learners frequently make the mistake of trying to say `/kn/`, turning **knife** into something resembling */knaɪf/*. Simply treat the cluster as a plain `/n/`, exactly matching the start of words like **nice** or **net**.
