## The Sound of K

The letter **K** represents the voiceless velar plosive `/k/`. At the start of a stressed syllable, it is pronounced with a distinct puff of air known as aspiration (`[kʰ]`), which is stronger than in languages like Romance tongues. When placed before an initial **n**, the letter is completely silent.

## Pronouncing K

Raise the back of your tongue until it forms a tight seal against your soft palate (velum). Allow breath pressure to build briefly behind the blockage, then release it sharply without vibrating your vocal cords. For words beginning with **k**, release a small burst of breath immediately following the sound.

## Spelling Patterns

**K** is used frequently before front vowels like **e** and **i** (as in **keep** or **kite**) where the letter **C** would otherwise sound like `/s/`. It often combines with **C** as **-ck** after short vowels at the end of words (like **back** or **lock**). Note the traditional silent pattern where **kn-** is always pronounced simply as `/n/`.

## Nuances and Common Mistakes

English speakers strongly aspirate **k** at the start of words like **key** (`/kiː/`), but drop this aspiration after an **s**, as in **sky** (`/skaɪ/`). A common mistake for non-native speakers is pronouncing the silent **k** in words like **know** or **knight**, which should begin purely with an `/n/` sound. Take care not to add an intrusive vowel between **k** and following consonants.
