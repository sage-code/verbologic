## The Sound of M

The letter **M** represents the voiced bilabial nasal consonant `/m/`. It is produced identically to the 'm' in almost all European languages, with vibrating vocal cords while the mouth is closed. Occasionally, after consonants in unstressed endings like in *rhythm*, it can act as a syllabic consonant `[m̩]` forming its own syllable.

## How to Pronounce M

Close both lips firmly together while keeping your tongue resting relaxed at the bottom of your mouth. Lower your soft palate to let air stream out freely through your nose. Activate your vocal cords so you produce a continuous, humming resonance before opening your lips for the next vowel.

## Spelling Patterns

The consonant sound `/m/` is almost always spelled with **M** or double **MM** as in *summer*. It is completely silent in a handful of Greek loanwords where it precedes 'n' at the start of a word, such as *mnemonic*. It can appear freely in initial, medial, and final word positions.

## Comparing Variations and Common Errors

Learners rarely struggle with the basic `/m/` sound because it is universal, but beware of dropping it before other nasals or vowel reductions. In words like *rhythm* (`/ˈrɪð.əm/` or `[ˈrɪðm̩]`), avoid inserting a strong, artificial vowel between the consonants. Ensure your lips make full, complete contact rather than approximating a nasal glide.
