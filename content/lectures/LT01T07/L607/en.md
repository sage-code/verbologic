## The Sound of '-mb'

The consonant cluster **-mb** at the end of an English word is pronounced simply as a single `/m/`. The final letter **b** is completely silent, meaning it sounds identical to a plain letter **m**. Native speakers do not produce any trace of an explosive `/b/` sound at the end.

## How to Form the Sound

Close both lips firmly together while keeping your tongue resting flat on the floor of your mouth. Vibrate your vocal cords and let the voiced air flow entirely out through your nose. Do not pop your lips open with a burst of air at the end; simply transition directly to silence or the next sound.

## Spelling and Distribution

This combination appears almost exclusively at the end of native Germanic root words such as **lamb**, **comb**, and **numb**. It keeps its silent **b** even when inflectional endings are added, as in **climbing** or **bomber**. Contrast this with medial Latinate words like **timber** or **amber**, where the **b** belongs to a separate syllable and is pronounced.

## Silent 'b' Pitfalls

The most frequent mistake learners make is attempting to pronounce the written **b**, turning `/læm/` into `*/læmb/`. Remember that words like **crumb**, **plumb**, and **dumb** must rhyme perfectly with pure **m** words like **hum** and **gum**. Always treat the final letter as invisible in these roots.
