## The Sound of Soft C

In words like **city**, the letter **C** represents the voiceless alveolar fricative `/s/`. This is often referred to as a **soft C**, which sounds identical to the standard English letter **S**. Unlike languages where **C** might represent `/ts/` or `/tʃ/`, English never uses a single **C** for those sounds without an accompanying **H**.

## Pronouncing Soft C

To produce `/s/`, place the tip or blade of your tongue very close to the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth. Keep your teeth close together and blow a steady, unvoiced stream of air through the narrow central groove. Your vocal cords must remain completely relaxed and silent.

## Spelling Rules for Soft C

The letter **C** softens to `/s/` reliably when it appears directly before the front vowels **E**, **I**, or **Y**, as in **cent**, **circle**, and **cycle**. In almost all other environments—such as before **A**, **O**, **U**, or consonants—it represents the hard sound `/k/`. In borrowed French words, a cedilla may indicate this sound (**façade**), though standard English spellings simply rely on the following vowel.

## Comparing Hard and Soft C

Be careful not to pronounce **C** as `/k/` when followed by **E**, **I**, or **Y**; saying **city** as */ˈkɪti/* is a common mistake for beginners. Additionally, learners from Slavic or Germanic backgrounds must avoid pronouncing this soft **C** as an affricate `/ts/`. Contrast the soft `/s/` in **cinder** with the hard `/k/` in **candle** to master the distinction.
