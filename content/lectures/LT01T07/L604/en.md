## The Sound of 'kn-'

In Modern English, the digraph **kn-** is pronounced simply as `/n/`. The letter **k** is entirely silent, preserving a historical spelling from Old English when the consonant cluster was fully pronounced. It sounds identical to a standard English 'n' sound as heard in words like *no* or *net*.

## How to Produce the Sound

Place the tip of your tongue against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper front teeth. Completely block the airflow through your mouth, lower your soft palate, and let voiced air flow out smoothly through your nose. Do not attempt to articulate a `/k/` sound before releasing into the nasal tone.

## Spelling and Distribution

This spelling pattern appears exclusively at the beginning of root words or syllables, such as in *knot*, *knight*, and *acknowledge*. Because the initial **k** is silent, it creates numerous homophones in English, such as *knot* and *not*, or *know* and *no*. It is never used at the end of syllables.

## Common Traps and Distinctions

The most frequent mistake for learners from languages like German or Slavic languages is trying to pronounce the initial 'k'. Words like *knee* `/niː/` must never sound like 'k-nee'. Always treat initial **kn-** as an ordinary `/n/` sound without any preceding plosive click.
