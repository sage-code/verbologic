## The Sound of P

The letter **P** primarily represents a voiceless bilabial plosive, written as `/p/` in IPA. At the beginning of stressed words, it is pronounced with a noticeable puff of air called aspiration (`[pʰ]`), which is stronger than in many European languages. In word-final position or after `s`, this aspiration disappears completely.

## How to Produce the Sound

Press both lips firmly together to completely block the airflow from your lungs. Build up slight air pressure behind your lips without vibrating your vocal cords. Release your lips sharply to let the burst of air escape; when starting a word, make sure there is an audible puff of breath.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **P** appears in single form as in *pen* and doubled as in *happy*, both sounding identical. It forms the digraph **PH**, which is pronounced `/f/` in words of Greek origin like *phone*. Additionally, **P** is silent when followed by certain consonants at the start of words, such as in *psychology*, *pneumonia*, and *pterodactyl*.

## Accents and Pitfalls

Learners from Romance languages or German often fail to aspirate initial **P**, which can cause English listeners to hear it almost as a `/b/`. Conversely, take care not to pronounce the silent **P** in common vocabulary items such as *receipt* or *cupboard*. Practice contrasting *pin* (with a puff of air) and *spin* (without a puff) using a piece of paper held in front of your mouth.
