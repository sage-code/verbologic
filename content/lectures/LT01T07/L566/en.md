## The Sound of N

The letter **N** most commonly represents the voiced alveolar nasal `/n/`, identical to the default 'n' sound in many European languages. Before velar consonants such as `/k/` or `/ɡ/`, it assimilates and is pronounced as the velar nasal `/ŋ/` (like the sound in *sing*). In unstressed endings after stops or fricatives, it can form its own syllable as a syllabic `[n̩]` without any vowel preceding it.

## How to Form the Sound

To produce the standard `/n/`, place the tip of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge behind your upper front teeth. Lower your soft palate so air flows freely through your nose while your vocal cords vibrate. For `/ŋ/`, press the back of your tongue against the soft palate instead while letting voice resonate through the nasal cavity.

## Spelling Patterns

**N** appears in initial, medial, and final positions across words of all origins, written as uppercase **N** or lowercase **n**. Doubled **nn** indicates a short preceding vowel in words like *manner* or *dinner*. The letter is completely silent when it follows an **m** at the end of a word, as in *autumn*, *hymn*, and *column*.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Notice the difference between the alveolar `/n/` in *thin* `/θɪn/` and the velar nasal `/ŋ/` in *think* `/θɪŋk/`. Learners sometimes insert an unintended vowel before syllabic `[n̩]` in words like *hidden* or *button*, saying `/ˈhɪd.ən/` instead of jumping straight to the nasal release. Also beware of pronouncing the silent final **n** in words like *solemn*.
