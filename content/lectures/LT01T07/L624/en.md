## Sound Overview

The letter group **-ough** is infamous for representing multiple entirely distinct sounds in English, but in the word *through* it sounds like a long, smooth **/uː/**. This vowel is identical to the sound in *blue* or *too*, with no consonant sound at the end. Because the spelling gives no visual clue to this pronunciation, it must simply be memorized as an arbitrary convention.

## How to Say It

Pull your tongue body back and raise the back of your tongue toward the soft palate. Round your lips tightly into a small circle, pushing them slightly outward as if blowing out a candle. Let your vocal cords vibrate steadily without adding any trailing consonant or friction.

## Spelling Patterns

The string **-ough** represents the vowel **/uː/** in only a tiny handful of English words, most notably *through* and its derivatives like *throughout*. Unlike in *cough* or *rough*, the **gh** produces no **/f/** sound here and remains completely silent. In informal American writing, this spelling is sometimes simplified phonetically to *thru*.

## Comparing Pronunciations

Do not confuse the **/uː/** in *through* with the **/oʊ/** sound in *though* or the **/ʌf/** sound in *tough*. Beginners often mistakenly attempt to pronounce the historical guttural consonant or substitute an **/f/**, turning *through* into something resembling *throw* or *thruff*. Always treat the combination in this specific word as a simple, unadorned vowel sound.
