## The Sound of E

The letter **E** represents several distinct vowel sounds in English depending on stress and syllable structure. In stressed closed syllables, it typically represents the short vowel `/ɛ/`, as in *energy* or *bed*. In open syllables or digraphs, it frequently represents the long vowel `/iː/`, while in unstressed syllables it often reduces to `/ə/` or `/ɪ/`.

## How to Pronounce It

To produce the short sound `/ɛ/` heard in *energy*, open your mouth moderately wide with your lips relaxed. Position the front part of your tongue mid-low in the mouth, keeping the vocal cords vibrating smoothly. Avoid opening your jaw too wide, which can turn it into an `/æ/` sound.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **E** is the most frequent letter in English and appears in numerous common digraphs like *ee*, *ea*, and *ie*. It is famously used as a silent final letter (magic 'e') at the end of words to indicate that the preceding vowel is long, as in *site* versus *sit*. At the start of words like *energy*, it reliably produces a short stressed `/ɛ/` before consonants.

## Variants and Pitfalls

Be careful not to pronounce the letter **E** as `/iː/` when it occurs in closed syllables like *pen* or *test*. Non-native speakers often substitute their native pure /e/ sound or confuse `/ɛ/` with the `/æ/` in *pan*. Remember also that a word-final **E** is very often entirely silent, as in *name* or *make*.
