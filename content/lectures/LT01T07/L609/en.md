## The Sound of '-lf'

In traditional Germanic words like 'half' and 'calf', the spelling combination **-lf** is pronounced simply as `/f/`. The letter **l** historically influenced the vowel before falling completely silent in modern standard English. In contrast, words derived from other roots or with different vowel histories, such as 'gulf' or 'wolf', preserve a distinct `/lf/` consonant cluster.

## How to Produce the Sound

To pronounce words like 'half', completely ignore the letter **l**. Rest your upper front teeth lightly against your lower lip and push air through the narrow gap to make a continuous, unvoiced fricative `/f/`. Make sure your tongue stays down and does not lift toward the roof of your mouth, which prevents an accidental `/l/` sound.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter pattern **-lf** occurs exclusively at the ends of morphemes and syllables. In the small set of words 'half' and 'calf', the 'l' is completely silent, though it reappears as a voiced `/v/` in the irregular plurals 'halves' and 'calves'. In almost all other English words ending in '-lf', such as 'shelf', 'elf', and 'golf', both letters are sounded as `/lf/`.

## Common Traps and Comparisons

A very frequent error made by non-native speakers is pronouncing the letter **l** in 'half' and 'calf', saying `/hælf/` or `/hɑːlf/`. Listeners will understand you, but it sounds unnatural in both British and American English. Practice shifting directly from the open vowel into the fricative `/f/` without letting your tongue tip make contact with the alveolar ridge.
