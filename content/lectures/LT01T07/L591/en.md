## The /ʃ/ Sound of 'S'

Although the letter **S** usually represents `/s/`, it makes the voiceless postalveolar fricative sound `/ʃ/` in a small set of words like *sure* and *sugar*. This sound is identical to the standard English digraph **sh** as in *ship*. It is produced without voice, releasing a broad stream of air across the palate.

## How to Articulate /ʃ/

Round your lips slightly and flare them outward as if you are telling someone to be quiet. Raise the blade of your tongue close to the roof of your mouth just behind the alveolar ridge, leaving a wide channel for air. Blow air steadily over the tongue without vibrating your vocal cords.

## Spelling and Occurrence

The spelling of `/ʃ/` with a simple **S** is historically rare at the beginning of words, occurring almost exclusively in *sure*, *sugar*, and their derivatives. Inside words, it regularly occurs in suffixes such as **-sion** following a consonant (e.g., *tension*, *expansion*). Always distinguish this from the standard **sh** spelling found in native Germanic vocabulary.

## Common Pitfalls

A common mistake among non-native speakers is pronouncing *sure* with an initial `/s/`, sounding like *sewer*. Remember that while **S** is overwhelmingly pronounced `/s/` or `/z/`, words in the *sure* and *sugar* family require the full hushing sound `/ʃ/`. Do not insert an extra `/j/` sound after the `/ʃ/` in modern English.
