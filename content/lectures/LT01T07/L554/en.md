## The Sound of B

The letter **B** primarily represents the voiced bilabial stop `/b/`, exactly as heard in **book**. Unlike unvoiced `/p/`, your vocal cords vibrate the moment the lips release air. At the ends of words, English `/b/` remains voiced or partially voiced, resisting the complete devoicing heard in several other Germanic languages.

## How to Produce /b/

Bring both lips completely together to block the airflow coming from your lungs. Build up slight air pressure behind the lips while engaging your vocal cords to create vibration. Open your lips quickly to release a small, voiced burst of sound without puffing extra air.

## Spelling and Patterns

This sound is written as a single **b** in most words, such as *bat* or *above*, and doubled as **bb** after short vowels in words like *rabbit* and *rubber*. Note that **b** becomes completely silent when following **m** at the end of a syllable (as in *lamb* and *thumb*) or before **t** in classical roots like *debt* and *subtle*.

## Common Pitfalls and Comparisons

The standard pronunciation is `/b/`, contrasting directly with the silent **b** in words like *tomb* or *doubt*. A frequent mistake among foreign speakers is pronouncing this silent letter aloud. Additionally, speakers of languages like German or Russian often mistakenly devoice a final **b** into a crisp `/p/`, which can turn *cab* into *cap*.
