## The Sound of W

In English, the letter **W** typically represents the voiced labial-velar glide `/w/`. It sounds like a very quick, consonant-like `[uː]` sound gliding into the next vowel, exactly like the beginning of *water* or *we*. Unlike in German or Slavic languages, it does not sound like a `[v]` consonant.

## How to Pronounce W

Pucker your lips tightly into a small, rounded circle, as if you are preparing to whistle or say an exaggerated 'oo'. Raise the back of your tongue toward the soft palate at the back of your mouth. Release the air with vocal cord vibration while quickly opening your lips into the following vowel sound without touching your teeth to your lips.

## Spelling and Positions

**W** appears frequently at the beginning of words (*wind*, *wait*) and inside consonant clusters (*swim*, *twin*). When paired with other letters, it often functions as part of a vowel digraph, such as **aw** in *law* or **ow** in *cow*. Before the letter **r** at the start of a word, such as in *wrong* or *wrap*, it is entirely silent.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

A very frequent mistake for speakers of German, Romanian, or Slavic languages is substituting `/v/` for `/w/`, making *vine* and *wine* sound identical. Remember that for `/w/`, your teeth must never touch your bottom lip. Practise contrasting minimal pairs such as *west* versus *vest* to keep the rounded lips distinct from the biting friction of `/v/`.
