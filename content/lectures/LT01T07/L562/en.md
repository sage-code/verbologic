## The Sound of J

The letter **J** in English almost invariably represents the voiced postalveolar affricate `/dʒ/`. Unlike languages such as German or Spanish, English **J** never represents a glide `/j/` or a soft fricative `/x/`. It begins with a firm stop that immediately releases into a voiced friction.

## How to Pronounce /dʒ/

Place the tip and blade of your tongue firmly against the alveolar ridge just behind your upper teeth, as if preparing to say `/d/`. Round your lips slightly and build up mild air pressure behind the blockage. Release the seal smoothly while vibrating your vocal cords, letting the sound burst into a voiced `/ʒ/`.

## Spelling Patterns

The letter **J** is usually found at the beginning of words or roots, such as in *judge* or *project*. English words virtually never end in the letter **J**; final `/dʒ/` is almost always spelled with **-dge** or **-ge**, as in *badge* or *page*. A rare exception occurs in foreign borrowings where **J** may reflect Spanish `/h/` or French `/ʒ/`.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

Learners whose native languages use **J** for the `/j/` sound (like *yes*) frequently mispronounce English words like *jacket* as *yacket*. Remember that English **J** is a harsh, explosive affricate `/dʒ/`, not a smooth semivowel. Be careful not to devoice it into `/tʃ/` at the end of syllables.
